ONE BILINEAR PENCIL BEHIND THE DETERMINANT, ENERGY, AND PERTURBATION CONSTRUCTIONS
Research note, 14 November 2026

Scope and status

This note reorganizes the constructions in the local manuscript
sources/Fauzan_2026_zeta5.txt, principally Sections 2 and 6 and Appendix C.
It is a structural derivation from that manuscript, not an independent audit
of its numerical certificates or its irrationality claim. Equations below
labelled "derived" are consequences of its definitions and Proposition 2.2.
The optional orthogonal-polynomial and Stieltjes viewpoints are not needed
for the paper's proof. They explain which apparently separate constructions
really are representations of a common object, and which are additional
estimates or certificates.

1. THE MASTER OBJECT: A RATIONALLY DEFINED BILINEAR PENCIL

Use the manuscript's K=40n, N=3n, h=K-N=37n, xi=zeta(5). Write

  D_m(t) = product_{j=1}^m (t+j^2),
  R_K(t) = D_N(t)^6 / D_K(t) = W_K(t)/T_K(t),
  W_K(t) = D_N(t)^5,       T_K(t) = D_K(t)/D_N(t).

The notation T_K here means the manuscript's D_tail, not a new construction.
On E_K=Q[t]_{<h}, define

  L_{K,X}(p) = mu_X(R_K p),
  B_{K,X}(p,q) = L_{K,X}(pq).

Thus B_{K,X} is one symmetric bilinear form with values in Q[X], affine in X.
Its matrix in the monomial basis 1,t,...,t^{h-1} is exactly G_K(X), and
Delta_K(X)=det G_K(X). This is the minimal unifying object: not merely the
functional mu_X, and not merely the value Delta_K(xi), but the twisted
bilinear pencil on a specified h-dimensional polynomial space.

Why the twist matters: mu_X itself is defined on rational functions with
the allowed simple poles. Twisting by R_K turns the problem into a polynomial
moment problem, while retaining the complete rational functional, including
its polynomial-division contribution. Subsequent bases, residue formulas,
positive measures, and perturbation matrices all act on this same B_{K,X}.

Reference: manuscript (2.4), p. 4; cancellation in Section 2.3, p. 4.

1A. THE DEFORMATION IS A RESIDUE AT INFINITY

The functional-analysis investigation by the other agent supplies an even
shorter exact description of the X direction. On the manuscript's rational
domain,

  mu_X(R)=mu_xi(R)+(X-xi)[t^(-3)]R(t)
         =mu_xi(R)-(X-xi)Res_infinity(t^2 R(t) dt).

Proof: polynomial terms have no negative Laurent coefficients. A simple
pole r_j/(t+j^2) contributes j^4 r_j to [t^(-3)]R, exactly its X coefficient
under mu_X. Thus the deformation is a single coefficient-extraction
functional, not a separately adjustable value at each pole. Likewise
tau_X(g)=tau_xi(g)-(X-xi)[x^(-1)]g(x), consistent with the x^5 pullback.

After the R_K twist, the directional bilinear form is therefore

  M_K(p,q)=[t^(-1)] H_K(t)p(t)q(t)/T_K(t),
  H_K(t)=t^2 W_K(t).

Let A_K=Q[t]/(T_K), and let epsilon(f) be the coefficient of t^{h-1} in
the degree<h remainder of f modulo T_K. Polynomial division proves

  [t^(-1)] f(t)/T_K(t)=epsilon(f),
  M_K(p,q)=epsilon(H_K p q).

Thus M_K is a twisted residue (Frobenius) pairing on the finite algebra A_K.
This description is equivalent to the signed atomic formula below, because
partial fractions give epsilon(f)=sum_j f(a_j)/T_K'(a_j).

The determinant sign and resultant can be checked without any convention
ambiguity. In the monomial basis, the untwisted pairing epsilon(pq) has zero
entries when i+j<h-1 and antidiagonal entries 1. Its determinant is
(-1)^{h(h-1)/2}. Multiplication by H_K on A_K has determinant
Res(T_K,H_K)=product_j H_K(a_j), since T_K is monic with distinct roots.
Consequently

  det M_K=(-1)^{h(h-1)/2} Res(T_K,H_K)
         =(-1)^{h(h-1)/2} product_{j=N+1}^K j^4 D_N(-j^2)^5.

This is exactly (2.9). Since gcd(H_K,T_K)=1, multiplication by H_K is
invertible; the pairing is nondegenerate. No analysis is needed for this
degree assertion. The residue pairing is especially useful for connecting
the arithmetic bases to the real pencil.

Only the directional form M_K descends intrinsically to A_K. The full
positive form B_{K,xi} does not annihilate multiples of T_K. It is viewed
on A_K through its unique representatives of degree<h, or more simply on
E_K. Conflating these two assertions would be an error.

2. AN EXACT AFFINE SIGNED-MEASURE REALIZATION

Define the positive base measure on (0,infinity)

  dnu_0(t) = w(sqrt(t))/(2 sqrt(t)) dt,
  dnu_K(t) = R_K(t) dnu_0(t).

All polynomial moments exist, and nu_K has positive density. Proposition 2.2
and t=y^2 give L_{K,xi}(p)=integral p(t) dnu_K(t).

Set a_j=-j^2 and

  c_j = j^4 W_K(a_j)/T_K'(a_j),       N<j<=K,
  sigma_K^res = sum_{j=N+1}^K c_j delta_{a_j}.

The superscript "res" distinguishes this signed atomic measure from the
regularized configuration measure called sigma in Section 6.

MASTER IDENTITY (derived): for every real X and every polynomial p,

  L_{K,X}(p)
    = integral p(t) dnu_K(t)
        + (X-xi) sum_{j=N+1}^K c_j p(a_j).

Equivalently, L_{K,X} is integration against

  nu_{K,X} = nu_K + (X-xi) sigma_K^res.

Proof: divide W_K p by T_K. Its simple-pole coefficient at a_j is
W_K(a_j)p(a_j)/T_K'(a_j). The derivative with respect to X of
mu_X(1/(t+j^2)) is j^4, whereas polynomial values do not depend on X.
Therefore d L_{K,X}(p)/dX=sum c_j p(a_j). Evaluate the affine identity at
X=xi using Proposition 2.2.

This identity is stronger than a loose analogy: the whole real pencil is a
positive continuous measure plus a specified signed mass perturbation on the
negative axis. The continuous part, atomic part, and rational description
are exact presentations of the same functional.

Important limitations:
* nu_{K,X} is generally signed for X!=xi. Its positivity on the entire
  polynomial algebra is not asserted. Positivity on E_K is the finite
  dimensional question controlled in Appendix C.
* This does not give a positive measure for the original mu_X at arbitrary X.
* This analytic identity uses xi. Arithmetic estimates must still use the
  rational formula for mu_X; it is not legitimate to introduce xi into Q_p
  or to estimate only the atomic term.
* sigma_K^res represents the X coefficient, not the whole rational
  functional. Replacing G_K(X) by its residue coefficient matrix would drop
  the polynomial quotient and most of the constant term.

3. THREE REPRESENTATIONS OF THE SAME DETERMINANT

(a) Gram volume. With v(t)=(1,t,...,t^{h-1})^T,

  G_K(xi) = integral v(t)v(t)^T dnu_K(t).

For any nonzero q in E_K, integral q(t)^2 dnu_K(t)>0. Thus G_K(xi) is
positive definite and Delta_K(xi)>0. This is ordinary positive definiteness
of the master form, not a separate nonvanishing mechanism.

(b) Orthogonal-polynomial norms. Fix K and Gram-Schmidt the monomials for
nu_K. Let p_{r,K}(t) be the resulting monic orthogonal polynomials, and
d_{r,K}=integral p_{r,K}(t)^2 dnu_K(t)>0. The basis transformation is unit
triangular, so

  Delta_K(xi) = product_{r=0}^{h-1} d_{r,K}.             [derived]

This is the same Gram determinant diagonalized by a monic basis. The paper
does not need to construct these polynomials. Their measure depends on K,
so this is a varying family of orthogonality problems.

(c) Vandermonde/log-gas integral. Expansion of the two determinants gives

  Delta_K(xi)
    = (1/h!) integral_{(0,infinity)^h}
        product_{i<j}(t_i-t_j)^2 product_i dnu_K(t_i).

This is exactly the manuscript's (6.10), after t_i=y_i^2. It is the
continuous Cauchy-Binet/Andreief formula. Positivity, the product of norms,
and the multiple integral are interchangeable expressions of one Gram
volume; they are not three independently constructed objects.

In fact the determinant integral is valid for EVERY real X if dnu_K is
replaced by the finite signed measure dnu_{K,X} from Section 2. Absolute
integrability follows from the existence of all moments of its total
variation. At X=xi only the continuous positive part remains. The coefficient
of (X-xi)^h takes all h measures from the atomic part, and the Vandermonde
annihilates terms with repeated nodes. Because there are exactly h nodes,
the surviving h! permutations give (det V)^2 product_j c_j. Thus positivity
at xi and the degree-h coefficient are literally the continuous and purely
atomic cases of ONE determinant integral identity. For other coefficients,
mixed continuous/atomic integrals occur; there is no blanket positivity
claim because c_j have alternating signs.

The standard orthogonal-polynomial definitions and moment determinant
formulas can be checked in NIST DLMF Section 18.2(i),(ix):
https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.2#i
https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.2#ix
Andreief's identity and its discrete Cauchy-Binet counterpart are stated and
derived in P. J. Forrester, "Meet Andreief, Bordeaux 1886, and Andreev,
Kharkov 1882-83", equations (1.7), (2.2), Section 2.3:
https://arxiv.org/html/1806.10411

4. THE RESIDUE MATRIX IS THE SAME GRAM CONSTRUCTION WITH DISCRETE MASS

Write M_K=[X]G_K(X) and let V have columns v(a_j). The master identity gives

  M_K = V C V^T,       C=diag(c_{N+1},...,c_K).

There are exactly h distinct nodes, so V is square and invertible. Thus
det M_K=(det V)^2 product c_j is nonzero. This is the degree calculation
in (2.9): degree Delta_K=h. It is the discrete, signed version of the same
Gram/Vandermonde identity used continuously for Delta_K(xi).

This makes the parallel particularly clean:

  continuous positive mass nu_K  --> G_K(xi) --> nonvanishing at xi;
  discrete signed mass sigma_K^res --> M_K --> nonzero leading coefficient.

Neither Gram matrix equals the other; they are the constant-at-xi and
directional parts of one pencil.

A useful exact basis is the Lagrange basis

  ell_j(t)=T_K(t)/((t-a_j)T_K'(a_j)),  ell_j(a_i)=delta_{ij}.

In this basis the residue form is diagonal, with diagonal c_j. This basis
usually has troublesome p-adic denominators. When nodes collide modulo p,
the p-adic bases/divided differences in the manuscript can be understood
as integral replacements for naive global Lagrange interpolation. This is
a conceptual relation, not a claim that those local bases equal ell_j.

5. THE PERTURBATION MATRIX IS THE RESIDUE FORM IN AN ORTHONORMAL FRAME

Let G=G_K(xi)>0 and define the manuscript's

  B_K=G^{-1/2} M_K G^{-1/2}.

Then, exactly,

  G_K(xi+delta)=G^{1/2}(I+delta B_K)G^{1/2},
  Delta_K(xi+delta)/Delta_K(xi)=det(I+delta B_K).

Thus B_K is not a new functional: it is the residue form measured relative
to the positive real form. More invariantly it represents the unique
operator A on E_K satisfying

  B_{K,xi}(Ap,q) = sum_j c_j p(a_j)q(a_j).

The self-adjoint operator A and B_K have the same eigenvalues. Its norm is
the generalized Rayleigh quotient

  ||B_K|| = sup_{q!=0}
    |sum_j c_j q(a_j)^2| / integral q(t)^2 dnu_K(t).

Appendix C is precisely an estimate for this quotient: its numerator is
bounded by residues and polynomial evaluations outside the positive
support; its denominator is bounded from below by an interval L^2 norm.
The Legendre expansion is an efficient comparison norm, rather than
another determinant construction.

If beta_1,...,beta_h are the eigenvalues of B_K, then

  Delta_K(X)=Delta_K(xi) product_r(1+(X-xi)beta_r).

The same identity produces both the nearby-rational nonvanishing argument
in C.2 and the coefficient height bound in C.3. Multiplying by positive
normalization factors gives the corresponding identities for Q_K.

Derived consequences worth recording explicitly:
* All beta_r are real and nonzero, because B_K is real symmetric and M_K
  is invertible. Therefore all h roots of Delta_K are real, counting
  multiplicity: x_r=xi-1/beta_r. No simplicity is asserted.
* dist(xi,{roots of Delta_K})=1/||B_K||. Consequently the matrix-norm bound
  is exactly a lower bound on the closest root distance.
* sign(c_j)=(-1)^{j-1}. Indeed W_K(a_j) has sign (-1)^N and T_K'(a_j)
  has sign (-1)^{j-N-1}. By congruence, M_K and B_K have the same inertia
  as diag(c_j). Positive c_j count roots below xi, negative c_j roots above
  xi. This is a structural consequence, not an estimate of their distances.

Manuscript references: coefficient matrix in Section 2.3; Appendix C.1,
equations (C.2)-(C.5), p. 29; factorization in C.2-C.3, pp. 30-31.

6. A REPRODUCING-KERNEL FORMULATION OF APPENDIX C

Define the degree<h reproducing kernel for nu_K by

  K_K(s,t)=v(s)^T G^{-1}v(t)
          =sum_{r=0}^{h-1} p_{r,K}(s)p_{r,K}(t)/d_{r,K}.

Cauchy-Schwarz applied to the orthonormal expansion proves

  |q(a)|^2 <= K_K(a,a) integral q(t)^2 dnu_K(t).

The exact best constant in this inequality is K_K(a,a); equality is attained
by the kernel polynomial K_K(a,t), up to a scalar. Hence

  ||B_K|| <= sum_j |c_j| K_K(a_j,a_j).                 [derived]

Writing the finite node kernel matrix as H_{ij}=K_K(a_i,a_j), the determinant
identity det(I+AB)=det(I+BA) gives

  Delta_K(xi+delta)/Delta_K(xi)=det(I+delta C H).        [derived]

H is positive definite because evaluation at the h distinct nodes is
invertible. C H need not be symmetric, but it is similar to the real
symmetric matrix H^{1/2} C H^{1/2}; its eigenvalues are the beta_r above.
This is the finite signed-mass perturbation (often discussed under Uvarov
modifications) behind the appendix. It shows a possible route to improving
the norm estimate if sufficiently good kernel estimates become available.
No improvement is established merely by introducing this notation.

For the standard kernel and reproducing property see NIST DLMF
equations 18.2.12_5 and 18.2.13:
https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.2#v
The inequalities and determinant identity specialized here follow directly
from the displayed finite-dimensional linear algebra.

7. RATIONAL MODIFICATION AND STIELTJES/PADÉ INTERPRETATION

The positive measure nu_K is a rational modification of the fixed measure
nu_0:

  dnu_K = [D_N^6/D_K] dnu_0 = [W_K/T_K] dnu_0.

Multiplication by a polynomial is customarily called a Christoffel
modification. Division by factors outside the support is in the
Geronimus/Uvarov rational-modification family; conventions for these names
vary, especially about whether masses at the denominator zeros are included.
In this example every denominator zero lies on the negative axis, outside
the positive support. Our explicit formula is unambiguous and preferable
to relying on terminology alone. The X deformation then adds the signed
masses c_j delta_{a_j} with the single common parameter X-xi.

Primary reference on rational modifications and mass terms:
C. Alvarez-Fernandez, G. Ariznabarreta, J. C. Garcia-Ardila, M. Manas,
F. Marcellan, "Transformation theory and Christoffel formulas for matrix
biorthogonal polynomials on the real line", Sections 2-3:
https://arxiv.org/html/1605.04617
An alternative scalar determinant treatment is C. Krattenthaler,
"A determinant identity for moments of orthogonal polynomials that implies
Uvarov's formula for the orthogonal polynomials of rationally related
densities": https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.03969

Define the actual Stieltjes/Markov transform of nu_0 by

  s_0(z)=integral dnu_0(t)/(z-t),   z outside [0,infinity).

Then the manuscript's pole data at xi are its sampled negative values:

  -s_0(-j^2)=mu_xi(1/(t+j^2)).

The polynomial data are the moments of nu_0. Thus mu_X packages moments and
specified resolvent values into rational arithmetic data; at xi they come
from one positive measure. Do not claim that mu_X itself is the value of
this same Stieltjes transform for every X, or define mu_X at arbitrary poles
without an extension: the original domain only allows the specified simple
poles.

For fixed K, s_K(z)=integral dnu_K(t)/(z-t) gives an optional Padé viewpoint.
If p_{h,K} is monic orthogonal of degree h, set

  q_{h-1,K}(z)=integral [p_{h,K}(z)-p_{h,K}(t)]/(z-t) dnu_K(t).

Then

  p_{h,K}(z)s_K(z)-q_{h-1,K}(z)
    =integral p_{h,K}(t)/(z-t) dnu_K(t).

Orthogonality cancels the first h coefficients of the asymptotic moment
expansion on the right, explaining the Padé connection. This is a direct
derivation, not a proposed replacement proof of irrationality. Because the
support is unbounded and moments grow fast, a moment expansion at infinity
should be treated as formal/asymptotic, not asserted convergent in a full
neighborhood of infinity.

Two important distinctions:
* p_{h,K}(t), orthogonal in the spatial variable t, is NOT Delta_K(X), the
  determinant polynomial in a deformation parameter. Real-rootedness of
  Delta_K follows from the symmetric pencil, not from an assertion that
  Delta_K is an orthogonal polynomial in X.
* The real orthogonal basis typically contains xi-dependent coefficients.
  It is excellent for positivity and norms, but is not automatically useful
  for integral or p-adic estimates.

8. LOGARITHMIC ENERGY IS THE LARGE-K EXPONENT OF THE SAME GRAM VOLUME

In the Vandermonde integral set the original spatial coordinate to K^2 t,
equivalently y=K sqrt(t). The logarithms of the weight factors produce

  V(t)=2 pi sqrt(t)
       + integral_0^1 log(t+u^2) du
       - 6 integral_0^alpha log(t+u^2) du,   alpha=N/K.

Thus the large exponent is

  2 sum_{i<j} log|t_i-t_j| - K sum_i V(t_i).

The first term is the logarithm of the squared Vandermonde. The second is
the large-K negative logarithm of the rationally modified weight. This is
the mechanism by which a Gram determinant becomes a logarithmic particle
energy, not a separate model imposed by analogy. The manuscript bounds the
finite-product/integral replacement errors in (6.12), retains the power
factors and Jacobian in (6.13), and integrates an explicit residual tail.
Discarding these terms without control would not be a valid proof.

If eta is a positive measure of mass lambda=h/K and finite logarithmic
energy, define (with the manuscript's log|t-u| sign convention)

  E_V(eta)=I(eta)-integral V d eta.

The regularized empirical measure sigma=(1/K)sum_i omega_i has this same
mass lambda. The omega_i spread the particles around tiny complex circles;
this is needed because point masses have infinite negative self-energy.
The paper explicitly controls the introduced diagonal and smoothing terms.

9. RHO IS A DUAL CERTIFICATE, NOT THE ORIGINAL MOMENT MEASURE

Let rho have mass lambda and finite energy, and suppose

  2 U_rho(t)-V(t)<=M on [0,infinity).

For any admissible eta of the same mass, the zero-mass negativity statement
I(eta-rho)<=0 gives

  I(eta)<=2 integral U_rho d eta-I(rho),
  E_V(eta)<=lambda M-I(rho).                          [derived]

Thus

  D(rho)=lambda sup_t(2U_rho(t)-V(t))-I(rho)

is an explicit upper certificate for the maximal energy. The actual
equilibrium measure would give a sharp certificate under its usual
variational equalities. The manuscript needs only a sufficiently good
certificate, and chooses a rational sum of sixteen arcsine measures because
their potentials and mutual energies are elementary to bound exactly.

This explains the roles precisely:
* nu_K is the genuine K-dependent moment measure defining G_K(xi).
* sigma is a smoothed representation of an arbitrary integration-point
  configuration after scaling, used to remove infinite diagonal energies.
* rho is a fixed explicitly chosen comparison measure with the same mass
  as sigma, used to certify an upper bound for every such configuration.

The equality of masses is essential for I(sigma-rho)<=0. Equality of the
measures is neither claimed nor needed. Rho need not be the exact equilibrium
measure, a pushforward of nu_K, or a limit that has been proved for particles
or orthogonal-polynomial zeros.

Manuscript references: Lemmas 6.1-6.2, (6.6)-(6.9), pp. 17-19; Appendix A.
General potential-theory background, with an opposite kernel sign to be
translated carefully, is E. B. Saff, "Logarithmic Potential Theory with
Applications to Approximation Theory", Section 6:
https://www.math.vanderbilt.edu/saffeb/texts/230.pdf
The dual inequality above is derived here directly from the manuscript's
own zero-mass lemma, so no external equilibrium existence theorem is needed.

10. BASES AND NORMALIZATIONS: WHAT IS ONE OBJECT AND WHAT IS A NEW CHOICE

For a basis change with coefficient matrix C, every matrix of the same
bilinear form obeys

  G_new(X)=C^T G_old(X) C,
  det G_new(X)=(det C)^2 det G_old(X).

This one identity covers monic Gram-Schmidt bases, interpolation bases,
locally integral bases, and real orthonormalization. Their purposes differ:
an integral unimodular basis preserves p-adic determinant valuation; an
orthonormal basis reveals a real relative operator; a monic triangular
basis preserves the determinant exactly. One cannot assume all these
properties for one convenient basis.

In basis-free language det B is a tensor in (det E_K^*)^{tensor 2}, with
polynomial dependence on X. This "determinant line" vocabulary records the
squared basis-change factor. It is optional; the elementary identity above
is sufficient for an accessible presentation.

The scalars S_K and m_{K,M} are NOT generally basis changes. They multiply
the determinant itself:

  F_K=S_K Delta_K,
  Q_{K,M}=m_{K,M} F_K.

S_K is an explicit factorial renormalization whose main analytic effect is
to cancel the K^2 log K terms in log Delta_K. The arithmetic normalization
m_{K,M}=product_p p^{-L_p(K,M)} compensates the proved coefficientwise
valuation bounds. Positive L_p permit division, so m is not just a common
denominator. These are different steps, even though both are scalar
renormalizations.

The primitive polynomial P_K=Delta_K/cont_Q(Delta_K) is the canonical
positive rational scaling with primitive integral coefficients. The explicit
Q_{K,M} is a positive integer multiple of P_K; it is computable from bounds
without finding the exact content. They share degree and roots, but neither
the primitive scale nor an arbitrary basis automatically supplies the
required quantitative arithmetic cost.

The final comparison is between two costs for the same rational polynomial:

  log m_{K,M} <= A_M K^2+o(K^2),
  log F_K(xi) <= U K^2+o(K^2).

If A_M+U<0, an integral polynomial remains small at xi. This resembles a
local/global norm comparison, but it is not an application of the number-
field product formula to Delta_K(xi): xi is the number whose irrationality
is under investigation. The final integer argument is indispensable.

11. A PROPOSED SIMPLER PRESENTATION

A short conceptual prelude could replace many disconnected introductions:

1. Define the single rationally specified form B_{K,X}(p,q).
2. Prove the master affine identity: positive continuous Gram form at xi
   plus the residue-at-infinity pairing. Present its discrete evaluation
   formula and finite quotient-algebra description as equivalent forms.
3. Derive two nonvanishing facts from continuous/discrete Gram identities:
   Delta_K(xi)>0 and det([X]G_K)!=0.
4. State a generic determinant lemma containing the three representations
   (Gram determinant, product of monic norms, Vandermonde integral). Use the
   integral version for the real estimate; do not require the optional OPs.
5. Explain that arithmetic chooses p-integral frames for this same form,
   while analysis scales its positive moment measure. Prove the separate
   local valuation and real energy bounds with all existing error control.
6. Isolate the equal-mass energy certificate lemma once, then supply rho
   and its certified constants in an appendix.
7. Normalize and compare the two quadratic costs.
8. Put all of Appendix C under a generic positive Gram-pencil perturbation
   lemma. Root exclusion and coefficient height become two consequences
   of the same relative operator norm estimate.

This would simplify conceptual bookkeeping. It would not remove the hard
p-adic valuation estimates, the rational numerical certificate for rho, or
the comparison of constants. Orthogonal polynomials and Stieltjes transforms
are useful optional interpretations; adding their full theory to the main
proof could make the exposition longer rather than simpler.
