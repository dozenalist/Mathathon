ADDITIONAL ANALYTIC CHECKS
14 November 2026

These observations are independent algebraic checks, not external results
or computational experiments. They address simple changes of presentation.

1. Multiplying the functional by a fixed nonzero rational constant

An h by h determinant is multiplied by the h-th power of that constant.
Since h is proportional to K, the logarithmic change is O(K), below the
K^2 scale at which the obstruction occurs. This cannot repair the leading
budget gap. More generally, even a K-dependent rational rescaling does
not change the underlying primitive integer polynomial: clearing content
undoes that scalar. A better arithmetic estimate is different from merely
renaming the same rational polynomial with a scalar factor.

2. Shifting or rescaling the polynomial variable by fixed rationals

For a fixed prime p and r in Z_p, substitution X -> X+r preserves the
Gauss valuation of a polynomial: it cannot decrease the valuation since
the binomial coefficients and r are integral, and applying the inverse
substitution X -> X-r gives the opposite inequality. A substitution
X -> aX+b likewise preserves it when a is a p-adic unit and b is integral.

For fixed rational a != 0 and b, these conditions hold at all but finitely
many primes. Thus the general large-prime denominator obstruction cannot
be removed by a fixed rational change of coordinate. The diagnostic at
X=0 is relevant because the integrality requirement applies to every
coefficient, not because zero is supposed to approximate zeta(7).

This does not assert that a parameter-dependent affine change with growing
denominators is harmless; it would require its own arithmetic accounting.

3. Improving only the original numerical enclosures

This escape route was tested more strongly in main_measure_certificate.py.
Exact finite integration sharpens the local-cost bound, and the potential
is tested at a single rational point. Even if the potential supremum for
the original measure were bounded perfectly, the resulting coefficient
would still exceed 0.9. This excludes improving only those enclosures as
a repair of the specified local-valuation certificate. It does not
exclude a different comparison measure or stronger local valuations.

4. The smallest illustrative integer polynomial

The K=2,N=0 example is useful for understanding the construction, but its
positive value gives no irrationality criterion by itself. The proof
requires an entire sequence with integrality, nonvanishing, degree O(K),
and decay exp(-c K^2). Keeping these four obligations separate prevents
the successful small example from being mistaken for progress on the
final irrationality claim.
