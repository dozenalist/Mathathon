OA120 / CC60: INDEPENDENT NOVELTY AUDIT
14 November 2026. Bounded mathematical and primary-literature review.

VERDICT

The supplied OA120 and CC60 arguments are mathematically sound at paper
level under their stated hypotheses. Their public-literature novelty is
NOT established. Classification: NEW CANDIDATE for the exact outer-side,
open-120-degree endpoint statement and its signed closed-60-degree cone
corollary; KNOWN for the underlying binary local-rigidity principle.
The candidate is a focused geometric application of established methods,
not a new general rigidity method or a new bound for the plane.

The strongest defensible contribution is the explicit endpoint-sensitive
geometric obstruction and its reusable label-union consequence, with no
palette-cardinality assumption. The exact formulation was not located
in the primary sources inspected. This is a bounded search, not an
exhaustive priority check; absence from these sources is not proof of novelty.

A particularly useful next result is sharpness of the 60-degree constant
for arbitrary finite k. Section 5 gives a self-contained candidate proof
with k=20. It is newly derived in this audit and still needs independent
refereeing; it is not a literature-certified novelty claim. It would not
establish optimality for the special six-color common-interface problem.

2. THE CLOSEST PRIMARY PRIOR ART

(a) Kruskal's binary rigidity is a direct predecessor.

Clyde P. Kruskal, "The chromatic number of the plane: The bounded case,"
Journal of Computer and System Sciences 74(4) (2008), 598-627,
DOI 10.1016/j.jcss.2007.06.003. Author manuscript [S5], section 3,
Lemmas 3.3 and 3.4, manuscript pages 3-4 (PDF pages 5-6).
Lemma 3.3 says that if radius-epsilon disks around the ends of a unit
segment lie in a properly two-colored region, radius-epsilon^2/8 disks
around those endpoints are monochromatic. Lemma 3.4 propagates the
endpoint colors during continuous motion of such a segment.

Consequently the pointwise counterpart of the source's local binary
rigidity is established prior work. The new role of OA120 is to supply
the appropriate neighbors and connected region at the exact endpoint.

(b) Townsend makes the inner/outer endpoint distinction unavoidable.

S. P. Townsend, "Every 5-coloured map in the plane contains a monochrome
unit," J. Combinatorial Theory A 30(1) (1981), 114-115,
DOI 10.1016/0097-3165(81)90046-7 [S6]. This note explicitly constructs a
proper two-coloring of an INNER annular sector subtending 2*pi/3, refuting
the equality case of a claim used by Woodall. This is a counterexample to
an undirected-side version of OA120, not to OA120 as stated.

S. P. Townsend, "Colouring the plane with no monochrome units,"
Geombinatorics 14(4) (2005), 184-193 [S7]. Definition 2 and Theorem 1,
pages 186-189, concern a finitely disconnected unit annulus whose closure
contains the whole unit circle and a cut-point-free arc longer than pi/3.
Every two-coloring of that annulus has a monochromatic unit pair.
OA120 does not assume such a full annulus, so this theorem is not a
direct substitution proof of OA120. The shared exclusion/annulus method
is nevertheless clear. Woodall's own publication list records Townsend's
1981 note as the erratum to his 1973 paper [S8].

(c) Related annular and measurable literature does not remove this gap.

Jeremy F. Alm and Jacob Manske, "On radial colorings of annuli,"
Australasian J. Combinatorics 60(3) (2014), 270-278 [S9], section 2,
recall Kruskal's rod lemma and give ordinary non-two-colorability of
positive-thickness annuli around radius 1/2, including an odd-cycle
viewpoint. Their radial-coloring results address full annuli and a
restricted coloring class, not OA120's variable-width outer arc.

Michael S. Payne, "Unit distance graphs with ambiguous chromatic number,"
Electronic J. Combinatorics 16 (2009), N31 [S10], section 2.1,
Lemmas 1-4 and Proposition 1, develops density-point and finite-configuration
methods extending Falconer's measurable-coloring argument. Its circle
lemma requires a full circle and an irrational angular rotation condition;
it is not the open 120-degree arc statement. It does show that moving
finite configurations to avoid null exceptions is established machinery.

Georgy Sokolov and Vsevolod Voronov, "On the chromatic number of the
plane for map-type colorings," arXiv:2502.01958v1 (4 February 2025) [S11],
Propositions 1-5 and Lemma 2, use boundary exclusions, binary arcs, and
60-degree sectors. Their Jordan-map/local-finiteness assumptions and
restrictions on unit-circle boundary arcs are stronger and different
from the hypotheses of CC60. I found no direct equivalent there.

3. MATHEMATICAL AUDIT AND A SIMPLIFICATION

No counterexample or missing premise was found in the stated OA120/CC60.
The substantive geometry is acos(r/2)<pi/3 for r>1. It puts the auxiliary
unit neighbor strictly inside the supplied angular interval, even when
the cone aperture is exactly pi/3. Compactness is used only on a strict
90-degree subarc. Uniform widths near the missing endpoints are not needed.
The sign choice in beta and the continuous q(s) construction are valid.

Required hypotheses must remain explicit:
  * one fixed pair of residual labels throughout W;
  * openness and actual outer strips, not merely a boundary trace;
  * finitely many support exclusions at one common center;
  * signed approach directions in a closed cone, not independent axes;
  * measurable weak properness and its all-fixed-directions consequence.

The theorem does not follow from a palette word alone. Opposite approaches
cannot be flipped independently to fit the cone. Townsend's example
explains why changing OUTER to INNER at equality would also be unsafe.

There is a shorter conceptual route that makes the prior-art relationship
precise. First prove the purely graph-theoretic OA120:

  Any open W with the stated outer strips has a non-bipartite induced
  unit-distance graph, hence contains a finite odd unit-distance cycle.

Indeed, suppose a pointwise proper two-coloring of W existed. Use the
supplied geometric construction to give every p in the connected sector
S a unit neighbor q in W. Small disks about p,q lie in W, so Kruskal's
Lemma 3.3 makes the coloring locally constant at every p in S. Connectedness
makes S monochromatic, contradicting the explicit unit pair in S.
Non-bipartiteness gives a finite odd cycle by the standard parity-of-paths
characterization. No measurability is needed in this part.

Now transfer the finite cycle into the a.e. setting. Given its finitely
many vertices z_j in open W, a small open ball of translations keeps every
z_j+t in W. The a.e. two-label restriction excludes finitely many null
translation sets. The all-directions weak-coloring lemma excludes another
finite union, one for each fixed edge. A translation outside their union
would properly two-color the odd cycle, a contradiction.

Thus the weak/a.e. version follows from the ordinary geometric obstruction
using standard finite-translation reasoning. It is valid but should not be
advertised as a wholly new measurable-rigidity phenomenon. An explicit
odd cycle would also provide a portable certificate independent of the
source's larger analytical framework.

4. SHARPNESS OF OA120 BELOW THE THRESHOLD

This elementary comparison is independent reasoning, not an assertion of
priority. If L<2*pi/3, a sufficiently thin open OUTER annular sector of
angular width L admits a proper two-coloring: bisect its angular interval.
Each half approaches a unit-circle arc of width L/2<pi/3, whose diameter
is 2*sin(L/4)<1. For sufficiently small radial thickness both halves still
have diameter strictly less than one, and can be assigned different colors.

For example, for radial thickness eta, the bound
  eta + 2*(1+eta)*sin(L/4) < 1
suffices. The coloring is Borel. It can be extended to a finite measurable
proper coloring of the whole plane using separate labels outside the sector.
So no smaller universal arc-length threshold follows just from outer-strip
geometry. Pairing this observation with OA120 would make a sharp geometric
statement, but the sharper cone optimality below is more directly useful.

5. FOCUSED NEXT LEMMA: GENERIC CC60 IS OPTIMAL

Status: CANDIDATE THEOREM WITH COMPLETE PAPER-PROOF SKETCH; independently
derived here, not yet independently refereed or formalized. Check these
inequalities and the support bookkeeping before treating it as accepted.

Claim. For every aperture A>pi/3 there exist two compact connected segments
K_+,K_- approaching 0 in a cone of aperture less than A, a Borel proper
20-coloring c of the whole plane, and support-excluded label sets H_+,H_-
such that only two labels remain outside H_+ union H_-. Hence the universal
finite-k version of CC60 cannot replace pi/3 by any larger aperture.

Construction. Choose
  pi/3 < Delta < min(A,pi),
  a=(pi-Delta)/2 < pi/3,
  v_+=exp(i*Delta/2), v_-=exp(-i*Delta/2),
  K_+=[0,rho*v_+], K_-=[0,rho*v_-].
Let S_+=Straddle(K_+), S_-=Straddle(K_-), W=S_+ intersection S_-.
The actual approach data use r_n=rho/(n+2), w_n=v_+ or v_-.

Choose rho>0 sufficiently small. Put
  epsilon=rho/(2*(1-rho)), t=asin(epsilon).
Impose epsilon<1, Delta<2*acos(epsilon), and
  a+t < pi/3,
  2*rho + 2*(1+rho)*sin((a+t)/2) < 1,                 (I)
  2*sin((Delta-t)/2) - 2*rho > 1.                     (II)
All hold for sufficiently small rho: their limiting strict inequalities
are a<pi/3, 2*sin(a/2)<1, and 2*sin(Delta/2)>1.

Geometry. Every z in W has 1-rho<|z|<1+rho, by the triangle inequality
applied to its two strict straddle witnesses. Write z=r*exp(i*theta).
If r>=1, the witness |z-u*v_+|<1 with 0<u<=rho forces z dot v_+>0,
and similarly for v_-. Thus theta belongs to the positive horn |theta|<a.
If r<1, a witness |z-u*v_+|>1 gives z dot v_+<u/2<=rho/2, and similarly
for v_-. Hence exp(i*theta) dot v_+, exp(i*theta) dot v_- < epsilon.
The condition Delta<2*acos(epsilon) makes the intersection of these two
angular constraints the single negative horn
  circular_distance(theta,pi) < a+t.
Thus W is contained in two opposite horns, one of half-width a and the
other of half-width a+t, in the stated thin radial band.

Bisect each horn along its central ray. Every resulting half-horn has
diameter less than one by (I): for two radii in the band, use
  |r*e(theta)-s*e(psi)|
    <= |r-s| + s*|e(theta)-e(psi)|
    <= 2*rho + 2*(1+rho)*sin((a+t)/2).
Every point in one horn is farther than one from every point in the other
horn by (II), because their minimal circular angular separation is at least
Delta-t and changing both radii from one costs at most 2*rho.
Assign label 0 to one half of each horn and label 1 to the other half,
assigning the splitting rays consistently. This is a Borel proper
two-coloring of W. No distances between the two horns are unit distances.

Self-contained extension and support exclusion. Let f be the nine-color
square-grid coloring of the whole plane with cells of side 3/5 and label
pair (floor(5*x/3) mod 3, floor(5*y/3) mod 3), identified with 0,...,8.
Each cell has diameter 3*sqrt(2)/5<1; distinct cells with the same label
are separated by at least 6/5 in some coordinate. Thus f is Borel proper.
Define c by the preceding two-coloring on W and by
  c(z)=2+f(z)   if z is outside S_+,
  c(z)=11+f(z)  if z is in S_+ but outside S_-.
These three cases partition the plane. Different cases use disjoint
palettes, so c is a Borel proper 20-coloring, and hence a WeakColoring.

Set H_+={2,...,10}, H_-={11,...,19}. Every H_+ color class is contained
in the closed complement of the open set S_+, so its spatial support is
disjoint from S_+. Every H_- color class is contained in the closed
complement of S_-, giving the second exclusion. Their union leaves exactly
the labels {0,1}. All hypotheses other than the 60-degree aperture are
supplied by actual sets, not a finite palette relaxation.

Using a standard Borel seven-coloring instead of the square-grid coloring
would reduce 20 to 16, but no such reduction is necessary for optimality.
This construction deliberately does not settle the k=6 case, nor show that
the segments arise from the stronger interface-extraction mechanism.

Recommended immediate task: independently referee and formalize this
sharpness lemma, then package it with OA120/CC60 as a sharp local exclusion
result with explicit attribution to Townsend and Kruskal. Only after a
further targeted priority check should it be described as new mathematics.
