1. EXACT VERIFIED STATEMENT

For every Delta with pi/3 < Delta < pi, there are two nondegenerate compact
segments K_+,K_- containing the SAME centre 0, actual ApproachingContinuum
0 K_+ v_+ and ApproachingContinuum 0 K_- v_- data, and a Borel pointwise
proper unit-distance colouring c:C -> {0,...,19}, such that:

  v_+ = exp(i Delta/2), v_- = exp(-i Delta/2);
  H_+ = {2,...,10}, H_- = {11,...,19};
  spatialSupport(c,j) intersect Straddle(K_+) = empty for every j in H_+;
  spatialSupport(c,j) intersect Straddle(K_-) = empty for every j in H_-.

The complement of H_+ union H_- in the declared palette is exactly {0,1}.
Both residual colours actually occur on the common straddle region.
The two signed directions have minimum containing cone aperture Delta.
Consequently, for every A > pi/3, choosing Delta < min(A,pi) supplies a
counterexample lying strictly inside a cone of aperture A.

Thus pi/3 is optimal for the CC60 assertion quantified universally over
finite palettes, already with the fixed palette size 20. This does NOT
assert a counterexample for every prescribed k. In particular it proves
neither sharpness nor failure of a stronger theorem at k=6, and does not
supply the stronger interface-extraction hypotheses of the six-colour
problem. "20-colouring" means a colouring into Fin 20, not a requirement
that all twenty classes be nonempty.

2. PARAMETERS AND HORN ESTIMATES

Write d=Delta/2 and a=(pi-Delta)/2. Choose 0<rho<1/2 sufficiently small,
and set epsilon=rho/(2(1-rho)), t=asin(epsilon). Require exactly the audit's
strict conditions:

  epsilon<1, Delta<2 acos(epsilon), a+t<pi/3,
  2 rho + 2(1+rho) sin((a+t)/2) < 1,                    (I)
  2 sin((Delta-t)/2) - 2 rho > 1.                       (II)

Such rho exists: as rho tends to zero these reduce to Delta<pi,
a<pi/3, 2 sin(a/2)<1, and 2 sin(Delta/2)>1. All hold strictly. In
particular (II), with Delta<pi and t>0, implies Delta-t>pi/3.

Let K_+=[0,rho v_+], K_-=[0,rho v_-], S_+=Straddle(K_+),
S_-=Straddle(K_-), W=S_+ intersect S_-. These are the source's STRICT
straddle sets, with one distance <1 and another >1. The two witnesses
and the triangle inequality give 1-rho<|z|<1+rho for every z in W.

Write z=r exp(i theta). If r>=1, an inside witness u v, 0<u<=rho,
gives r^2-2u(z dot v)+u^2<1, hence z dot v>0. Applying this for both
segments gives the positive horn |theta|<a, with theta centred at zero.

If r<=1, an outside witness u v has u>0 and gives
  z dot v < (r^2+u^2-1)/(2u) <= u/2 <= rho/2.
Consequently exp(i theta) dot v < epsilon. For b=acos(epsilon), the
forbidden arcs cos(theta-d)>=epsilon and cos(theta+d)>=epsilon overlap
because Delta<2b. Their union is one arc, whose complement is exactly
the single negative horn
  circular_distance(theta,pi) < pi-d-b = a+t.
This proves the claimed negative-horn containment. Extending this argument
to r=1 also shows that W contains NO unit-circle points: the two horns
are disjoint, since their angular gap is Delta-t>0.

For two points in one half of either horn, the angular separation is at
most a+t. Their distance is at most
  2 rho + 2(1+rho) sin((a+t)/2) < 1.
This is a bound strictly below one for the diameter, not merely a
pointwise strict bound with supremum one. It remains valid on the
splitting rays and even on the bounding angular/radial closures.

The circular angular separation between opposite horns is at least
pi-a-(a+t)=Delta-t. Hence any points in different horns have distance
at least 2 sin((Delta-t)/2)-2 rho>1. This justifies sharing colours
between opposite horns without introducing unit edges.

3. CONNECTED COMPONENTS: AN OPTIONAL EXACT DESCRIPTION

The horn containments suffice for the colouring. They need not be
mistaken for equalities. In fact W does have exactly two components,
one wholly outside and the other wholly inside the unit circle.

For a unit v, put c_v(theta)=exp(i theta) dot v and define
  T_v(theta,u)=u c_v(theta)+sqrt(1-u^2(1-c_v(theta)^2)),
  F_v(theta)=max_{0<=u<=rho} T_v(theta,u).
The lower root of |r exp(i theta)-u v|^2=1 is negative because u<1.
Thus for r>0, |r exp(i theta)-u v|<1 iff r<T_v(theta,u).
The functions T and F are continuous; continuity of F follows from a
continuous maximum over the fixed compact interval [0,rho]. Moreover
F_v(theta)>1 iff c_v(theta)>0 (use a sufficiently small u>0 for the
forward construction). Therefore the outer component is precisely

  W_out = {r exp(i theta): -a<theta<a,
                       1<r<min(F_v+(theta),F_v-(theta))}.

For r<1, the origin supplies the inside witness. The squared distance
as a function of u is convex, so an outside witness exists iff the
endpoint rho v has distance >1. Put t0=asin(rho/2). Since
Delta<2 acos(epsilon)<2 acos(rho/2), the two endpoint angular conditions
c_v+(theta)<rho/2 and c_v-(theta)<rho/2 intersect in one negative arc.
Using theta=pi+phi, the inner component is precisely

  W_in = {r exp(i(pi+phi)): |phi|<a+t0,
             max(T_v+(pi+phi,rho),T_v-(pi+phi,rho))<r<1}.

The lower radial bound here is positive and strictly below one throughout
its angular interval. Each displayed set is homeomorphic to its angular
interval times (0,1), by linear interpolation between its continuous
radial bounds. Both are nonempty and path connected. They exhaust W,
and the preceding distance estimate separates them by more than one.
Thus they are exactly the two connected components. There is no hidden
third component or bridge through |z|=1.

4. COLOURING EVERY POINT, INCLUDING BOUNDARIES

On W use the explicit convention
  c(z)=0 if Im(z)>=0, and c(z)=1 if Im(z)<0.
In each horn this bisects along its central ray. Points of one colour
in the same horn have distance <1 by (I); points in opposite horns have
distance >1 by (II). Therefore this is a Borel pointwise proper
two-colouring of W. Both colours occur, for example in W_out at small
positive and negative angles. Both central rays receive colour 0.

Let p(x)=floor(5x/3) mod 3, taking residues in {0,1,2}, and put
  f(x+iy)=p(x)+3p(y).
The half-open grid cells have side 3/5 and diameter at most
3 sqrt(2)/5<1. Distinct cells with the same residue pair have index
difference at least three in some coordinate, hence coordinate
separation at least 6/5>1. Thus f is Borel and pointwise proper on the
entire plane, including cell boundaries and negative coordinates.

Extend c by
  c(z)=2+f(z)  for z outside S_+;
  c(z)=11+f(z) for z in S_+ and outside S_-.
Together with W these are three disjoint Borel cases covering every
point. The palettes {0,1}, {2,...,10}, {11,...,19} are disjoint.
Properness within each case follows from the preceding constructions;
properness across cases follows from disjoint palettes. All boundary
points are assigned by these literal cases. No null-set reassignment,
unstated limiting convention, or extension theorem is needed.
Borel pointwise properness immediately implies the source's WeakColoring.

5. ACTUAL SUPPORT EXCLUSIONS AND APPROACH DATA

Each H_+ colour class lies in the CLOSED set C minus S_+, and each
H_- colour class lies in the CLOSED set C minus S_-. For any Borel
class E, support(volume restricted to E) is contained in closure(E):
outside closure(E) there is an open neighbourhood with zero restricted
measure. The two required spatial-support exclusions therefore hold
pointwise, even if class supports include straddle boundary points.
Those boundaries are outside the corresponding OPEN straddle sets.

The segments are compact, connected, nondegenerate, and both contain 0.
For each sign set r_n=rho/(n+2), w_n=v_sign. These radii are positive,
at most rho, and tend to zero; w_n has norm one and converges to v_sign;
and 0+r_n w_n lies in K_sign. These supply every field of the actual
ApproachingContinuum structure at the same centre 0, with the intended
signed directions. There is no independent reversal of directions.

CORRECTIONS/CLARIFICATIONS TO THE AUDITED SKETCH

No mathematical reversal is required. State 0<rho<1/2 explicitly; use a
