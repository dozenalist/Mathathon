### 1.1 Is `4cos ρ_c` attained?  No — and the parallel-pair threshold is exactly `6cos ρ_c`

**Proposition (this block).** Let `w` and `p` be centres of two regular dodecahedra of inradius 1 whose
twelve contacts are at distance exactly 2, with the two contact icosahedra **parallel** and `p − w`
along a common face normal. Then the configuration is 2-separated **iff**

```
|p − w|  ≥  6 cos ρ_c  =  4.767926833750597…  =  1.5 × 4cos ρ_c .
```

*Proof.* Write the contacts as `2u_i` and `p + 2u_i`, `p = d e`, `e` the centre of the face `F` spanned
by `a_1,a_2,a_3`, so `e·a_k = cos ρ_c = c` for those three. Within one icosahedron all distances are
`≥ 2.102924`. For the cross pairs, the binding one is `(2a_i, d e − 2a_k)` with `a_i, a_k` adjacent
vertices of `F`: then `a_i·a_k = 1/√5`, `e·(−a_k) = −c`, so

```
|d e − 2a_k − 2a_i|²  =  d² + 4(2 + 2/√5) − 8 d c ,
```

which equals 4 exactly when `d² − 8cd + 4 + 8/√5 = 0`, i.e. at

```
d  =  4c ± √(16c² − 4 − 8/√5)  =  4c ± 2c  =  6c   (the larger root),
```

using `3c² = 1 + 2/√5` (checked symbolically: the larger root is exactly `6cos ρ_c` and the smaller is
`2cos ρ_c = 1.5893 < 2`, irrelevant). All other cross pairs are further: the same-face pairs have
`e·(u_j−u_i) = 0` and distance² `= d² + 4(2−2/√5) = 27.155`; the antipodal cross pair gives
`d² + 16 − 8dc = 16 − 12c² = 8.42`; and the numerical minimisation below attains exactly `2` at
`d = 6c`, confirming that this pair is the binding one. ∎

Numerically (`c133_parallel_threshold.py`, bisection at 40 digits, 200 steps): `mindist(6c) = 2.000000`
and `mindist(6c − 10⁻⁹) = 1.9999999992`, so the threshold is exactly `6cos ρ_c`.

**Consequence.** The true threshold for two dodecahedral cells lies in

```
[4cos ρ_c, 6cos ρ_c]  =  [3.1786178891670644, 4.767926833750597] ,
```

so `C133`'s constant is exactly `2/3` of the natural one and must not be quoted as the threshold. (The
`[·,·]` upper end is an explicit configuration, hence a bound on the threshold, not an estimate; whether
a *non-parallel* placement does better than `6cos ρ_c` is open, and the scan in `c133_threshold.py`, which
maximises over the relative rotation, also returns `4.767578 < d* ≤ 4.767969`, consistent with the
parallel placement being optimal.)

**Why `4cos ρ_c` is not attained.** At `d = 4cos ρ_c` the contacts of `p` are *forced* into the
face-centre alignment: `|p + 2v_j| ≥ 2` for all `j` needs `v_j·e ≥ −d/4`, while the covering radius gives
`max_j(−v_j·e) ≥ cos ρ_c`; equality is therefore required, and in that alignment the best twist leaves a
pair at `1.214124` (`c133_attain.py`). The constant is a limit of the method, not a threshold.

## 2. `C132` — the displayed coverage inequality is reversed

**Disposition: the conclusion is correct and unchanged; the displayed chain is mis-signed, and the
derivation must use the disjointness upper bound. No constants move.**

The display reads

```
(4π/3)R³ − O(R²)  ≥  Σ_{v∈B_R} vol Ω(v)  ≥  (|B_R| − |S_ε∩B_R|)(4π/3)(1+ε/2)³
```

and the sentence above it justifies the first inequality from saturation (`Ω(v) ⊆ B(v,2)`). But saturation
with disjointness gives an **upper** bound:

```
Σ_{|v|≤R} vol Ω(v) ≤ vol B(0,R+2) = (4π/3)R³ + O(R²),
```

while **coverage** gives a **lower** bound: the cells of centres with `|v| ≤ R+2` cover `B(0,R)`, the shell
cells have volume `≤ 32π/3` each and there are `O(R²)` of them, so

```
Σ_{|v|≤R} vol Ω(v) ≥ (4π/3)R³ − O(R²).
```

So the displayed first inequality uses the coverage bound as if it were an upper bound — the reversal. It
is invisible on dense examples (where both bounds hold by a wide margin), which is why it survived; on the
saturated lattice `2·{(i,j,k) : i+j+k even}` (nearest `2√2`, covering radius exactly `2`, cells of volume
`16`) the ratio `Σ/((4π/3)R³)` is `1.013658` at `R = 20`, `1.000110` at `R = 40`, `0.999759` at `R = 80`
(`audit_c132.py` §C), so no negative slack is available to be spent.

**The derivation the conclusion actually needs** (saturation + disjointness only, no coverage):

```
(4π/3)(R+2)³ ≥ Σ_{v∈B_R} vol Ω(v) ≥ (N_R − S_R)(4π/3)(1+ε/2)³
⟹ N_R(1+ε/2)³ ≤ (R+2)³ + S_R(1+ε/2)³,   S_R = o(R³)
⟹ limsup N_R/R³ ≤ 1/(1+ε/2)³  and the same for liminf.
```

Both quantifiers follow because the inequality holds for every `R`; no limit is assumed, and the `O(R²)`
boundary term of `C132(2)` enters only through `S_R = o(R³)`.

Constants re-derived: `D(ε) = (3+ε)³−1` = `63` at `ε = 1` and `87` at `1.4555`; `2+2D = 128`, `176`;
`1/1.5³ = 0.296296`; the crossover `2((1/0.75469739933740583)^{1/3} − 1) = 0.1967082337` ✓. Nothing in
`C132` depends on the value of `D`, only on its finiteness — which is the right way to have repaired it.

### 3.2 The `≤ 12 faces` in Theorem 4.1 is essential, in the polyhedral category

`c133_12face_essential.py` (exit 0). The regular icosahedron circumscribed about the unit ball has
**20 faces** and volume `V_ico = 70√3 − 30√15 = 5.054056143599 < V_dod = 5.550291028516`
(`ratio 0.910593`). So Theorem 4.1 with "at most 20 faces" is **false** — the 20-face analogue is smaller,
not larger. And 13-face polyhedra with volume exactly `V_dod` exist by a two-parameter continuity
argument: push one dodecahedron face out by `δ` (volume `+δA + O(δ²)`) and truncate one vertex by `ε`
(a new triangular face, volume `−cε³`); one equation, two parameters, so the level set
`vol = V_dod` meets the 13-face class. Hence the hypothesis cannot be dropped *in the polyhedral category*.
It does **not** leave a hole in `C133`: the paper's main theorem (p1, §4 below) states the unrestricted
version, so the correct fix is a citation change, not a mathematical one (`X-C097`). What remains open is
whether a *Voronoi cell of a 2-separated packing* can have `≥ 13` faces and volume `≤ V_dod` — that is
`C125`/`X-C066`.

## 4. Attribution: what Hales–McLaughlin Theorem 4.1 actually gives

Fetched `https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~seanmcl/papers/McLaughlin-Dodec.pdf`, p16, and read Theorem 4.1:

> **Theorem 4.1.** Let `P` be any polyhedron with **at most 12 faces** that contains a unit sphere `S²`.
> Let `B` be the ball of radius `t_dod` concentric with `S²`. The volume of the intersection of `P` with
> `B` is at least `ω_dod`. Equality holds exactly [if] `P` is congruent to the regular dodecahedron
> `Ω(Λ_dod, 0)`.

Three consequences for our citations:

1. **The class is "at most 12 faces", and the equality case is equality *within that class* — but the
   paper's main theorem has no such restriction.** Theorem 4.1 says nothing about a cell with `≥ 13`
   faces, so on its own it cannot supply "`vol Ω ≥ V_dod`, with equality iff dodecahedron" for an
   arbitrary Voronoi cell. The paper's introduction (p1) states the unrestricted form:

   > "`vol(Ω(Λ,v)) ≥ vol(Ω(Λ_dod,0))` for every `v ∈ Λ`, and for every set of points `Λ ⊂ R³` whose
   > pairwise distances are at least the diameter 2. **The case of equality occurs exactly when
   > `Ω(Λ,v)` is congruent to a regular dodecahedron of inradius 1.**"

   That is the Dodecahedral Conjecture itself, and it *is* the theorem proved in the paper — Theorem 4.1
   is Fejes Tóth's ≤ 12-face reduction used inside the proof. **So `C133`'s Consequence 1 and `C108` step
   (3) are sound, provided they cite the main theorem and not Theorem 4.1.** My first reading (below, in
   `X-C090`) flagged the `≤ 12` as an unstated hypothesis; that is right about Theorem 4.1 and wrong about
   the paper. The fix is the citation, not the mathematics, and `X-C090`'s polyhedral counterexample is
   unaffected (it shows 4.1 cannot be strengthened *as a polyhedral statement*).
2. **The quantity is the truncated volume** `vol(P ∩ B_{t_dod})`, not the volume of `P`. That matches the
   project's truncation, and it is why `t_dod` appears at all.
3. **Attribution**: the theorem is Fejes Tóth's, *Regular Figures*, Section 33 and Section 41 (with the
   note on p265), as Hales–McLaughlin restate it; the proof sketch on p16 is theirs. `Lindelöf` is not the
   source for the covering-radius statement in `C133` and should not be cited for it — the covering radius
   `cos ρ_c = √((1+2/√5)/3)` is the circumradius of the icosahedral spherical Delaunay triangle, which is
   what `C133` proves directly.
