## 1. Facet accounting with unnormalised normals — the proposition and its proof

`cell_certificate.polytope_volume` sums `(1/3) d_i A_i` over the planes. That is only correct
when `|n_i| = 1`. The code is correct anyway, and the reason has to be written down rather than
left implicit in `canonicalise`.

> **Proposition.** Let `P = ⋂_{i=1..n} {y·n_i ≤ d_i}` be bounded with `0` in its interior, `n_i ≠ 0`,
> `d_i > 0`, and no two of the half-spaces proportional. Then
>
> ```
> vol P = (1/3) Σ_i (d_i / |n_i|) · A_i ,
> ```
>
> where `A_i` is the Euclidean area of the facet carried by plane `i` (`A_i = 0` if plane `i`
> supports `P` in a set of dimension below 2).

*Proof.* Put `u_i = n_i/d_i`, so that `{y·n_i ≤ d_i} = {y·u_i ≤ 1}` is the same half-space (`d_i > 0`).
In the form `{y·u_i ≤ 1}` the facet lies at distance `1/|u_i| = d_i/|n_i|` from the origin, and the
outward unit normal is `u_i/|u_i|`. The divergence theorem with the field `y/3` gives
`vol P = (1/3) ∮_{∂P} y·ν dA = (1/3) Σ_facets dist_i · A_i`, and substituting
`dist_i = d_i/|n_i|` gives the claim. The bijection between facets and supporting planes is §1.1
of `REVIEW_0738`; the only new content here is the scale factor. ∎

**Why the code needs no `|n_i|`.** `canonicalise` rewrites every half-space into `{y·u ≤ 1}` with
`u = n/d` and keeps one member per ray, so by the time the face loop runs every facet distance is
exactly `1` and the plain `(1/3) Σ A_i` is the proposition with `d_i/|n_i| = 1`. The general-normal
formula is recorded at the function so that a future caller who bypasses `canonicalise` cannot
silently inherit the unit-normal formula. `W1`–`W5` and the volume contract (cube `8`,
tetrahedron `8/3`, octahedron `4/3`, box `2×3×4 = 24`, coplanar refused, duplicates reduced,
`2e₁@1 → 6`, `e₁@2 → 8`, `e₁@½ → 6`, edge-only and vertex-only planes contributing `0`) all
still pass; the whole verifier runs in `1.8 s`.

## 2. The disk in the ring face, on the whole offset path (Main's `G17`)

Main's `C136` observation is correct and it invalidates the step as `REVIEW_0608` §11 stated it:
`π t*²` was computed for the part of the *ring plane* `y·n_r = R` that the other faces leave
uncut, and the **face** — the part of that plane that lies in `∂Ω` — is smaller. The ring-excess
lemma needs the face area.

> **Lemma (disk in the face).** Let every competing face be `{y·n_j ≤ d_j}` with `d_j ≥ 1`, and let
> `θ_j = ∠(n_r, n_j)`. For `R ∈ [1, b]` the ring face `F_R = {y·n_r = R} ∩ ⋂_j {y·n_j ≤ d_j}`
> contains the disk of in-plane radius
>
> ```
> t_R = min_j (d_j − R cos θ_j) / sin θ_j ,
> ```
>
> and on the admissible family of `K15-E099`, `min_{R ∈ [1,b]} t_R ≥ t* = ρ_r/4 = 0.5257311121`.

*Proof of the per-face bound.* Write `n_j = cos θ_j · n_r + sin θ_j · m_j` with `m_j ⊥ n_r`,
`|m_j| = 1`. For a unit in-plane direction `e` and `t ≥ 0` put `y = R n_r + t e`. Then
`y·n_j = R cos θ_j + t sin θ_j (e·m_j) ≤ R cos θ_j + t sin θ_j`, so `y·n_j ≤ d_j` whenever
`t ≤ (d_j − R cos θ_j)/sin θ_j`. Intersecting over `j` gives the disk. ∎

*The minimum over the three classes of competing plane* (`ring_face_disk.py`, all PASS):

| class | `(d, θ)` | `t_R` | min over `R ∈ [1,b]` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| the other four ring faces | `d = R`, `θ = 72°` | `R(1−cos72°)/sin72°` | `0.726542528` at `R = 1` |
| the axial face `c` | `d = 1`, `θ = 121.717474°` | `(1 + 0.525731 R)/0.850651` | `1.793604493` at `R = 1` (increasing in `R`) |
| a free centre at distance `ρ` | `d = ρ/2`, `cos θ ≤ (ρ² + ρ_r² − 4)/(2ρρ_r)` | — | `0.525731112` at `ρ = 2`, `R = b` (decreasing in `R`) |

The free-centre class is the binding one and its minimum is `t*` **exactly**, attained at
`ρ = 2, R = b`: the margin is `+1.11e-16`. So the hypothesis "angle `≥ 58.28252559°` **and**
distance `≥ 1`, jointly" is load-bearing; either half alone is not enough. This is the same
identity as `REVIEW_0608` §11, now attached to the object that the lemma actually needs, and it
closes `G17` without the `(d_j − b cos θ_j)/sin θ_j` reach formula Main used — the two agree at
the binding plane, as they must.

**What the lemma does not give.** `π t*² = 0.868315` is `69 %` of the measured ring-face area
(`1.17–1.28` on the realisable completions). It is a lower bound adequate for the twelve-face
theorem and inadequate for the thirteen-face case, which needs `1.030400` (§4). The distinction
matters and is the reason §5 exists.

## 3. Language repairs: three phrases that claimed more than was computed

1. **`1.172495` is a finite-sample minimum.** It is the least ring-face area over the free-centre
   configurations that were sampled, not the minimum over legal completions. Correct phrasing:
   *"the least sampled ring-face area is `1.172495`; no lower bound over all completions is
   known."* The number may not be used as a lower bound anywhere.
2. **`0.052007` is a grid maximum.** It is the largest cap removed at `d = 1` over a `2° × 4°`
   direction grid on the dodecahedron. A maximum over a grid is a **lower** bound for the true
   maximum, so quoting it as "what a thirteenth face costs" understates the cost — the direction
   of the error is safe for the obstruction in §4 and unsafe for any bound that uses it as an
   upper bound. Correct phrasing: *"some direction costs `0.052007`; the proven maximum is at
   least that and is not known."*
3. **`V_dod`-based accounting with thirteen faces.** The `0.010301` slack
   (`5.550291 + 0.223427 − 5.763417`) is the slack of the **twelve**-face bound. With thirteen
   faces the base step `vol D' ≥ V_dod` is not available at all, so `0.010301` is an upper bound
   for the affordable cap in the most favourable accounting, not the true allowance. Everything
   below is stated with that reading.

Also repaired here: `pair_theorem_12faces.py`'s docstring still called step (1) "Lindelöf". The
primary statement is Fejes Tóth's (restated as Theorem 4.1 of Hales–McLaughlin, p. 16, quoted in
`REVIEW_0738` §3); Lindelöf 1869 is a second, independent route that also covers `n = 12` but not
`n ≥ 13`. Two routes to one premise is worth having; calling the premise by the wrong name is not.

## 4. The thirteen-face case, restated without treating examples as minima

What is actually known, in the order in which it is used:

1. **`K15-E014`**: at most twelve centres lie within `2.09` of `w1`. Hence a thirteenth face has
   bisector distance `d > 1.045`. (Provisional on the Tammes `N = 13` enclosure.)
2. **Distances only, no volumes**: a thirteenth face at distance `d` is *affordable* in the
   twelve-face accounting only if the cap it removes costs at most `0.010301`; the measured cap

## 5. **The counting route is dead** — an explicit legal thirteen-centre configuration

`face_count_obstruction.py`. The configuration of `K15-E099`: `w1` at the origin, `c = (0,0,−2)`
at distance `2`, five ring centres at distance `ρ_r = 2.1029244485`, at colatitude `58.28252506°`
(spacing `72°` in azimuth), i.e. at angle `121.7174744°` from `c`. The script reproduces the two
fixture volumes of `ring_excess.py` — `5.572648770` with all twelve faces at `1` and
`5.898007183` with the five ring faces at `b = 1.0514622242` — as a control that the
orientation is the project's.

> **Proposition (witness).** There are **seven** further centres, all at distance exactly `11/5`
> from `w1`, pairwise at distance `> 2` and at distance `> 2` from all six fixed centres. Hence
> **thirteen centres lie within `2.20` of `w1`**, and the cell of `w1` in that configuration has
> thirteen faces, at distances `1` (six) and `11/10` (seven).

The check is rigorous and uses no floating point. The witness vectors are exact rationals, scaled
by a rational factor `(11/5)/U` with `U` a rational upper bound for `|x|`, so `|x| ≤ 11/5` holds
exactly. The six fixed centres are algebraic and are enclosed as intervals built from
`ρ_r² = 8 − (8/5)√5`, `z_ring = ρ_r²/4` (because `cos 58.28252506° = ρ_r/4` exactly),
`h_ring = √(ρ_r² − z²)` and Taylor-enclosed `cos(2πk/5)`, `sin(2πk/5)`:

```
|x| <= 11/5 for all seven               PASS   (exact rational)
|x| >= 2    for all seven               PASS   (exact rational)
min pairwise squared distance  4.202534192 > 4   PASS   (exact rational)
min squared distance to fixed  [4.030029994, 4.030029994] > 4   PASS   (interval, width 6e-61)
```

Its thirteen-face cell has volume **`6.332553136`** (`22` vertices), far above the threshold
`5.763417470`: the seven extra faces sit at `1.10` and cut almost nothing, so **this is not a
counterexample to `(P')`** and must not be quoted as one.

**One retraction inside this block, because the error class is the project's recurring one.** The
first version of the script put the ring normals at `z = −ρ_r/4` instead of `+ρ_r/4` — the mirror
image through the equator — and the witness it produced solved that mirrored problem, not the
project's. It was caught by recomputing the two `ring_excess.py` control volumes, which came out
`8.44` and `8.65` instead of `5.5726` and `5.8980`. Every number in this section is from the
corrected orientation, and `face_count_obstruction.py` now prints the controls first.

**Why that closes route (c).** A counting statement would have to say "at most six further
centres within `r`" for some `r` with `r/2` beyond the affordability crossing of §4.2, i.e.
`r ≥ 2.20` at the very least. But thirteen centres inside `2.20` exist, and the threshold is not
at the boundary — a numeric bisection puts it at

```
seven further centres fit at r* <= 2.1742;  none found below 2.1725
```

so the counting statement is true only for `r < 2.174` and gives at best `d > 1.0871`, where the
cap costs `≈ 0.0169` — a factor `1.6` above the `0.010301` available. Every refinement of the
counting makes the radius smaller and the shortfall larger. **No bound on the number of centres
with faces inside `1.10` can close the thirteen-face case**, and the `2.8×` in `REVIEW_0738` §4
was the measure of a route that was dead for a different reason than the one it named.

Scope, stated exactly. This closes a *route*, not a case: `(P')` for `13+` faces remains open, and
the honest statement of the remaining obstruction is now

```
needed:      vol D' >= V_dod - eps   for a base with 13 faces, or  A_min >= 1.030400
excluded:    any argument that sees the thirteen centres only through their COUNT
untouched:   the cap table of s4 (a cap constant is not what fails), the ring-excess lemma, the
             twelve-face theorem
```

### 5.1 A configuration with **no** thirteenth face at all: the C5 one

The witness of §5 needs the extra faces at `1.10`. The project's own extremal configuration is
much tighter, and there the thirteen-face question does not arise:

> **Proposition.** Let `C` be the twelve C5 centres (`c` at `2` on the `−z` axis, five ring at
> `ρ_r` at colatitude `58.28252506°`, five at colatitude `112°` and `36° + 72°k`, one at `2` on
> `+z`). Then no centre at distance `≤ 2.604` from `w1` is at distance `≥ 2` from all twelve, and
> a centre at distance `> 2.604` has bisector distance `> 1.302`, which **exceeds the circumradius
> `1.301968` of the C5 cell**. Hence no completion adds a face, and

```
vol Omega(w1) = 5.898007183   for every C5 completion, whatever the number of centres
margin over the threshold 5.763417470 : +0.134590
```

*Proof of the separation claim.* At radius `r = 2.604`, the maximum over directions of the least
distance to the twelve centres is at most `1.678227`: the `600 × 1200` direction grid gives
`1.664593`, and the map `u ↦ min_i |r u − y_i|` is `r`-Lipschitz in the angular distance with
grid step `0.00524` rad, so the true maximum is at most `1.664593 + 0.013635`. That is below `2`,
so nothing at radius `≤ 2.604` is admissible. ∎ *(both checks: `face_count_obstruction.py`, section (1b), which also recomputes the C5 volume from the twelve normals)*

**Why this matters, and what it does not say.** It says the twelve-face hypothesis is *free* for
the configuration the project actually uses: `K15-E099`'s margin `+0.134590` does not depend on
Fejes Tóth at all there, because there is no thirteenth face to worry about. It does **not** close
the general thirteen-face case: a 13th face requires a base distorted away from the icosahedral
shape, and the question is exactly how far it must distort — the sharp form of that trade-off is
`(S_dod)`, and §5 shows the trade cannot be bypassed by counting.

### 5.2 The direct minimisation over thirteen-facet cells: what it did and did not establish

The obvious follow-up is to minimise `vol Ω(w1)` over legal completions whose cell has thirteen
**facets**. Three things came out of trying, and the first two are warnings.

**(a) The twelve-face control caught a mis-specified search.** A push-in search — scale the free
centres toward `w1` until some 2-separation binds, perturb, repeat — was first run with the
**five ring planes at distance `1` instead of `b`**. Its six-free-centre control then returned
`5.634127`, *below* the twelve-face theorem's `5.7737`, which is impossible for a twelve-facet
cell. With the ring distance corrected the control returns **`5.965151` ≥ `5.7737`** and is
satisfied. Every number in this subsection is from the corrected version. This is the third
instance of the project's recurring error class (after the retracted `0.707055` and the "every
cell fcc" of `K15-E075`): a number produced by an object that is not the object the statement is
about.

**(b) The volume objective does not flow to thirteen facets.** With seven free centres the same
search reaches **`6.080235472`**, but that cell has **twelve** facets: the seventh centre sits at
`|x| = 3.616510`, its bisector at `1.808` misses the cell, and the objective is indifferent. A
search that only minimises volume will therefore report a "thirteen-centre" configuration that is
not a thirteen-face one. §5's witness *is* a genuine thirteen-facet cell — all thirteen planes
support facets, `22` vertices, `6.332553136` — so such cells exist; they are simply not what an
unconstrained volume minimisation finds.

**(c) With the facet condition enforced, no thirteen-facet completion was found in `40 s`.** Eight
structured starts, a jam-and-perturb loop of `2 200` steps each, rejecting any configuration whose
cell has fewer than thirteen facets: zero hits. Consistent with §5.1 — a thirteenth facet needs a
base distorted far enough to open a hole, and *that distortion is the variable to search over*, not
the centre positions.

So the honest state of the thirteen-face case at the end of this block is: **open**, with two of
its three proposed routes closed (`REVIEW_0738` §4's counting route by §5, and the "better cap
constant" by the same section's arithmetic), the C5 configuration settled unconditionally (§5.1),
and the remaining route a **distortion-versus-cap trade-off** which this block specified but did
not run:

```
object      min vol over legal seven-free-centre completions with all 13 planes supporting facets
variables   the six free centres' directions AND radii (the distortion), plus the seventh
constraint  every plane a facet (>= 3 vertices on it), checked, never assumed
control     six free centres with the ring at b must return >= 5.7737
```

## 6. The auxiliary-function side: the obstruction, and the one instance evaluated

Main's `C138` records the CE hybrid as structurally failed; the numbers below are from the
project's own record (`ANALYTIC_LOCAL_MASS.md`, `CANDIDATES.md`, `Literature/CORE_SOURCES.md`),

| CDS22 three-point (published) | `0.770271` | `+4.02 %` | Fourier, 3-point |
| DLOV14 strengthened two-point | `0.773080` | `+4.40 %` | Fourier + local rows |

**The obstruction, and it is a theorem, not a numerical observation.** Li (Adv. Math. 460 (2024)
110043, Thm 6.1, as recorded in `CORE_SOURCES.md` LI24) proves that the *optimal* 3D ordinary
two-point bound exceeds `0.18398089` in centre density, i.e. `0.77061` in occupied volume. So
**no** admissible auxiliary function of the ordinary two-point class reaches even `0.7706`, which
is `4.07 %` above the truth. Ordinary CE is obstructed as a matter of proof, not of degree or
solver strength; adding local-mass rows does not touch the class it is obstructed in (`C138`), and
the three-point class — the only widening that is known to move the number at all — moves it to
`0.770271`, still `4.02 %` short, and this project has no three-point evaluator.

**The small 3D instance actually evaluated this block** is the counting instance of §5, which is
the natural "one instance" test because it is exactly the auxiliary object the geometric route
needs: a bound on the number of `2`-separated centres in a ball, i.e. a Delsarte-type spherical
problem with the six fixed centres as forbidden caps. Explicit inequality, computable objective,
both comparisons available:

| instance | objective | control | result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| count in `B(w1, 2.09)` | max `#` of `2`-separated centres | unconstrained kissing number `12` | `≤ 12` (`K15-E014`, provisional) |
| count in `B(w1, 2.20)` | same, with six forbidden caps | unconstrained count at `2.20` | **`13` — the bound needed is false** |
| count in `B(w1, 2.174)` | same | — | threshold, found numerically |

So the instance is decisive where it is asked and negative where it is decisive. That is a
stronger outcome than a reformulation, and it is worth saying plainly: the analytic and the local
route have now both been pushed to a *proof* of their own insufficiency at the specific step each
was supposed to supply — Li's theorem for the Fourier side, §5 for the counting side. The local
route remains the live one because its remaining gap (`1.92 %` at twelve faces; the thirteen-face
case open) is the smallest, and because §5 removes one of the two ways of attacking it, leaving
the face-area bound `A_min ≥ 1.0304` and the stability statement `(S_dod)`.
