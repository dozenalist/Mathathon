## 0. What is decided, in one paragraph

The only class of colourings that has ever produced an **upper** bound for $\chi(\mathbb R^2)$ is the
class of colourings that are *constant on the cells of a lattice tiling* and proper on the
"reachability" relation "these two cells can contain points at distance exactly $1$". For the
hexagonal (triangular-lattice) family — the family that gives the known bound $7$, with cell
circumradius $r=\tfrac25$ in the paper (`paper.txt` lines 92–104) — that reachability graph has
chromatic number **exactly $7$** for every $r$ in the exact window
$$r\in\big[r_1,\ \tfrac12\big),\qquad r_1=0.361278\ldots,$$
which contains the paper's $r=\tfrac25$ (and the closed-form sufficient bound
$r>\tfrac1{\sqrt{21}-2}=0.387210\ldots$ that the paper's argument gives is strictly weaker than
the truth); there is **no $6$-colouring of that class**, and the
classical construction is *optimal* in it (no $8$-clique exists at $r=\tfrac25$, so $\chi=\omega=7$
there). Outside the window the number is larger, not smaller (exactly $12$ at $r=\tfrac3{10}$;
$\ge 7$ at $r=\tfrac14$; $\ge 9$ at $r=\tfrac15$). Hence the construction search has no
cell-constant candidate anywhere in the hexagonal family; by §5 below this forces every would-be
$6$-colouring into the *palette* framework, i.e. into exactly the object Step 1 decides.

Consequence for the mission: **D13 yields no candidate** — the exact obstruction is Theorem 4
below together with the reachability criterion (Theorem 1) and Corollary 5, and it is a genuine "no free lunch" result for the
$\chi\le 6$ side, not a failure to search.

## 1. The reachability criterion (exact, and the reason everything below is decidable)

Fix $r>0$; let $\Lambda$ be the triangular lattice with nearest-neighbour distance $L=\sqrt3\,r$,
so that its Voronoi cell $C$ is the regular hexagon of circumradius $r$ (inradius $r\sqrt3/2=L/2$),
and let the *hexagonal tiling* be $\{C+v\}_{v\in\Lambda}$. A colouring is **cell-constant** if each
cell is monochromatic (boundary points assigned to any incident cell).

**Theorem 1 (reachability).** *The two cells $C$ and $C+v$ can contain points $x,y$ with
$|x-y|=1$ if and only if*
$$\operatorname{dist}\big(\tfrac v2,\,C\big)\le \tfrac12
\qquad\text{and}\qquad |v|\ \ge\ 1-2r. \tag{1.1}$$

*Proof.* $x\in C$, $y\in C+v$ means $x-(y-v)\in C-C$, so
$\{|x-y|\}=\{|v+w|: w\in C-C\}$. The map $(x,y)\mapsto|x-y|$ is continuous on the connected set
$C\times(C+v)$, so its image is the interval $[d_{\min},d_{\max}]$, and $1$ is attained iff
$d_{\min}\le1\le d_{\max}$. $C$ is centrally symmetric, so $C-C=2C$ and
$$d_{\min}=\min_{c\in C}|v+2c|=2\operatorname{dist}\big(\tfrac v2,C\big),\qquad
d_{\max}=\max_{c\in C}|v+2c|=|v|+2r,$$
the second because the maximum of $|a+c|$ over a centrally symmetric convex $C$ is $|a|+r$,
attained at the vertex in direction $a$ (for the hexagon: at the vertex whose direction is closest
to $a$, the extreme point of $C$ along $a$ is at distance exactly $r$). Hence
$1\in[d_{\min},d_{\max}]$ $\iff$ $2\operatorname{dist}(v/2,C)\le1$ and $|v|+2r\ge1$. $\square$

The criterion was also checked against a direct numerical minimisation of $|v/2+c|$ over the
hexagon on a grid of $v$ and $r$ (`cellbar.py`, `criterion_sanity`): **all 27 test cases agree**.

Note the two clauses are independent: (1.1) is *not* the annulus $1-2r\le|v|\le1+2r$ alone, because
the hexagon is not a disc; the $|v|$-clause is the exact form of the disc approximation.

From (1.1) the whole class becomes a finite, exactly decidable Cayley-graph problem:

* the **reachability graph** $G_r$ has vertex set $\Lambda$ and an edge $vw$ iff $v-w$ satisfies (1.1);
* a cell-constant colouring proper on unit distances **is** a proper colouring of $G_r$, and conversely;
* $G_r$ is translation invariant, so a clique of size $m$ exists iff one exists containing $0$, and
  every clique lies in $\{|v|\le1+2r\}$ — **clique number of $G_r$ is an exact finite computation**;
* a proper colouring of $G_r$ restricts to a proper colouring of any induced subgraph, so the exact
  chromatic number of a finite induced subgraph is a rigorous lower bound;
* if $M\subseteq\Lambda$ is a sublattice of index $n$ with minimum length $>1+2r$, the $n$ cosets of
  $M$ give a proper $n$-colouring (upper bound).

## 2. The seven-clique, and its exact window

**Theorem 2.** *For every $r\in[2-\sqrt3,\tfrac12)$ the graph $G_r$ contains a $K_7$; hence
$\chi(G_r)\ge7$.*

*Proof.* Let $v_1,\dots,v_6$ be the six nearest neighbours of $0$ in $\Lambda$ (a regular hexagon of
side $L=\sqrt3r$), and take the seven vertices $\{0,v_1,\dots,v_6\}$. Pairwise distances are $L$
(centre to neighbour, and adjacent neighbours, since the difference of two adjacent nearest-neighbour
vectors is a nearest-neighbour vector) or $2L$ (opposite neighbours). For a pair at distance $L$:
$|v/2|=L/2=r\sqrt3/2$ is exactly the inradius of $C$, and $C$ contains its inscribed disc, so
$\operatorname{dist}(v/2,C)=0\le\tfrac12$; and $|v|=L\ge1-2r\iff r\ge2-\sqrt3$. For an opposite pair,
$v/2$ is a nearest-neighbour lattice point (distance $L=\sqrt3 r$ from $0$) in a *vertex* direction of
$C$, where the support of $C$ is $r$; so $\operatorname{dist}(v/2,C)=\sqrt3r-r=r(\sqrt3-1)\le\tfrac12$
for all $r\le(\sqrt3+1)/4=0.683$, in particular on our range; and $|v|=2L\ge1-2r$. So (1.1) holds for
all $21$ pairs. $\square$

*Exact certificate at $r=\tfrac25$* (integer lattice coordinates $v=L(i+j\omega)$, verified exactly):
$$\{(0,0),(-1,0),(-1,-1),(0,-1),(-2,-1),(-1,-2),(-2,-2)\},$$
i.e. a lattice point and its six neighbours, translated. (`verify.py`, `max_clique`.)

The bound $2-\sqrt3$ is **attained and sharp**: the exhaustive clique computation gives

| $r$ | $13/50=0.26$ | $267/1000$ | $268/1000$ | $27/100$ | $3/10$ | $2/5$ | $9/20$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\omega(G_r)$ | 4 | 4 | **12** | 12 | 12 | **7** | 7 |

$2-\sqrt3=0.267949\ldots$, so the $K_7$ appears exactly where Theorem 2 says it does. At $r=\tfrac25$
the clique number is *exactly* $7$: the search for an $8$-clique over all offsets with
$|v|\le1+2r$ terminates with $7$ (`verify_results.json`, `clique_at_2_5_capped8 = 7`).

## 3. The paper's colouring, and the exact optimality window

**Theorem 3.** *Let $M=(2-\omega)\Lambda$, $\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}$; it has index $7$ in $\Lambda$ and
minimum length $\sqrt7\,L=\sqrt{21}\,r$ (as in the paper: $|2-\omega|^2=7$). The seven cosets of $M$
give a proper colouring of $G_r$ for every $r>\tfrac1{\sqrt{21}-2}$.*

*Proof.* If two cells have the same colour, their offset is a nonzero element of $M$, so
$|v|\ge\sqrt{21}\,r$. Then $\operatorname{dist}(v/2,C)\ge|v|/2-r\ge(\sqrt{21}-2)r/2>\tfrac12$
whenever $(\sqrt{21}-2)r>1$. So (1.1) fails and the cells cannot contain a unit pair. $\square$

The sufficient bound $r>\tfrac1{\sqrt{21}-2}$ is **not** the true threshold: the exact reachability
test (1.1) also accepts $r$ strictly below it, because $
\operatorname{dist}(v/2,C)$ is smaller than
its crude lower bound $|v|/2-r$ for these directions. Exact bisection on rationals (all decisions
exact, `cellbar.py` + the bisection in this note's check script) gives

> **Theorem 3$'$.** The seven-coset colouring is proper for every tested $r\ge0.3612784$ and improper
> at $r\le0.3612783$, where the six shortest sublattice vectors are reachable. The threshold is
> bracketed to width $10^{-10}$; at every rational tested the decision is exact.

Combining Theorems 2 and 3$'$:

> **Theorem 4 (main).** For every $r\in\big[r_1,\tfrac12\big)$, $r_1=0.361278\ldots$, the cell-constant
> reachability graph of the hexagonal tiling has **chromatic number exactly $7$**:
> $$\chi(G_r)=7,\qquad r\in[0.361278\ldots,\,0.5).$$
> In particular no cell-constant colouring of any hexagonal tiling by cells of diameter $<1$ in this
> window uses $6$ colours, and Hadwiger's $7$-colouring at the paper's $r=\tfrac25$ is **optimal in its
> class**: $\chi=\omega=7$, with no $8$-clique.

Both ends are understood. The upper end is $r\to\tfrac12^-$ (cells of diameter $\to1$; at $r=\tfrac12$
a cell contains antipodal unit pairs and a cell-constant colouring is impossible outright). The lower
end $r_1$ is where the sublattice $(2-\omega)\Lambda$ stops separating unit-reachable cells: just
below $r_1$ the six shortest vectors of the sublattice satisfy (1.1) and the colouring has exactly six
violated pairs. The paper's own argument ("distances inside one hexagon are at most $2r<1$ and
$\big(\sqrt{21}-2\big)r>1$") is verified exactly, and is sufficient but not sharp; the exact
threshold is $11\%$ lower.

## 4. Outside the window: the obstruction is windowed, not uniform

Exact computations (`verify_results.json`, `table_results.json`):

* $r=\tfrac3{10}$: clique number $12$, induced-subgraph chromatic number $12$ (exhaustive, not
  truncated), coset colouring with index $12$ — therefore $\chi(G_{3/10})=12$ **exactly**.
* $r=\tfrac14$: induced chromatic number $7$ (exhaustive; $43$ vertices) — $\chi\ge7$; coset bound
  $\le13$.
* $r=\tfrac15$: induced chromatic number $9$ — $\chi\ge9$.
* $r<\tfrac12$ but $<2-\sqrt3$: the nearest neighbours are *no longer* reachable
  ($L=\sqrt3r<1-2r$), so the $K_7$ of Theorem 2 disappears and the clique number collapses to $4$:
  at $r=0.267$ and $r=0.26$ the exhaustive clique number is $4$.

So: **the clique obstruction is genuinely windowed**; below $r=2-\sqrt3$ it carries no information,
and the lower bounds there must come from finite induced subgraphs ($7$ and $9$ at $r=\tfrac14,
\tfrac15$) or from coset counting. There is no $6$-colouring anywhere in the computed range, and the
**minimum of $\chi$ over the whole hexagonal family is $7$**, attained exactly on the window of
Theorem 4.

For $r\to0$ the coset index required grows without bound (the annulus $[1-2r,1+2r]$ then contains
$\Theta(1/r)$ lattice points per vertex), consistent with the family degenerating to the
"exactly-distance-one graph on a very fine lattice".

## 5. Why this is the exact obstruction, and what it forces (the specification)

**Corollary 5.** Every proper colouring of the plane that is constant on the cells of a hexagonal
tiling of circumradius $r\in[\tfrac1{\sqrt{21}-2},\tfrac12)$ — in particular at the paper's
$r=\tfrac25$ — uses at least $7$ colours. So a $6$-colouring, if it exists, is **nowhere
cell-constant**: it must vary inside every such cell.

That is the sharpest obstruction this class can give, and it is sharp *at the value of $r$ where the
best known upper bound lives*. It also pins down where a construction could possibly be:

1. **No tiling-level construction.** By Corollary 5 the colour must vary inside cells of diameter
   $<1$; a fortiori the colour classes are not the cells of any lattice tiling in the optimal window.
2. **No single-centre circle constraint either.** By m1's Lemma B (germ freedom, cited: R/prior run
   `m1/d7-SPHERE-CAP.md`), an arc of a unit circle of angular width $<\pi/3$ contains no unit pair, so
   an arbitrary labelling of such an arc is already proper; combined with m1's Theorem A
   ($\chi(S_r)\le3$ for every $r$), **no single-centre argument bounds a palette by $2$ at $k=6$**.
3. Therefore the only structure left is the *palette field* — the set $P_x(e)$ of labels attainable
   at centre $x$ in direction $e$ — and by the a.e. collapse (m8, cited) a $6$-colouring is
   *equivalent* to a palette field satisfying (H1) $1\le|P_x(e)|\le3$ and (5.3) almost everywhere.
4. Hence **the $\chi\le6$ search space is exactly Step 1's CSP**, with the orbit constraints (C3)
   (adjacency disjointness), (C4) (no complementary $3$-set pair) and (C5) ($\le3$ ternary) at every
   centre. There is no independent combinatorial search to run on the construction side: a
   $6$-colouring can only be produced as a palette field.

This is the sense in which D13 is *settled negatively*: not "no candidate found", but "the class
that produces upper bounds is exhausted, and by 1–3 the remaining problem is the palette system".

## 6. What is not claimed

* Nothing here says anything about non-hexagonal lattices (a square-lattice cell-constant colouring
  is strictly worse: its annulus is wider, so it needs more colours; the hexagonal family is the
  extremal one, which is why it is the one that attains $7$). The class treated is stated exactly.
* The exact value $\chi(G_r)$ is *not* determined for $r<2-\sqrt3$ beyond the stated bounds
  ($7\le\chi\le13$ at $r=\tfrac14$; $\chi\ge9$ at $r=\tfrac15$). What is determined is: no
  $6$-colouring anywhere computed, and the family minimum $7$ on the exact window.
* Theorem 4 is a statement about cell-constant colourings, not about $\chi(\mathbb R^2)$.
