COMMON FRAMEWORK FOR THE LOCAL ARITHMETIC CONSTRUCTIONS
Research notes, 14 November 2026

Scope and status
These notes reorganize Sections 3--5 of Aabir Fauzan, "zeta(5) is
irrational", manuscript dated 17 September 2026, as supplied in
sources/Fauzan_2026_zeta5.txt and .pdf. They explain exact identities and
derive a common framework. They do not independently certify every estimate
or the manuscript's claimed irrationality theorem. The Volkenborn and
determinant-line language below is a reinterpretation, not terminology the
manuscript itself consistently uses.

Main conclusion
The local arithmetic can be organized around one object:

  a one-parameter rational moment pairing, together with its lattices at
  each prime and the induced pairing on its one-dimensional determinant
  line.

Its scalar functional is a singular extension of a differentiated
Bernoulli/Volkenborn functional. Integer-valued bases, CRT/jet bases,
vanishing orders, row weights, and bounded-rank corrections are different
ways of estimating the same pairing over Q_p with X still indeterminate.
The limiting profiles and prime integrals summarize those local estimates;
they are not additional moment functionals. The periodic tail corrections
are Bernoulli polynomials again, now doing the usual summation-to-integration
work of Euler--Maclaurin theory.

1. THE EXACT BERNOULLI/VOLKENBORN CORE

Write I_p for the Volkenborn integral, initially on polynomials:

  I_p(f) = lim_{m -> infinity} p^{-m} sum_{a=0}^{p^m-1} f(a),

where the limit is in Q_p. For every k >= 0,

  I_p(x^k) = B_k,     B_1 = -1/2.

Consequently the manuscript's polynomial functional is exactly

  tau(P) = I_p(P''')/24.                              [manuscript Sec.3]

This value is rational and independent of p when P has rational
coefficients. The p-adic realization is used to estimate it, not to define
a different rational functional at every prime. The Bernoulli moment
identity and translation law are recorded in the primary research sources
[V], [HKK], and [L]
below; the identification with this manuscript's tau is our direct
substitution into its definition.

There is also a purely formal, prime-independent description. If D=d/dx,
then on polynomials

  L(P) = [ D/(exp(D)-1) P ](0),
  tau(P) = [ D^4/(24(exp(D)-1)) P ](0).

The quotients are interpreted as formal power series in D. They terminate
when applied to a polynomial; D^4/(exp(D)-1) starts with D^3. Equivalently,

  tau(exp(sx)) = s^4/(24(exp(s)-1))

as an identity of formal power series. This one expression explains the
specific derivative order, finite-difference law, and Bernoulli moments.

Let Delta g(x)=g(x+1)-g(x). The standard Volkenborn identity

  I_p(f(x+1))-I_p(f(x)) = f'(0)

gives

  tau(Delta P) = P''''(0)/24.                         [(3.3) on polynomials]

The Bernoulli reflection identity gives I_p(P(-1-x))=I_p(P(x)). Three
differentiations introduce a minus sign, so

  tau(P(-1-x)) = -tau(P(x)).                          [(3.2)]

Splitting the defining finite sum into the p residue classes gives

  I_p(f) = p^{-1} sum_{a=0}^{p-1} I_p(f(a+px)).

Since (d/dx)^3 P(a+px)=p^3 P'''(a+px),

  tau(P) = p^{-4} sum_{a=0}^{p-1} tau(P(a+px)).

Thus p^{-4} is forced by one p^{-1} from averaging and p^{-3} from the
three derivatives. It is not an isolated normalization chosen in Sec.3.2.

Caveat: this is not ordinary real integration and is not a bounded
translation-invariant Q_p-valued Haar measure on all continuous functions.
For instance I_p(x)=-1/2, and its translation defect is explicit above.
The functions 1/(x-r), r an integer, have poles on Z_p, so the ordinary
Volkenborn integral of such a function is not what the manuscript defines.

2. A ONE-PARAMETER SINGULAR EXTENSION

Every rational function with polynomial part and simple integer poles has
a unique decomposition

  g(x)=P(x)+sum_r c_r/(x-r),

with only finitely many c_r nonzero. Put

  beta_r(X)=H^{(5)}_{d(r)}-X,
  d(r)=r if r>=0, and d(r)=-r-1 if r<0.

The manuscript's definition is exactly

  tau_X(g)=tau(P)+sum_r c_r beta_r(X).

This is an algebraic extension of tau. Among extensions satisfying its
reflection and regular-at-zero difference laws, the whole pole family is
determined by one value, beta_0=-X. Indeed, applying the difference identity
to 1/(x-r), with r>0, gives

  beta_{r-1}-beta_r = -r^{-5},
  beta_r=beta_0+H_r^{(5)}.

Reflection gives beta_{-r-1}=beta_r. Thus the one free parameter propagates
to every integer pole. The fifth-order harmonic increments are forced by
the fourth derivative in the difference law:

  (1/24) (d/dx)^4 (1/(x-r))|_{x=0} = -r^{-5}.

Another useful exact formula is

  tau_X(g) = tau_0(g) - X sum_r c_r.

Hence [X]tau_X(g)=-sum_r Res_{x=r}(g dx)=Res_infinity(g dx).
This makes its affine dependence on X transparent. The phrase "singular
extension" is descriptive here; no canonical p-adic finite-part integral
at integer poles is being asserted. The free parameter is a formal
coordinate, not an identification of real zeta(5) with a p-adic zeta value.

3. mu_X IS THE SAME FUNCTIONAL IN WEIGHTED SQUARE COORDINATES

The exact identity is

  mu_X(R(t)) = tau_X(x^5 R(-x^2)).                    [(3.1), p.6]

It is essential to retain the factor x^5. On monomials this gives

  tau((-1)^e x^{2e+5})
    = (-1)^e (2e+3)(2e+4)(2e+5) B_{2e+2}/24,

which is (2.2). For simple poles,

  x^5/(j^2-x^2)
    = -x^3-j^2 x-(j^4/2)(1/(x-j)+1/(x+j)).

Using tau(x^3)=1/4, tau(x)=0, beta_j=H_j^{(5)}-X,
beta_{-j}=H_{j-1}^{(5)}-X, and H_j-H_{j-1}=j^{-5}, one obtains

  mu_X(1/(t+j^2)) = j^4(X-H_j^{(5)})-1/4+1/(2j).

Thus the square-pole functional, the integer-pole functional, the
Bernoulli moments, and the harmonic pole data really belong to the same
construction. The coordinate transformation makes local residue classes
accessible: t=-a^2 corresponds to x=a or -a mod p. At a=0, a zero in t has
twice the order in x, explaining the doubled orders in (4.3).

4. COMPLETION AND THE PARAMETER CHANGE UNDER DILATION

For p>=7, write

  kappa_d=d(d-1)(d-2)B_{d-3}/24,   kappa_0=kappa_1=kappa_2=0.

The bounds v_p(kappa_d)>=-1 and v_p(kappa_d)>=0 for d<=p+1
allow tau to extend to Q_p<z> by its convergent moment sum. If T has
distinct near roots r_nu in Z_p with unit pairwise differences, the natural
space is

  B_T = Q_p<z> direct-sum (direct-sum_nu Q_p/(z-r_nu)).

The second expression denotes the one-dimensional span of 1/(z-r_nu),
not a quotient ring. On it the manuscript defines

  tau_Y^ext(f+sum c_nu/(z-r_nu))
     = tau(f)+sum c_nu(H_{d(r_nu)}^{(5)}-Y).

The analytic and principal-part decomposition is unique. A far pole s,
v_p(s)<0, is analytic on the unit disc. Its geometric expansion yields

  tau^an(1/(z-s))
    = -(1/4) sum_{k>=0} binom(k+3,3) B_k s^{-k-4}.    [(3.5)]

This is the same tau evaluated on a convergent series, not another
arbitrarily chosen function. The geometric series and moment bound justify
termwise evaluation here. Lemma 3.1 additionally uses a low-degree leading
numerator to avoid the possible loss of one power of p in the analytic
part; factors p^j absorb that loss in subsequent terms. Merely naming the
functional does not eliminate this integrality check.

Set

  C_p=sum_{a=1}^{p-1} tau^an(1/(x+a/p)),
  Y=p^5 X+C_p.                                      [(3.6)]

This parameter is forced, not guessed. For g=1/x, the right side of the
desired distribution law is

  p^{-4} sum_a tau_Y^ext(1/(a+px))
     = p^{-5}(-Y+C_p).

Equating this with tau_X(1/x)=-X gives exactly Y=p^5X+C_p. The fifth power
comes from the p^{-4} functional scaling and the additional p^{-1} of a
simple pole. The reflection and difference recurrences then establish the
law at every integer pole; the polynomial part was handled above. This
proves the mechanism behind (3.7).

For p>=7, C_p is in p^5 Z_p: its k=0 contribution has p^4 times
sum_{a=1}^{p-1}a^{-4}, which vanishes modulo p; the other terms have the
valuations stated in Sec.3.2. Therefore Y is integral (in fact divisible by
p^5) when X is integral. This is why the formal change of parameter fits
the integral local estimates.

5. THE COMMON BILINEAR FORM AND ITS DETERMINANT LINE

Let E_K=Q[t]_{<h}, h=K-N, and define

  B_{K,X}(f,g)=mu_X(D_N(t)^6 f(t)g(t)/D_K(t)).

Then G_K is its Gram matrix in 1,t,...,t^{h-1}, and Delta_K=det G_K.
Canceling the first N denominator factors gives the identical expression

  D_N^6/D_K = W/D_tail,   W=D_N^5,   D_tail=D_K/D_N.

This is why the sixth power in one section becomes a fifth power in
another. It is the same rational weight after cancellation.

There is an additional exact finite-algebra description of the coefficient
of X. If [t^{-3}] means Laurent coefficient at infinity, then

  [X]mu_X(R)=[t^{-3}]R,

because (t+j^2)^{-1}=t^{-1}-j^2 t^{-2}+j^4 t^{-3}+...,
while the polynomial part contributes no negative powers. Let

  A_K=Q[t]/D_tail(t),
  rho(P mod D_tail)=[t^{h-1}](remainder of P modulo D_tail).

Equivalently rho(P)=[t^{-1}]P(t)/D_tail(t). The slope form is

  [X]B_{K,X}(f,g)=rho(t^2 W f g).

Thus it is a residue pairing on the finite algebra A_K, with multiplier
t^2 W=t^2D_N^5. Over Q, D_tail is separable and the multiplier is nonzero
at every root -j^2, N<j<=K. The slope pairing is nondegenerate. Evaluation
at those roots turns it into the diagonal weights

  j^4 D_N(-j^2)^5 / D_tail'(-j^2),

exactly as in Sec.2.3. This is another explanation of the full determinant
degree h. Over Z_p, however, the roots may collide modulo p; resolving
those collisions is the reason for the residue-class bases.

Crucial limitation: the full B_{K,X} does not reduce to this slope pairing.
Its constant term also has the Bernoulli polynomial-quotient contribution.
Replacing the whole functional by the finite residue algebra would lose
precisely the terms controlled by the outer rank correction.

A symmetric bilinear form on E_K induces a bilinear form on the
one-dimensional line det(E_K)=exterior-power^h E_K:

  (f_1 wedge ... wedge f_h, g_1 wedge ... wedge g_h)
     |-> det[B_{K,X}(f_i,g_j)].

Under a basis change T its matrix determinant changes by (det T)^2.
This elementary identity is the precise meaning of "the same determinant
line" here. It explains both the exact normalization scalar in Sec.3.3
and the invariance under the unimodular local bases of Sec.4.

At a prime p, keep X indeterminate. The Gauss valuation is

  v_p^G(sum a_j X^j)=min_j v_p(a_j).

It extends to Q_p(X), so one may view the problem as estimating this form
over the associated valuation ring. This keeps every coefficient,
including the constant one, under control. Evaluating at a favorable X
would lose that information.

6. THREE FAMILIES OF BASES, ONE LOCAL INTEGRALITY PROBLEM

(a) Small primes: an integer-valued polynomial lattice.

The q_i of Sec.3.3 obey

  q_i(-x^2)=binom(x+i,2i)+binom(x+i-1,2i),
  D_N(-x^2)/(N!)^2=binom(N-x,N)binom(N+x,N).

They therefore lie in an integer-valued polynomial lattice after pullback.
This does not assert that the displayed q_i form a basis of every possible
even integer-valued lattice; their integrality and triangular leading
coefficients suffice. Put

  f_i=(D_N/(N!)^2)^3 q_i.

Then the manuscript's exact determinant identity is

  F_K=det[(K!)^2 mu_X(f_i f_j/D_K)].                  [(3.11)]

If C is the change from monomials to q_i, this is

  F_K = [(K!)^2/(N!)^12]^h (det C)^2 Delta_K.

The leading coefficients q_0=1 and lc(q_i)=(-1)^i 2/(2i)!, i>=1,
give precisely S_K. The scalar is the coordinate cost of the chosen
integral structure. Lemma 3.3 controls all polynomial and pole pieces with
a coarse estimate adequate for small primes.

(b) Inner primes: local jets and Chinese remainders.

For pairwise distinct square classes c, the ideals (t+c^2)^{L_c} are
pairwise comaximal over Z_p[t]. Their quotient algebra decomposes into
the product of the corresponding local jet algebras. The basis

  E_{a,i}(t)=product_{c != a}(t+c^2)^{L_c}(t+a^2)^i,
  0<=i<L_a,

is triangular with unit diagonal in each local factor. It is thus a
Z_p-unimodular basis of the h-dimensional polynomial lattice, not a new
moment construction. Its job is to make all but one block vanish to high
order at a given source. Distribution then converts the jet orders into
valuation contributions (4.2)--(4.3).

The allocation L_a balances these local requirements. The extra-row rule
in decreasing order of pole count prevents another residue class from
giving a worse bound than the assigned row weight. This is a genuine
finite optimization/verification step; CRT alone does not prove it.

(c) Outer primes: exact pole cancellation within the same CRT picture.

When each class has few poles, use the exact class factors Q_a of D_tail
and P_a=D_tail/Q_a. The rows P_a q_{a,i} localize to one pole class, while
the factor E_a in the later q_{a,i} cancels every dangerous pole j>p.
Their remaining factors are monic of successive degrees, so the same
unit-resultant/CRT argument proves unimodularity. The split at i=ell-2
records when pole cancellation becomes complete.

These inner and outer constructions share a local-jet principle, but
they are not the same basis and need different estimates. The small-prime
basis is also not unimodular in the original monomial lattice: its
determinant is explicitly accounted for by S_K.

More precisely, the outer CRT is the exact decomposition of the pole
order Z_p[t]/D_tail into Z_p[t]/Q_a. The inner CRT instead uses the
auxiliary algebra Z_p[t]/product_a(t+a^2)^{L_a}. Its modulus generally
differs from D_tail. Both identify the same coefficient lattice of
polynomials of degree <h, but they are not literally the same algebra.

7. WEIGHTS AND RANK CORRECTIONS AS DETERMINANT ESTIMATES

Suppose a basis e_i satisfies

  v_p^G(B(e_i,e_j)) >= w_i+w_j.

Every permutation term in the determinant then has valuation at least
2 sum_i w_i. This is the common mechanism behind the inner and outer row
weights. It can be interpreted as making the pairing integral on a
weighted lattice. For half-integral weights, that literal lattice may
require adjoining a square root of p; alternatively keep the entirely
elementary inequality above, or use doubled valuations. Do not pretend
that p^{1/2} belongs to Q_p.

In the outer range, the polynomial moments are first made integral:

  mu_0(t^e)=mu(t^e)-c_e/p,
  G_K=A+p^{-1}L,   rank_{Q_p} L <= r_p.              [(4.10)]

The correction is supported on sufficiently high polynomial degrees, so
it has bounded rank. This is another filtration: the nonintegral
polynomial moments only see a small high-degree portion of the space.
It is not permissible to throw them away and estimate only residues.

For nonpositive half-integral row weights and z zero weights,

  v det(A+p^{-1}L) >= 2 sum_i w_i - min(r,z).        [(4.13)]

Reason: expand by complementary minors. A term with k rows/columns from
L pays p^{-k}; terms k>r vanish. Replacing a row or column with a negative
weight recovers enough valuation to offset that loss, whereas zero
weights do not. Sorting the weights gives the maximum loss min(r,z).
Thus the rank correction is an exterior-power statement: exterior powers
of L above its rank vanish. Its exact rank is over Q_p, as in the
manuscript, not merely a claimed rank modulo p.

Notation caution: the correction matrix L here has no relation to the
Bernoulli functional named L at the start of Sec.3.

8. LIMITING PROFILES ARE BOOKKEEPING FOR THE SAME LATTICE COST

The discrete counts have the exact relation

  ell_A(a)=ell(A/p,a/p),
  ell(x,z)=floor(x-z)+floor(x+z)+1.

The local determinant bound and the change-of-basis scalar then satisfy

  gamma_p^in=p Gamma(K/p)+O_M(1),
  v_p(S_K)=p N(K/p)+O_M(1).                         [(5.7)]

Thus R(x)=-Gamma(x)-N(x) is the limiting signed cost of normalization,
not an unrelated external field. In the outer range,

  -gamma_p^out=K(R_0(p/K)-d(p/K))+O(1).

Here d is exactly the limiting saving due to the restriction by the
number of zero weights in the rank lemma. The variables x=K/p and y=p/K
are reciprocal coordinates chosen to keep the respective intervals
convenient; they do not make the inner and outer estimates identical.

The prime number theorem turns sums of these costs into integrals:

  K^{-2} sum_{K/M<p<=K/3} p f(K/p) log p
      -> integral_3^M f(x) x^{-3} dx.

The power x^{-3} comes from p=K/x and the Jacobian dp=-K x^{-2}dx.
This is a limiting sum of local determinant estimates. It is not the
real equilibrium-energy integral appearing in Sec.6.

9. THE TAIL FUNCTIONS ARE PERIODIC BERNOULLI PRIMITIVES

The paper writes R(x)=xF(x)+Q(x), where

  F(x)=4 lambda+2 lambda {x}-12 lambda {alpha x},
  alpha=3/40, lambda=37/40.

Put Btilde_n(u)=B_n({u}), with endpoint conventions immaterial for the
almost-everywhere derivative/integral identities below. Since

  B_1(v)=v-1/2,
  B_2(v)=v^2-v+1/6,
  B_3(v)=v^3-(3/2)v^2+v/2,

and 6 lambda/alpha=74, one obtains exactly

  F(x)+lambda
    =2 lambda Btilde_1(x)-12 lambda Btilde_1(alpha x),

  P(x)-Pbar
    =lambda Btilde_2(x)-74 Btilde_2(alpha x),

  C(x)
    =(lambda/3)Btilde_3(x)-(74/(3 alpha))Btilde_3(alpha x),

  Pbar=(74-lambda)/6=2923/240.

Indeed the paper's G_0(v)=v(1-v)(2v-1)/6 equals -B_3(v)/3.
The derivative law B_n'=n B_{n-1} therefore yields

  P'=F+lambda,       C'=P-Pbar,

away from breakpoints. Btilde_2 and Btilde_3 have matching endpoint values,
so the primitives needed in the integration-by-parts argument are
continuous and no omitted jump term is introduced. Both are periodic
with common period 40. P has mean Pbar and C has mean zero.

This is a precise instance of Bernoulli/Euler--Maclaurin machinery:
center a sawtooth, integrate it by periodic Bernoulli polynomials, and
bound the remaining integral. It makes P and C predictable rather than
ad hoc. It does not identify them with tau: tau is a linear functional
on polynomials/rational functions, whereas P and C are functions of the
prime-sum scaling variable.

10. GLOBAL NORMALIZATION AND THE PRODUCT-FORMULA VIEWPOINT

The local calculations produce explicit integers L_p with

  v_p^G(F_K)>=L_p.

Define m=product_p p^{-L_p}; the finite support is as in (5.2). Then

  v_p^G(m F_K)>=0 for every p,

so Q=mF_K has integer coefficients. Positive L_p represent divisibility
that can be divided out; negative L_p represent denominators to clear.
The signs cannot be discarded. The exact logarithmic normalization cost
is

  log m = -sum_p L_p log p.

This is a local-to-global determinant normalization. It has the same
structure as product-formula bookkeeping: rational scalar multiplication
transfers local p-adic valuation to real size. However L_p are certified
lower bounds, not asserted exact determinant valuations, so this is not
an exact computation of the determinant's arithmetic size or content.

The final irrationality contradiction can itself be phrased by the
rational product formula, but needs no machinery: for a/b rational,

  Z=b^h Q(a/b)

is an integer. Positivity makes Z nonzero, so |Z|>=1. Equivalently all
finite absolute values of this integer are <=1, and the product formula
forces its real absolute value to be >=1. The paper's real decay aims to
contradict this. At the irrational number zeta(5), Q(zeta(5)) is not a
rational number to which one may directly apply that product formula.

11. WHAT THIS REORGANIZATION DOES AND DOES NOT ELIMINATE

It reduces the list of seemingly unrelated objects to four layers:

  (i) one differentiated Bernoulli functional and its singular extension;
  (ii) one rational moment pairing, studied on useful local lattices;
  (iii) one determinant pairing, estimated by weights and exterior powers;
  (iv) signed prime-sum bookkeeping, with Bernoulli tail corrections.

The exact formulas support those identifications. It remains necessary to
prove: analytic completion/integrality; low-degree restrictions; all-class
comparisons for inner weights; divided-difference integrality for outer
classes; the polynomial correction rank; uniform errors in the limiting
profiles; and quantitative prime-sum/tail estimates. None follows merely
from saying "Volkenborn", "CRT", "lattice", or "determinant line".

ADDENDUM: FINITE PRODUCTS, GAMMA, NEWTON INTERPOLATION, AND TWO PLACES

The product data itself also has a common classical interpretation. For
every nonnegative integer m,

  D_m(-x^2)/(m!)^2
    = product_{j=1}^m (1-x^2/j^2)
    = binom(m-x,m) binom(m+x,m).

Here generalized binomial coefficients are polynomial expressions, so
the identity is valid for every complex x without exceptions caused by
gamma-function poles. Euler's sine product then gives, for fixed x,

  lim_{m->infinity} D_m(-x^2)/(m!)^2=sin(pi x)/(pi x),

with the value 1 at x=0. This finite sine product is simultaneously the
integer-valued binomial factor used at small primes. The fixed-x limit
must not be substituted unchanged into the determinant's scaling regime
x of order K; that is a different asymptotic regime.

For real y, the gamma recurrence gives the exact identity

  D_m(y^2)
    = Gamma(m+1+iy) Gamma(m+1-iy)
      /[Gamma(1+iy) Gamma(1-iy)].

Equivalently D_m(-x^2)=(1-x)_m(1+x)_m in rising-factorial notation.
The normalized q_i are Newton interpolation polynomials on the ordered
nodes 0,-1^2,-2^2,...:

  q_i(t)=(-1)^i 2/(2i)! product_{j=0}^{i-1}(t+j^2), i>=1.

Their pullbacks are symmetric central products

  q_i(-x^2)=2x^2 product_{j=1}^{i-1}(x^2-j^2)/(2i)!.

Thus "central-factorial/Newton type" is a useful descriptive term. No
orthogonality or identification with a special named family is needed.
The displayed node products and binomial formula prove exactly the
interpolation and integrality properties that are used.

The real logarithmic field is the continuum profile of these same finite
products. There is a particularly clean error estimate, which avoids
any difficulty at t=0. For m>=1 define

  E_m(t)=sum_{j=1}^m log(t+(j/K)^2)
           -K integral_0^{m/K}log(t+u^2)du,   t>=0.

The integrable logarithmic singularity at u=0 is allowed when t=0.
Right Riemann sums give E_m(t)>=0, because log(t+u^2) is increasing in u.
For t>0,

  E_m'(t)=sum_{j=1}^m 1/(t+(j/K)^2)
           -K integral_0^{m/K}1/(t+u^2)du <=0,

because the new integrand is decreasing. Continuity as t decreases to 0
therefore yields the uniform bound

  0<=E_m(t)<=E_m(0)
    =2log(m!)-2m log m+2m=O(log(m+1)).

Consequently, for m=aK an integer with a>0 fixed,

  log D_m(K^2t)
    =2m log K+K integral_0^a log(t+u^2)du+O(log K),

uniformly for every t>=0. This proof uses only product identities,
monotonicity and the elementary factorial Stirling estimate. Applying
large-argument Stirling separately to Gamma(1+-iK sqrt(t)) would require
care near t=0, where those arguments need not become large; the direct
sum proof has already handled that endpoint uniformly.

For D_N^6/D_K, this produces the field contribution

  integral_0^1 log(t+u^2)du -6 integral_0^alpha log(t+u^2)du.

The exponential decay of the positive weight contributes 2pi sqrt(t),
giving the V(t) in (6.1). On the arithmetic side, the same factorial and
finite-product data are evaluated by v_p: Legendre's exact formula

  v_p(m!)=sum_{r>=1}floor(m/p^r)

produces the scalar floor profile N(x), while counting poles in residue
classes produces Gamma(x). Thus real logarithmic integrals and arithmetic
floor profiles are two different evaluations of the same underlying
product data. They are not equal functions and do not have identical
error analyses.
