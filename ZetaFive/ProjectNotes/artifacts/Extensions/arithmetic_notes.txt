14 November 2026. Independent derivation; not a proof of irrationality.

Scope of the main computation: retain K=40n, N=3n, h=37n,
alpha=3/40, lambda=37/40, the numerator D_N^6, and the scalar S_K
from (2.5). Replace the functional by its honest zeta(7) analogue.
This is an audit of a specific transferred certificate. A positive final
UPPER bound does not prove growth of the actual polynomial values.

1. THE FUNCTIONAL AND SMALL EXCEPTIONS

Use tau_7(P)=L(P^(5))/720 and
tau_{7,X}(1/(x-r))=H_{d(r)}^(7)-X.
Then mu_{7,X}(R)=tau_{7,X}(x^7 R(-x^2)).
The polynomial moments are
mu_7(t^e)=(-1)^e B_{2e+2} product_{j=3}^7(2e+j)/720.
The pole values are
mu_{7,X}(1/(t+j^2))=j^6(X-H_j^(7))-1/6+1/(2j).

For large primes p>=11, the denominator 720 is a unit. The analytic
coefficients kappa_d=d(d-1)...(d-4) B_{d-5}/720 have valuation >=-1.
They are integral at least for d<=p+3: in that range the nonzero even
Bernoulli index is below p-1. In particular the original sufficient
degree bound d<=p+1 still works.

For a far pole s, vp(s)<0, direct expansion gives
tau_7^an(1/(z-s))=-(1/6) sum_{k>=0} binom(k+5,5) B_k s^(-k-6).
Define C_{p,7}=sum_{a=1}^{p-1} tau_7^an(1/(x+a/p))
and Y=p^7 X+C_{p,7}. For p>=11, C_{p,7} lies in p^7 Z_p.
The k=0 term is proportional to p^6 sum a^-6, whose residue sum
vanishes because p-1 does not divide 6. The k=1 term has at least
seven powers of p; k>=2 has at least k+5>=7 powers.
At p=7 the k=0 sum does NOT vanish (6 divides p-1); do not blindly
copy the original assertion with the threshold p>=7. This causes no
asymptotic problem because fixed small primes use the coarse bound.

Bernoulli multiplication after FIVE differentiations gives
tau_{7,X}(g)=p^-6 sum_{a=0}^{p-1} tau_{7,Y}^ext(g(a+px)).
The original (3.7) has p^-4. This change is essential, not notation.
Reflection is still odd and the translation difference is the sixth
Taylor coefficient. The same pole induction establishes distribution.

2. THE SMALL-PRIME ESTIMATE (SOURCE (3.8)--(3.12), PP. 8--9)

The integer-valued polynomial argument itself is unchanged. Five
derivatives cost 5 floor(log_p(d+1)), and the Bernoulli functional L
costs one further such factor. Thus the polynomial analogue of (3.9)
has -6 floor(log_p(d+1))-vp(720), in place of -4...-vp(24).
The source's subtraction of principal parts costs at most two more
logarithmic factors. Alternatively each pole term costs at most one
from its residue and seven from H^(7). Therefore the analogue of
Lemma 3.3 is
v_p^G(tau_{7,X}(g)) >= -8 floor(log_p max(2K,d+1))-vp(720).

The same basis f_i=(D_N/(N!)^2)^3 q_i gives the SAME scalar S_K.
Its pullback numerator now has degree <=12N+4h+3, rather than
12N+4h+1. For the large K in the paper this is still <5K-1.
Consequently
v_p^G(F_{7,K}) >= -8h floor(log_p(5K))-h vp(720).
For p<=K/M, the K^2-scale cost is 8 lambda/M instead of
6 lambda/M. Fixed exceptional primes and higher prime powers
contribute o(K^2), exactly as in Proposition 5.2.

3. THE INNER RANGE (SOURCE (4.1)--(4.8), PP. 9--11)

Keep the original class dimensions L_a, reserve L_0=4M+10,
b_a=3 ell_N(a), and the original T,E allocation and unimodular basis.
The ordinary class valuation changes from
nu_i+nu_j+6 ell_N-ell_K-4
to
nu_i+nu_j+6 ell_N-ell_K-6.
At zero the valuation remains
2nu_i(0)+2nu_j(0)+12m_N-2m_K+1,
because x^7 supplies two additional powers of p and distribution
loses the same additional two powers.

Thus for a>0 use
w7_{a,i}=i+b_a-(ell_K(a)+6)/2=w5_{a,i}-1.
For the zero block replace only the ordinary thresholds in (4.7):
w7_{0,i}=min(2i+6m_N-m_K+1/2,
             min_c[Z_c-(ell_K(c)+6)/2]).
The same cross-class comparison works: subtracting one from all
ordinary half-weights leaves their comparisons identical. The zero
source remains more than adequate. The leading numerator degree
at an ordinary source is unchanged. At zero it is greater by two,
bounded by 7+4L_0+12m_N <=16.9 M+47, still below p+1 because
p>200M. Thus the original completion argument applies to the
entire functional, including polynomial quotients.

There are h-L_0 ordinary rows and only O_M(1) zero rows. Therefore
gamma7_in=gamma5_in-2h+O_M(1).
Since h=lambda K, the limiting functions satisfy EXACTLY
Gamma7(x)=Gamma5(x)-2lambda x,
R7(x)=R5(x)+2lambda x.
The added cost over K/M<p<=K/3 is
2lambda(1/3-1/M).
In particular every existing finite-interval and tail estimate for R5
can be reused by integrating this explicit extra linear term. No
new numerical tail assertion is being assumed.

4. THE OUTER POLYNOMIAL CORRECTION IMPROVES (SOURCE P. 11)

The zeta(7) moment is integral for e<3p-4, whereas the zeta(5)
threshold is e<2p-3. Indeed if p-1 divides 2e+2 below 6(p-1),
write 2e+2=m(p-1), 1<=m<=5. Among 2e+3,...,2e+7 one
factor is divisible by p. It cancels the Bernoulli denominator.

Keep W=D_N^5 and D_tail=D_K/D_N. The polynomial quotient of
W t^(i+j)/D_tail has degree <=i+j+6N-K. Thus the nonintegral
correction L has entries zero when
i+j<K-6N+3p-4.
With h=K-N, this makes the first h-r7 rows/columns vanish, where
r7=max(0,K+4N-3p+3).
The original rank is r5=max(0,K+4N-2p+2).
This is a real improvement; the arithmetic changes are not uniformly
bad. The calculation below includes this improvement.

5. OUTER POLE CLASSES AND THE NEW TABLE (SOURCE (4.11)--(4.14))

Keep the original unimodular basis. Let ell=ell_K(a) and
delta=1 if a<=N, else zero. For low indices i,j<ell-2, the
residue valuation is >=i+j+6delta-ell-6. The harmonic term now
loses seven powers of p instead of five. Polynomial parts after
the correction are integral. Accordingly use
w7_{a,i}=min(0,i+3delta-(ell+6)/2), i<ell-2;
w7_{a,i}=0, i>=ell-2.

The high-index cancellation still works. The remaining pole values
at a and p-a are congruent modulo p, because H^(7) has the same
odd-exponent reflection identity as H^(5). Their coefficients of X
are a^6 and (p-a)^6, also congruent. The divided difference is
therefore integral. This step is not being discarded.

Removed classes (delta=1):
ell                         2  3  4  5  6
new cost -2 sum_i w7         0  3  6  9 12
new number of zero weights  1  1  1  1  2
old cost                    0  1  2  4  6
old number of zero weights  1  1  2  2  3

Unremoved classes cost 9(ell-2) and have two zero weights;
the old cost was 7(ell-2). Zero-class costs remain 1 (one pole)
and 4 (two poles), with respectively zero and one zero weights.

With v=K-p floor(K/p), u=max(0,N+v-p+1), t_p=min(N,v)+u,
the resulting determinant certificate is
gamma7_out =
  -9(K-p)+6t_p-1-min(r7,p-1-N),                    K<2p;
  -9(K-p)+5+12N+6t_p-min(r7,p-N+u),               K>=2p.
These formulas follow by summing the table and using the SAME
rank lemma 4.2. For p>K the determinant is again integral.

6. EXACT OUTER INTEGRAL AND FINAL COMPARISON

Put y=p/K. At alpha=3/40, the new rank r7/K=(1+4alpha-3y)_+
never exceeds the limiting number of zero weights, so the minimum
does not cause a further correction. Specifically on y in (1/3,1/2)
that number/K is y-alpha+(1+alpha-3y)_+; on (1/2,1) the rank
is already zero.

The new raw outer determinant cost is therefore
C7(y)=9(1-y)-12alpha-6 min(alpha,1-2y)
      -6(1+alpha-3y)_+ +(1+4alpha-3y)_+, 1/3<y<1/2;
C7(y)=9(1-y)-6 min(alpha,1-y)-6(1+alpha-2y)_+, 1/2<y<1;
C7(y)=0, y>1.

The old cost is C5=R0-d in (5.8)--(5.9). Exact rational integration
of piecewise affine functions (see arithmetic_costs.py) gives
integral C5 = 23329/19200,
integral C7 = 1261/800,
extra outer cost = 1387/3840 = 0.3611979166666667.
Because S_K is unchanged, its cost cancels in this comparison.
Thus Iout7=Iout5+1387/3840=27071/16000.

Adding the inner increment and the small-prime increment gives
1387/3840 + 2lambda(1/3-1/M) + 2lambda/M
 = 751/768 = 0.9778645833333333.

If A_M denotes the source's rigorous normalization upper bound
(5.20), then this transferred proof gives the valid bound
A7_M = A_M + 751/768.
The real determinant retains the original leading coefficient U
when D_N^6 and S_K are fixed (root agent separately audits this).
The last comparison becomes A_M+U+751/768, which is positive
at M=200 and remains positive as M tends to infinity. Thus the
source's conclusion of exponential decay is no longer obtained.

Precise logical conclusion: this explicit certificate does not prove
irrationality. It does not establish a lower bound on determinant
values, does not rule out better arithmetic estimates, and does not
rule out changing the numerator or optimizing the parameters.
