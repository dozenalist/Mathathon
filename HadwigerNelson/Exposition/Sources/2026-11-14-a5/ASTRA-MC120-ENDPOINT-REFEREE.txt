2. ANNULAR LOCALIZATION AND ALL ANGULAR LIFTS

Let S_+=Straddle(K_+), S_-=Straddle(K_-), and W=S_+ intersect S_-.
For z in either Straddle set, a close witness p and a far witness q give
  |z| <= |z-p|+|p| < 1+rho,
  |z| >= |z-q|-|q| > 1-rho.
Thus every z in W has 99/100<r=|z|<101/100. In particular r>0, so all
subsequent polar coordinates and divisions by r are legitimate.

For r>=1, a close witness on each arc must have parameter t_+,t_->0:
the zero parameter would have distance r>=1. Expanding squared distances,
  cos(theta-alpha(t_+)) > (r^2-1+t_+^2)/(2rt_+) >= 0,
  cos(theta+alpha(t_-)) > (r^2-1+t_-^2)/(2rt_-) >= 0.
Here z=r exp(i theta), theta in (-pi,pi].

The plus-centred interval where the corresponding cosine is positive is
  (alpha(t_+)-pi/2, alpha(t_+)+pi/2),
and the minus-centred one is
  (-alpha(t_-)-pi/2, -alpha(t_-)+pi/2).
Both full intervals lie inside (-pi,pi), because
  alpha(t)+pi/2 <= 5pi/6+1/50 < pi.
All their nonzero 2pi translates are disjoint from (-pi,pi]. Thus the
ordinary lifted inequalities are valid without a hidden wrap choice:
  -pi/6+2t_+ < theta < pi/6-2t_-.
The lower bound uses the PLUS witness and the upper bound uses the MINUS
witness; the two parameters need not be equal.

For r<=1, write z=-r exp(i beta), beta in (-pi,pi]. A far witness on each
arc again has strictly positive parameter. Squared-distance expansion gives
  cos(beta-alpha(t_+)) > (1-r^2-t_+^2)/(2rt_+) >= -t_+/(2r),
  cos(beta+alpha(t_-)) > (1-r^2-t_-^2)/(2rt_-) >= -t_-/(2r).
Put delta(t)=arcsin(t/(2r)). The annular bound gives
  0<t/(2r)<1/198<1/2.
On [0,1/2], the derivative of arcsin is at most 2/sqrt(3)<2. Therefore
  0<delta(t)<=t/r<(100/99)t<2t.
The strict cosine inequalities imply membership in intervals of angular
radius pi/2+delta(t), since cos(pi/2+arcsin u)=-u. These entire intervals
also lie inside (-pi,pi):
  alpha(t)+pi/2+delta(t)<5pi/6+4t<=5pi/6+1/25<pi.
Their translates cannot contribute another component in the principal
interval. Taking their intersection yields
  -pi/6+2t_+-delta(t_+) < beta < pi/6-2t_-+delta(t_-).
Since each 2t-delta(t)>0, this implies |beta|<pi/6.

All wrapping, positivity, and endpoint restrictions in these deductions
are justified. In particular, neither argument silently discards r=1.

3. OUTER DISK, INNER DISK, AND THE UNIT-CIRCLE BOUNDARY

Set a=sqrt(3)/2 and D_+=B(a,1/2), D_-=B(-a,1/2), with real centres.

Outer case, r>=1. Let h=r-1. The close witnesses satisfy r<1+t_+ and
r<1+t_-, by the triangle inequality. Thus
  0<=h<rho, t_+,t_->h, and |theta|<pi/6-2h.
The last angular upper bound is positive: pi/6-2h>pi/6-1/50>0.
For 0<=u<=s<=1/50, the sine Lipschitz bound gives
  sin(pi/6-u)>=1/2-u>=12/25>1/3.
Integrating from 0 to s gives
  cos(pi/6-s)>=sqrt(3)/2+s/3.
Cosine is strictly decreasing in the relevant positive-angle interval,
so the STRICT angular bound implies
  cos(theta)>sqrt(3)/2+2h/3.
This is strict even when h=0.

The open-disk condition for D_+ is
  |z-a|^2-1/4=r^2-sqrt(3)r cos(theta)+1/2<0.
Using the strict cosine estimate, its left side is strictly less than
  h(1/2+h)-(2sqrt(3)/3)(1+h)h
    <= h(1/2+h)-(1+h)h=-h/2<=0.
The comparison uses h>=0 and 2sqrt(3)/3>1. Therefore z belongs to the
OPEN disk D_+, including in the temporary r=1 case. Also Re z>0.

Inner case, r<=1. The bound |beta|<pi/6 gives cos(beta)>sqrt(3)/2.
Consequently

because 99/100<r<=1. Thus z belongs to the OPEN disk D_- and Re z<0.

At r=1 both deductions apply to the SAME z and force Re z>0 and Re z<0.
Hence W has no unit-circle points. This excludes boundary leakage exactly,
not almost everywhere. It proves

  W intersect {r<1} subset D_-,

7. INDEPENDENT CHECK OF THE EXACT STRAIGHT-NEEDLE CONTRAST

Let K_+=[0,L_+ exp(i pi/3)] and K_-=[0,L_- exp(-i pi/3)] with L_+,L_->0.
Choose 0<t<=min(L_+,L_-,1/10), and define
  d=t^2/(2(t+sqrt(3))), a=sqrt(3)/2-d,
  z_+=(-a,1/2), z_-=(-a,-1/2).

One has 0<d<t/2<=1/20<sqrt(3), so
  |z_+|^2=|z_-|^2=1-sqrt(3)d+d^2<1.
The origin is therefore a close witness for both points and both segments.
Their mutual distance is exactly 1, from the unit vertical displacement.

The same-sign endpoint distance (z_+ to t exp(i pi/3), or z_- to
t exp(-i pi/3)) has squared value minus 1 equal to
  t^2-(t+sqrt(3))d+d^2=t^2/2+d^2>0.
The opposite-sign endpoint distance exceeds this by sqrt(3)t>0.
Hence EVERY one of the four endpoint distances is strictly greater than 1.
All chosen endpoints are in the segments by the bound on t. Both z_+,z_-
belong to their common Straddle intersection and form the asserted unit pair.

The optional asymptotic comment is also consistent: d is of order t^2,
the radial deficit is of order t^2, and decreasing the horizontal magnitude
