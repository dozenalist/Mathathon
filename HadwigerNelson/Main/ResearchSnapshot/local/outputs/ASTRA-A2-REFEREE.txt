1. THE THREE-CENTRE ARGUMENT

The original States:704-711 assumes measurable weak c:C->Fin k; a centre x;
finite colour sets J,K and a colour X with i notin J union K iff i=X;
RealState hc x A J and RealState hc x B K; positive Lebesgue measure of A;
and b,d in supp(volume restricted to B). It concludes that
norm(direction(b)-direction(d)) is not one. There is no periodicity,
FourCenter, FiveCenter, palette-size, disjointness-of-J-and-K, or
finite-measure-of-A hypothesis. The proposed proof needs none of these.

Suppose the forbidden chord exists and put y=x+direction(b),
z=x+direction(d). The three distances xy,xz,yz are all one.
BoundaryTrace.state_support_avoiding_trace:24-52 gives actual K-avoiding
traces at y and z. BoundaryTrace.avoiding_trace_palette:53-72 gives
P_y(t) notsubset K and P_z(t) notsubset K almost everywhere.

FiveSupport.palette_neighbor_disjoint:23-31 applies to ANY fixed unit
pair of centres and compares palettes at the SAME angular parameter.
For yz choose an angle with direction equal to z-y, which is possible
because its norm is one. Symmetry of disjointness handles its orientation.
Thus P_x,P_y,P_z are pairwise disjoint for almost every common parameter.
I checked the source proof: its first endpoint is the identity and its
second endpoint is constant; its only parallel directions form a null set.
There is no hidden relation between the chosen parameter and b or d.

Pull these five conull statements to R using
angleMk_quasiMeasurePreserving. RealState, defined at States:92-93,
means that A is measurable and, for each j in J, almost every real
t in A belongs to liftPalette x j. Since J is finite, one additional
conull statement says t in A implies J subset P_x(t).

A has positive measure, so it intersects this common conull set, even
if its measure is infinite or it is nonperiodic. At such a t, select
i in P_y(t) outside K. Since P_y(t) is disjoint from P_x(t), which
contains J, i is outside J as well. The complement identity forces i=X.
The identical argument places X in P_z(t), contrary to disjointness.

This proves exactly the old fixed-state conclusion. Every chosen centre,
trace and chord direction is fixed before the exceptional sets are
combined. Only two trace conditions, three pair conditions and finitely
many J-membership conditions occur. No uncountable exceptional-set union,
simultaneous trace realization, or angular-periodicity assumption occurs.
The support membership of B supplies the avoiding traces directly;
there is no need to add positivity of B as a separate premise.

2. Z_K IS GENUINELY CLOSED IN THE ACTUAL TRACE TOPOLOGY

For fixed c,hc,K define
  Z_K={y: there is beta with IsTrace c hc y beta and
                    traceValue beta i=0 for every i in K}.
This is one global subset of the plane. Its definition does not involve
a base centre, A, B, a state encoding, or a chosen sequence.

The original Compactness file provides precisely the required topology:
  :159 WeakBall.Space is a subtype of the weak dual unit ball;
  :160 it is compact;
  :161-162 it is metrizable for the separable Hilbert space used here;
  :177-179 every fixed-test inner-product evaluation is continuous;
  :251 TraceVector k is the finite product of those spaces;
  :284-285 the actual trace graph in C x TraceVector k is closed;
  :289-292 trace_limit passes simultaneous centre/trace limits;
  :293-296 traceCode_tendsto_iff identifies convergence with convergence
            against every fixed L2 test function.

Independent proof: take y_n in Z_K with y_n->y, and choose avoiding
traces beta_n. Compact metrizability of TraceVector gives a convergent
subsequence beta_phi(n)->beta. Along it y_phi(n)->y, so trace_limit gives
IsTrace c hc y beta. For i in K, apply traceCode_tendsto_iff to the fixed
test v=traceValue beta i. Every sequence inner product is zero. Its limit
is therefore both zero and the inner product of v with itself, forcing
v=0. Thus beta avoids K and y is in Z_K. Since C is metric, sequential
closedness proves closedness.

The proof of closedness itself requires measurability of c but does not
use WeakColoring. It needs no local compactness or compactness of the
plane. The alternative topological proof is also valid: the zero-coordinate
conditions are intersections of kernels of continuous weak evaluations;
intersect them with the closed trace graph; project along the COMPACT
TraceVector factor. Projection from X x C to X is a closed map whenever
C is compact, without an extra local-compactness assumption on X.

Implementation caution: traceValue is NOT asserted continuous into
norm-topologized L2. Weak convergence need not be norm convergence, as
an orthonormal sequence shows. The tempting proof using the preimage of
the norm-closed singleton under a supposedly norm-continuous traceValue
would be invalid. The fixed-test argument above avoids this issue and
is already the exact pattern used at BoundaryTrace:49-52.

the B_n are represented K-states at x_n, and supported angles
b_n->b,d_n->d. Then x_n+direction(b_n) and x_n+direction(d_n) lie in Z_K

6. RESIDUAL FINITE-GRAPH STATEMENT

ACCEPT. Let L be the complement of J union K. For finitely many vertices
y_v in Z_K at distance one from x, choose avoiding traces and apply the
neighbour-palette theorem to x--y_v and every unit-distance edge y_v--y_w.
Finiteness permits one conull intersection. Choose a real parameter in
the positive J-state satisfying it. Each P_yv then has an element in L;
choose one as the vertex colour. Palette disjointness across edges makes
this a proper L-colouring. The proof requires neither palette-size bounds
nor simultaneous realization of the chosen traces.

For one residual label this excludes a unit edge. For two residual labels
the conclusion gives only bipartiteness, which already follows from
geometry on a SINGLE unit circle: a unit chord joins angles differing by
plus or minus pi/3, so each connected component lies in one six-point
rotation orbit and is a subgraph of C6. Repeated points in an indexed
family do not change bipartiteness. No measurable global choice of an
orbit representative is asserted or required for this finite statement.
Thus the claimed limitation at two residual colours is correct; it says
nothing against possible stronger constraints coupling several circles.
