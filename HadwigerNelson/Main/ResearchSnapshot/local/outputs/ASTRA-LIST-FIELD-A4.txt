1. EXACT DOMAIN AND EXPLICIT MAP

Translate the original centre to 0. Write e(t)=exp(it), and let
  S_m=((m-1/2)pi/3,(m+1/2)pi/3), modulo 2pi.
Let G consist of 0<|z|<2, with the six circles |z-e(pi/6+k*pi/3)|=1
removed. For z in G its unordered representation z=e(a)+e(b), with
the smaller angular gap in (0,pi), is unique, and both directions are
interior to sectors. Set
  L(z)={0,1,2,3,4,5}\(P(a) union P(b)).
Let T={z in G: |L(z)|=2}, and R_j={z in G: L(z)=A_j}.

Define the following OPEN sets (the plus sign denotes Minkowski sum):
  V=e((-pi/2,-pi/6)) + e((pi/6,pi/2)),
  H=e((pi/6,pi/2)) + e((pi/2,5pi/6)).
Then, exactly,
  R_0=V union (-V) union H union (-H),
  R_j=e(j*pi/3) R_0, j=1,2,
  T=R_0 disjoint-union R_1 disjoint-union R_2.

Indeed, the endpoints must use A1 and A2 to leave A0. The two sectors
for A1 are S1,S4 and those for A2 are S2,S5. The four combinations
give H,-V,V,-H respectively. Every displayed sum uses nonparallel
interior directions. Uniqueness of the unordered representation shows
that none lies on one of the removed boundary circles. Rotation shifts
all sector indices and therefore shifts the residual palette index.

On R0 define a bit b(z) by the following disjoint rules:
  z in V:   b(z)=0 if |z|<=1, and b(z)=1 if |z|>1;
  z in -V:  b(z)=1 if |z|<=1, and b(z)=0 if |z|>1;
  z in H or -H: b(z)=0 if Re(z)>=0, and b(z)=1 if Re(z)<0.
Give bit 0 label 1 and bit 1 label 4.
For z in R_j first rotate to w=e(-j*pi/3)z and use b(w), with
  (bit 0,bit 1) labels (1,4), (2,5), (0,3) for j=0,1,2.
This is the promised proper L-colouring of T.

The axis and radius-one conventions are arbitrary: those points have
NO unit-distance neighbour within their own R_j, as proved below.
All pieces are defined by continuous inequalities, so the map is Borel.

2. ELEMENTARY REULEAUX-TRIANGLE FACT

If A,B,C are the vertices of a unit equilateral triangle, then
  K=closedBall(A,1) intersect closedBall(B,1) intersect closedBall(C,1)
has diameter 1. If p and q both belong to all three OPEN balls, then
|p-q|<1.

For completeness, its boundary consists of the three minor 60-degree
arcs BC about A, CA about B, and AB about C. Fix q in K and consider
the first arc, taking A=0, B=1, C=e(pi/3). The two inequalities
|q-B|<=1 and |q-C|<=1 give q dot B>=0 and q dot C>=0. If q is nonzero,
its argument therefore lies in [-pi/6,pi/2]. On theta in [0,pi/3],
the function |e(theta)-q| cannot have an interior maximum: such a
maximum would require theta to point directly opposite q, which is
outside this interval. Its maximum is consequently at B or C, where
it is at most 1. The same argument holds for all three arcs. The
maximum of distance from q on the compact convex K is attained on
its boundary, proving the claim. If q is in all three open balls,
the endpoint distances are strictly below 1, so this proof also gives
strict inequality for every p in K. (The q=A special case is immediate.)

3. USEFUL EXACT GEOMETRIC BOUNDS

Put s=sqrt(3)/2, A=(s,1/2), B=(s,-1/2), C=(0,1),
D=(-s,1/2), Q=(s,3/2), E=(0,2).

For p=(x,y) in V:
  0<x<sqrt(3), -1/2<y<1/2, 0<|p|<sqrt(3),
  |arg p|<pi/6,
  |p-A|<1, |p-B|<1, |p-C|>1, |p+C|>1.             (V)

For q=(u,v) in H:
  -s<u<s, 1<v<2,
  |q-C|<1, |q-A|>1, |q-D|>1.                       (H)

Here are direct verifications of the circle inequalities, to avoid
relying on a picture. Write p=e(alpha)+e(beta), with
alpha in (-pi/2,-pi/6), beta in (pi/6,pi/2).
The chord e(beta)-C has direction (beta-pi/2)/2 in (-pi/6,0),
which makes an angle strictly less than pi/2 with e(alpha). Hence
|p-C|>1. Reflection gives |p+C|>1.
For |p-A|, the chord e(beta)-A has length
  d=2 sin((beta-pi/6)/2) in (0,1)
and direction (beta+pi/6)/2+pi/2. Its scalar product with e(alpha)
is -d sin((beta+pi/6)/2-alpha), where the sine is at least 1/2
(its argument lies between pi/3 and 5pi/6). Thus
  |p-A|^2 <= 1+d^2-d <1.
Reflection proves |p-B|<1. The other V bounds follow immediately
from the sum formula and the endpoint intervals.

For H, write q=e(alpha)+e(beta), alpha in (pi/6,pi/2),
beta in (pi/2,5pi/6). The chord e(alpha)-A has direction in
(2pi/3,5pi/6); its scalar product with e(beta) is positive, so
|q-A|>1. Reflection about the imaginary axis gives |q-D|>1.
For |q-C|<1, rotate by -pi/2 and put a=alpha-pi/2 in (-pi/3,0),
b=beta-pi/2 in (0,pi/3). With delta=(b-a)/2 and m=(a+b)/2,
  |e(a)+e(b)-1|^2-1=4 cos(delta)(cos(delta)-cos(m))<0,
because 0<=|m|<delta<pi/3. The remaining H bounds are immediate.

Consequently every p=(x,y) in V lies strictly BELOW
  f(x)=1-sqrt(1-x^2),                  0<x<=s;
  f(x)=-1/2+sqrt(1-(x-s)^2),           s<=x<sqrt(3).
For x<=s this follows from |p-C|>1 and y<1/2; for x>=s it follows
from |p-B|<1. The two formulas agree at x=s.
Every q=(u,v) in H with 0<=u<s lies strictly ABOVE
  g(u)=1/2+sqrt(1-(u-s)^2),
by |q-A|>1 and v>1. Also, by concavity of this arc above its chord,
  v>1+u/sqrt(3).                                      (W)

4. THE INDIVIDUAL COLOUR PIECES HAVE NO UNIT PAIRS

4a. V inside the closed unit disk.
If p,q in V have radii r,t<=1, their angular separation is less
than pi/3. Since r,t>0,
  |p-q|^2 < r^2+t^2-r*t <=1.
The last inequality follows by maximizing this convex quadratic on
the unit square: the four corner values are 0,1,1,1.

4b. V outside, INCLUDING its radius-one arc.
For p in V with r=|p|>=1, the angular bound gives x>s*r. Hence
  |p-(sqrt(3),0)|^2 < r^2+3-3*r <=1,
because 1<=r<sqrt(3)<2 and (r-1)(r-2)<=0.
Together with (V), this puts p in all three open unit balls centred
at A, B, and (sqrt(3),0), a unit equilateral triangle. Section 2
shows that this entire piece has all pairwise distances strictly <1.

4c. Each half of H, INCLUDING the imaginary axis.
Take q=(u,v) in H with u>=0. It lies in the open unit ball at C.
It also lies in the open unit ball at Q, because
  |q-Q|^2=(u-s)^2+(v-3/2)^2 < 3/4+1/4=1.
Finally it lies in the open unit ball at E. If v>=3/2, then
  |q-E|^2=|q-C|^2+3-2v <1.
If 1<v<=3/2, (W) yields
  |q-E|^2 < 3(v-1)^2+(v-2)^2
            =1+2(2v-3)(v-1) <=1.
The centres C,Q,E form a unit equilateral triangle. Section 2 proves
all distances in H intersect {Re>=0} are <1. Reflection proves the
same statement for H intersect {Re<=0}. Negation handles -H.

5. THE GLOBAL V--H SEPARATION: ALL DISTANCES ARE STRICTLY >1

This is the missing global check in the earlier tentative argument.
Let p=(x,y) in V and q=(u,v) in H.

If u<=0 then x>0 and v>1 give
  |p-q|^2 >= x^2+(v-y)^2 > x^2+(1-y)^2 = |p-C|^2 >1.

Suppose u>0. Replace p by p0=(x,f(x)) and q by q0=(u,g(u)).
Their horizontal difference stays fixed, whereas the positive vertical
gap STRICTLY decreases, since y<f(x)<=1/2 and v>g(u)>=1. Therefore
  |p-q|^2 > |p0-q0|^2.
Write q0=A+e(beta), beta in [pi/2,5pi/6]. There are two cases.

Case I: 0<x<=s. Write p0=C+e(gamma), gamma in [-pi/2,-pi/6].
After rotating the difference q0-p0 by pi/6 it is
  1+e(D0)-e(E0),
where D0=beta+pi/6 in [2pi/3,pi] and
E0=gamma+pi/6 in [-pi/3,0]. Exact expansion gives
  |1+e(D0)-e(E0)|^2-1
    =-8 sin((D0-E0)/2) cos(D0/2) sin(E0/2) >=0.
Each factor has the indicated fixed sign on these closed intervals.

Case II: s<=x<sqrt(3). Write p0=B+e(alpha), alpha in [pi/6,pi/2].
Then q0-p0=i+e(beta)-e(alpha), and exact expansion gives
  |i+e(beta)-e(alpha)|^2-1
    =8 sin((beta-alpha)/2)
       * sin((pi/2-alpha)/2) * cos((beta-pi/2)/2) >=0.

Both cases give |p0-q0|>=1, so the original strict vertical gap
gives |p-q|>1. Reflection in the real axis preserves V and carries
H to -H. Negation then proves all four separations
  dist(point of +/-V, point of +/-H)>1.
This is a pointwise statement about every pair, not only corners or
first-order tangent cones.

6. ALL REMAINING SAME-COLOUR PAIRS

H and -H have imaginary coordinates respectively >1 and <-1,
so their mutual distances are >2.

For the assigned common bit on V intersect {|z|<=1} and
(-V) intersect {|z|>1}, write the two points p and -q, p,q in V,
with |q|>1. Their angular separation is <pi/3, so p dot q>0 and
  |p-(-q)|^2=|p+q|^2>|q|^2>1.
The other common-bit pair V outside and -V inside is identical.

Sections 4-6 cover every pair of points assigned the same label in R0:
within a piece the distance is <1; between the relevant distinct pieces
it is >1. Thus the bit map is a proper 2-colouring of its unit graph.
Rotating proves the same for R1,R2. Since A0,A1,A2 are disjoint,
edges between different R_j cannot be monochromatic. This completes
the proof of the map in Section 1 on ALL of T.

For the advertised harmless boundary conventions: a point of V on
|z|=1 has distance <1 to every point of V, using 4a or 4b; it has
distance >1 to every point of -V by the positive-dot-product argument;
and distance >1 to +/-H by Section 5. Hence it has no unit neighbour
in R0. A point on the imaginary axis in H belongs to both independent
halves in 4c, and the remaining components are >1 away. The other
claims follow by symmetry.

The graph is not edgeless. Explicit examples are the pairs
  1/2 and -1/2; 1/2 and 3/2; 1/2+3i/2 and -1/2+3i/2.
They lie in the asserted open pieces and are unit pairs of opposite bits.

7. OPTIONAL FLIPS AND THE EXACT OBSTRUCTION BOUNDARY

One may independently interchange the two labels on V union (-V),
on H, and on -H. These sets have no unit edges between them.
Doing this independently after the three rotations gives a concrete
family of 2^9 proper list maps on T. This is a SUFFICIENT family;
there is no completeness assertion or licensed reduction of the full
four-list problem to 512 cases.

For any finite F contained in T, restricting any displayed map to F
is an explicit proper L-colouring. Therefore every finite obstruction
to the full one-centre list field must include at least one four-list
vertex. Unit triangles, odd cycles, or more complicated configurations
lying entirely in the two-list locus cannot work. In particular the
pure-two-list search suggested in Section 5 of Claude's report has a
negative answer, without interval arithmetic or a finite mesh.
