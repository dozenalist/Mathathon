1. EXACT LOCALIZED STATEMENT THAT THE SOURCE DOES PROVE

Use normalized angular Haar measure mu, tau=pi/3, gamma=arccos(5/6).
Let c:C-plane->Fin k be measurable and WeakColoring k c. Fix ONE centre x,
fixed label sets J,K with |Fin k minus (J union K)|<=2, and measurable
angular sets B0,B1. Write their real lifts as tilde(B0), tilde(B1).
Assume
  RealState hc x tilde(B0) J,
  RealState hc x tilde(B1) K,
  1_B0(t+tau)=1_B1(t), 1_B1(t+tau)=1_B0(t), for mu-a.e. t.
For the intended partial-state interpretation B0,B1 are disjoint and
A=B0 union B1 has positive measure. Disjointness and positivity of A are
not needed for the following null-return conclusion itself.

THEOREM NR:
  mu(B0 intersect (B0-gamma))=0,
  mu(B1 intersect (B1-gamma))=0.
Here B-gamma={t:t+gamma in B}. All centres and label sets stay fixed.

Proof. Suppose E=B_b intersect (B_b-gamma) has positive measure. The
sixty-degree swapping law, iterated finitely many times, gives for almost
every t in E and j=0,1,2:
  a_j=t+2*j*tau is in B_b,
  b_j=t+(2*j+3)*tau+gamma is in B_(1-b).
Apply Lebesgue differentiation to the RESTRICTED state sets B0 and B1,
not merely to fibres of a Boolean function defined outside A. Remove the
finite union of shifted non-density exceptional sets. The positive-measure
set E still contains t for which each a_j,b_j is a density point of its
stated state set. Angular-to-real lifting uses the same quasi-measure-
preserving map as in the source proof.

Set z_j=x+e(a_j)+e(b_j). States.states_densityOne gives density one of the
S-coloured set at EACH z_j, where S=Fin k minus (J union K). The two
RealState inputs are exchanged when b=1. The Jacobian condition is valid:
sin(b_j-a_j)=sin(pi+gamma) is nonzero. The source identity gives
  z_j=x+e(t+2*j*tau)*(1-e(gamma)),
and |1-e(gamma)|=1/sqrt(3). Thus z_0,z_1,z_2 form a unit equilateral
triangle (BinaryPalettes.sourceTriangle_pair). States.two_label_triangle_
impossible contradicts |S|<=2. This proves both null statements.

The genuinely minimal geometric input is even smaller: ONE t with the six
required density-point memberships suffices. Positive measure of E is a
convenient, source-licensed way to choose that t despite a.e. exceptions.
One can also replace the swapping law by a positive-measure set of exactly
those six memberships. Nothing requires A to be the whole circle at this
stage.

For A measurable and tau-invariant modulo null sets, with a Boolean s
flipping by tau on A, use B_b=A intersect {s=b}. NR becomes
  mu{t in A: t+gamma in A and s(t)=s(t+gamma)}=0.
It does NOT state mu(A)=0.

2. PRECISE AUDIT OF THE ORIGINAL WHOLE-CIRCLE STEP

States:243-307 first uses hflip to obtain equality at even tau shifts and
complementation at pi. It uses measurability for a.e. density points and
finitely many fixed translates. Those steps all work on B0,B1 as above.

The only indispensable WHOLE-CIRCLE step is the invocation of
BinaryPalettes.equal_triangleAngle_positive at States:289. If a Boolean
state has no same-phase gamma return on the whole circle, then
  s(t+gamma)=not s(t) a.e. on the WHOLE circle.
Applying this twice yields invariance under 2*gamma. That rotation is
ergodic because gamma/pi is irrational. Hence s is a.e. constant,
contradicting the tau flip.

On a partial A, absence of same-phase returns gives an anti-flip only on
A intersect (A-gamma). No relation is available when one endpoint is
outside A. The proof cannot iterate it through a gap. Poincare recurrence
gives some integer multiple of gamma, not the particular gamma that makes
the displayed unit triangle. Substituting n*gamma changes its side length.

An additional gamma-invariance hypothesis on A would suffice, but is a
substantive new input: an irrational rotation makes a positive invariant
A conull. Similarly a direct positive same-phase gamma-return hypothesis
is sufficient by NR. Neither follows from tau-invariance or a finite
partition into tau-orbit types.

3. AN EVEN STRONGER SOURCE-LICENSED TRIANGLE CONSTRAINT

Let I be finite, f:Angles->I measurable, and L_i fixed label sets. Suppose
every real fibre {t:f(t)=i} is a RealState at the SAME centre x representing
L_i, and f(t+2*tau)=f(t) a.e. Then
  for mu-a.e. t,
  |Fin k minus (L_(f(t)) union L_(f(t+pi+gamma)))| >= 3.              (TC)

Proof. Remove the finitely many fibre-density and shift exceptions. For
such t, the three angles t+2*j*tau have the same state f(t), and the other
three angles t+(2*j+3)*tau+gamma have the same state f(t+pi+gamma).
Use exactly the density-one triangle argument in section 1. A common
remaining list of size at most two cannot colour that unit triangle.

For actual palettes that are 120-degree periodic, this is the concrete
necessary condition
  |P_x(t) union P_x(t+pi+gamma)| <= k-3 a.e.
At six colours the bound is THREE. The fibres of the finite palette map
are measurable and represent their own palette labels, so this is a
legitimate consuming interface. It does not require global two-state
collapse. For partial B0,B1, introduce a third state outside A representing
the empty label set; its 120-degree-periodic state map recovers NR from TC.

TC is a useful constraint for existing whole-circle experiments. It still
does not by itself prove that the four-state model below is unrealizable.

4. EXACT FOUR-STATE COUNTERMODEL TO THE FINITE-PARTITION REDUCTION

Put h=pi/6 and f(t)=floor(t/h) mod 4. This is well-defined and measurable
on the angular circle because 2*pi=12*h and 4 divides 12. Set
  P0={0,2}, P1={0,3}, P2={3,5}, P3={2,5},  P(t)=P_(f(t)).
Use half-open sectors at boundaries; all analytic claims are a.e.

Then f(t+tau)=f(t)+2 mod 4 and f(t+2*tau)=f(t). Thus every individual
sixty-degree orbit alternates exactly ONE complementary pair:
  type A0={t:f(t) even}: 02 <-> 35,
  type A1={t:f(t) odd}:  03 <-> 25.
Both A0,A1 are tau-invariant, have measure 1/2, and together cover the
whole circle. There are nevertheless FOUR palettes globally. The choice
of pair depends on the angular phase. A finite 4^6 enumeration cannot
identify these two orbit types globally without an additional theorem.

This field satisfies the actual geometric SIGN pattern used in the m=6
finite certificate. Set the unit approach vectors to
  (v0,...,v5)=(1,-1,-1,-1,1,1).
At nonorthogonal t with cos(t)>0, positive edges are E0,E4,E5 and negative
edges are E1,E2,E3. Their allowed label sets are respectively {2,3} and
{0,5}; when cos(t)<0 these sets swap. Every P_r contains one label from
each pair. It therefore satisfies both radial-side palette intersections,
noncontainment in every cycle edge, and disjointness at sixty degrees.

The gamma bound is EXACT:
  pi/6 < arccos(5/6) < pi/3,
because cos(pi/6)=sqrt(3)/2>5/6>1/2=cos(pi/3).
Consequently f(t+gamma)-f(t) is either 1 or 2 modulo 4. It is never zero.
Whenever t,t+gamma belong to the same A_i, the increment is 2 and their
Boolean phases are opposite. Thus BOTH types satisfy NR with zero
same-phase gamma-return measure. Positive measure of one orbit type gives
no contradiction.

The model satisfies the stronger TC as well. Adding pi increments f by 2
modulo 4, so f(t+pi+gamma)-f(t) is 3 or 0. Consecutive palettes around
the displayed four-state cycle share one label, and equal palettes have
two labels. Hence |P(t) union P(t+pi+gamma)| is 3 or 2, as TC requires.

This is a countermodel to a NAMED reduction using measurable finite angular
states, orbitwise alternation, strip-side compatibility, and the localized
triangle-return rule. It is not a countermodel to any theorem assuming
that these are actual traces of a global WeakColoring 6 c. The stronger

5. POSITIVITY CANNOT BE REPAIRED BY A UNIFORM MEASURE THRESHOLD

For every epsilon>0 there are purely angular partial-state examples with
mu(A)>1-epsilon and no same-phase gamma returns. Thus no threshold below
one, based just on mu(A), tau-invariance and partial flip, repairs the step.

Choose an odd multiple n=3*(2m+1) such that n*gamma is arbitrarily close
to pi modulo 2*pi. Such choices exist because gamma/pi is irrational.
Write n*gamma=pi+delta mod 2*pi, with |delta|<eta. Let
  s(t)=sign(cos(n*t)) as a Boolean,
  A={t:distance(n*t, pi/2+pi*Z)>eta}.
Then A is tau-invariant, s(t+tau)=not s(t), and
mu(A)=1-2*eta/pi. For t in A, the perturbation delta cannot cross a cosine
zero; therefore s(t+gamma)=not s(t). In particular, there is no same-phase
return with both endpoints in A. Taking eta arbitrarily small gives the
claim. The equality events required by the original whole-circle theorem
are confined to the omitted small bands; no source theorem is contradicted.

This is an angular recurrence countermodel, not asserted RealState
realizability. The full colouring-level statement 'every positive A is
impossible' remains outside what these examples settle.

6. A BETTER FIXED CONSUMER: ALIGNED EXCLUSION CONTINUA

THEOREM AC (paper-level source corollary).
Let c:C-plane->Fin k be measurable and weak, x one fixed centre, v one
fixed unit vector, and K_i a finite family with ApproachingContinuum x K_i v
for EVERY i. Suppose each K_i excludes a specified finite label set H_i:
  Disjoint(spatialSupport c j, Straddle K_i), for j in H_i.
If the union of the H_i leaves at most two possible labels a,b, then False.
The K_i need not coincide. Their approach DIRECTIONS must coincide.
This is not a statement about approximately aligned vectors.

Proof, with every analytic input retained.

(a) Put W=intersection_i Straddle(K_i). This is open. Finite support
exclusion implies c(z) in {a,b} for plane-a.e. z in W. For every unit e
with <e,v> nonzero, the actual ApproachingContinuum strip lemmas give
a one-sided radial strip around x+e contained in W. Choose the OUTER side
when <e,v>>0 and INNER side when <e,v><0. Finiteness lets us take the
minimum of the positive widths. The only exceptional directions are the
two perpendiculars, a two-point angular set B.

(b) One-sided trace production (inner_strip_trace or outer_strip_trace)
puts a or b in P_x(t) on an angular neighbourhood of each t outside B,
almost everywhere there. The locally-a.e. gluing lemma and nullness of B
give BinaryCenter c hc x a b. If a=b, binary_pair_distinct already gives
a contradiction; otherwise continue. Set s(t)=false when a is in P_x(t),
true otherwise. BinaryPalettes.binary_alternation gives
  s(t+tau)=not s(t) a.e.,
and membership of b is equivalent a.e. to nonmembership of a.

(c) If t and t+tau are outside B, W contains the required two one-sided
strips at these equilateral-neighbour directions. The radial sides may be
chosen independently, exactly as permitted by
BoundaryTrace.binary_strip_palette_rigidity:280. Apply it with U=V=W and
the SAME pair {a,b}. It gives one fixed label i in {a,b} belonging to
P_x(u) for a.e. u in an open neighbourhood of t. Binary alternation then
makes s essentially constant on that neighbourhood, whether i=a or i=b.
This is precisely membership in angleEssential s false or true.

(d) Let D=angleBoundary s, the complement of the two essential-constancy
regions. We have D subset B union (B-tau), so D has at most FOUR points.
But angleBoundary_nonempty:381 makes D nonempty, and angleBoundary_six:435
makes it contain a full sixty-degree orbit. Those SIX points are distinct
by sixAngle_injective:425. Contradiction.

The proof works with global WeakColoring only through the source binary
transport/rigidity inputs. It does not assume palette size <=2, triple
exclusion, local finiteness of the cyclic core, or a global two-state
classification. No exceptional sets are unioned over uncountably many
centres. The source local-a.e. gluing lemma handles the angular cover.

SIX-COLOUR CONSUMER.
For a six-cycle of common interfaces at x, if three consecutive edges
have a common unit approach direction, their four endpoint labels are
excluded on the intersection of their straddling sets. Only two labels
remain, so AC rules this out. Arbitrary relabelling and a common rotation
do not affect the argument.

In particular q=(0,3,3,3,0,0) has edges E1={1,2}, E2={2,3}, E3={3,4}
all approaching in direction -1. Their union excludes {1,2,3,4}, leaving
{0,5}. Hence this exact configuration is impossible for ACTUAL common
interfaces. The four-state angular model is still a valid counterexample
to the weaker orbitwise/recurrence reduction, but cannot pass this stronger
source input. At t=0 its {0,5} choice jumps from 5 to 0, whereas the two
same-pair strips at 0 and 60 degrees force essential local constancy there.

AC supplies a specific, checkable new exclusion for the six-colour
common-interface stage. It is not the full F6 theorem: general direction
configurations, including nonaligned vectors, remain. Its novelty relative
to unpublished worker work or the literature has not been investigated.
