PROVENANCE AUDIT: DETERMINANT, RESIDUES, SPECTRAL ARGUMENT, ENERGY, NORMALIZATION

Compared directly with sources/Fauzan_2026_zeta5.txt, the preserved 32-page
manuscript, and zeta5_unified_framework.tex. Page numbers below are printed PDF
page numbers, which agree with the PAGE markers. This is an attribution audit,
not a new audit of the irrationality theorem.

Main finding
------------
Much more of the proposed conceptual account is already explicit in Fauzan
than the phrase 'one Gram pencil' might suggest. His introduction already
connects the affine matrix, positive moment realization, Vandermonde integral,
logarithmic energy, local bases, and relative matrix norm (pp. 2-3, section 1.1).
Our residue language and invariant notation can improve navigation, but they
do not introduce a different mathematical mechanism.

1. The affine positive Gram pencil

Original: section 2.1, pp. 3-4, (2.2)-(2.4), defines mu_X and G_K(X), and says
explicitly that only simple-pole values depend on X, affinely. Section 2.4,
p. 5, Proposition 2.2 and (2.10), realizes G_K(zeta(5)) as a positive Gram
matrix and writes its positive quadratic form. Appendix C.1, p. 29, explicitly
defines M_K=[X]G_K(X) and B_K=G^{-1/2}M_KG^{-1/2}.

Our master pairing B_{K,X}=integral+(X-xi)R_K is a coordinate-free assembly of
these displayed formulas. The Laurent-coefficient expression
[t^{-3}]_infinity R and -Res_infinity(t^2 R dt) is not stated in the original,
but follows immediately from its pole coefficient j^4 in (2.3).

Attribution status: original construction; additional compact notation and
interpretation. Do not describe the pencil or its link to positivity as a new
observation of our note.

2. The residue matrix, exact degree, and finite quotient algebra

Original: section 2.3, p. 4, displays
  [X]G_K(X)=V diag(j^4 D_N(-j^2)^5 / D_tail'(-j^2)) V^T
and then (2.9), the exact nonzero leading coefficient
  (-1)^{h(h-1)/2} product_{N<j<=K} j^4 D_N(-j^2)^5.
Appendix C.1, p. 29, (C.3), uses the same residue expression as a quadratic
form in evaluations at negative squares.

Our A_K=Q[t]/(T_K), epsilon, unit H_K=t^2 W_K, Frobenius-style pairing and
resultant proof are not presented under those names in the source. They are
the standard finite-algebra reformulation of precisely that Vandermonde
factorization. They reproduce the same determinant, with the same sign and
factors. The original does use 'resultants' elsewhere, for unimodularity of
local class factors on p. 12, but not this global resultant formula.

Attribution status: a different elementary packaging/proof of an already
proved identity. The claim that exact degree can be moved to a structural
corollary is an added proof-dependency observation: the basic irrationality
contradiction needs only degree <=h plus positivity. This does not replace
the exact-degree proof when exact degree remains in the theorem statement.

3. Signed-measure Andreief identity for every X

Original: section 6.2, p. 19, (6.10), gives the positive-measure Andreief formula
at X=xi, and cites Forrester. Section 2.3 provides the finite residue sum.

Our signed measure nu_{K,X}=nu_K+(X-xi)sum c_j delta_{-j^2} and the all-X
Andreief identity do not appear explicitly in the manuscript. They follow
by combining those two original formulas with the standard signed-measure
version of Andreief (or merely expanding determinants). Reading positivity
and the leading coefficient as two specializations of this single formula
is a real expository consolidation. It is not a new estimate or construction.

Attribution status: added synthesis, transparently derived from sections 2.3
and 6.2; attribute both and the classical identity.

4. Spectral factorization and roots

Original: Appendix C.1, p. 29, defines the real symmetric B_K and proves its
operator norm bound. Appendix C.2, p. 30, gives
  Q_K(a/b)=Q_K(xi) det(I+delta B_K)
and uses positivity of all eigenvalues when |delta| ||B_K|| is small.
Appendix C.3, p. 31, explicitly writes
  Q_K(X)=Q_K(xi) product_j (1+(X-xi) beta_j)
for the eigenvalues beta_j of B_K. The introduction already explains that
the matrix bound excludes vanishing at nearby rational arguments (p. 3).

Our displayed pencil congruence and eigenvalue product are the same spectral
argument. 'All roots are real' and
  dist(xi, zeros Delta_K)=1/||B_K||
are not stated as corollaries in the source. They are immediate consequences
of its explicit factorization and nonzero leading coefficient. They make
the meaning of the norm bound easier to state, but should not be presented
as a new spectral method or independent result of comparable depth.

Our orthonormal-polynomial/reproducing-kernel expression
  ||B|| <= sum |c_j| K_h(-j^2,-j^2)
is an added standard reformulation. Original (C.2)-(C.5), p. 29, already does
the operative comparison between negative-node evaluations and a positive
integral norm, using Legendre polynomials. No stronger bound is obtained by
the reformulation in our note.

5. Energy and the comparison measure

Original: section 6, p. 17, introduces exactly the external field V in (6.1),
U_rho and I(rho), and the potential inequality (6.2). It explicitly says the
argument needs a comparison measure, not an explicit solution of the
equilibrium problem. Therefore describing rho as a 'dual certificate' adds
a name, not a new mathematical distinction.

Section 6.1, pp. 18-19, proves the zero-mass energy inequality (Lemma 6.2,
(6.5)), uses circle regularization, derives the 60 sqrt(epsilon) circle
averaging error, and obtains precisely the configuration estimate (6.6).
Our abstract certificate lemma isolates the one-line expansion already used
there. Our fuller regularization discussion restates the original safeguards.

Section 6.2, p. 20, (6.12), already proves the uniform logarithmic
sum-to-integral bound by monotonicity, decreasing dependence on t, and the
factorial estimate at t=0. Our E_m(t) argument repeats that proof in named
error-term notation. It must not be credited as a newly supplied repair of
a nonuniform Stirling step: Fauzan already avoids that problem.

Section 6.2, p. 20, (6.14)-(6.16), also already explains cancellation of
K^2 log K by S_K. Appendix A constructs the sixteen-arcsine measure and
verifies its numerical certificate.

Attribution status: almost entirely restatement and terminology changes.
The source is already clear about the roles of the measures and the need
for error and tail bounds.

6. Primitive normalization and J_xi

Original: section 2.1, p. 4, (2.5)-(2.6), defines F_K=S_K Delta_K and
Q_{K,M}=m_{K,M}F_K, and explicitly says m is specified through prime
exponents without computing content. Section 7, p. 21, (7.1), combines the
real and arithmetic costs, then gives the integer contradiction. The same
contradiction is outlined on p. 2.

Appendix C.3, p. 31, defines rational content exactly, sets
  P_K=Delta_K/cont_Q(Delta_K),
and proves Q_{K,M}=a_{K,M} P_K for a positive integer a_{K,M}. It explicitly
states that this removes the cutoff from the canonical primitive polynomial
and transfers the upper bounds; it is a consequence of explicit
normalization rather than a second irrationality argument.

Our J_xi(A)=log|A(xi)|-sum_p v_p^G(A)log p is not named in the source. But it
is exactly log|A(xi)/cont_Q(A)|. Its rational-scale invariance and the fact
that explicit normalization bounds it are immediate restatements of the
primitive normalization already in Appendix C.3. The abstract primitive
evaluation criterion merely abstracts the integer contradiction already on
pp. 2 and 21.

Attribution status: useful invariant notation and moving an existing
Appendix C.3 observation to the organizing front of the exposition. No new
local-to-global principle is needed or proved.

Suggested honest positioning
---------------------------
'An explanatory reorganization of Fauzan's construction, with classical
functional interpretations and a few elementary structural corollaries.'

For this part of the note, the most useful added synthesis is the all-X
signed-measure identity and the quotient-algebra viewpoint. The source
already contains the positive Gram structure, residue matrix, spectral
factorization, primitive normalization, comparison-measure strategy, uniform
sum-to-integral bound, and the quantitative implementation. Reproducing them
with attribution is legitimate exposition, but presenting those mechanisms
as newly discovered would overstate the note's contribution.
