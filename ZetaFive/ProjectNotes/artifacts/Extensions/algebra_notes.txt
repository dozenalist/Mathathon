1. The first honest replacement
------------------------------
The original definitions are (2.2)-(2.3), p.3. Merely evaluating the old
functional at X=zeta(7) does not produce the desired positive moment
functional. Both its polynomial moments and its pole values must change.

For any odd integer s>=3 define H_j^(s)=sum_{v=1}^j v^(-s), H_0^(s)=0.
The following is an independently derived generalization:

  mu_s(t^e) = (-1)^e B_(2e+2) (2e+3)(2e+4)...(2e+s)/(s-1)!
  mu_(s,X)(1/(t+j^2)) = j^(s-1)(X-H_j^(s))-1/(s-1)+1/(2j).

Extend by polynomial division and simple partial fractions. This is
well-defined: the polynomial part and the residues at distinct simple
poles are uniquely determined. Its dependence on X is affine.

For s=7 this reads

  mu_7(t^e) = (-1)^e B_(2e+2)
              (2e+3)(2e+4)(2e+5)(2e+6)(2e+7)/720,
  mu_(7,X)(1/(t+j^2)) = j^6(X-H_j^(7))-1/6+1/(2j).

In particular mu_7(1)=7/12, not 7/6. This is an important factor check.

2. Positivity really does survive
--------------------------------
The source is Proposition 2.2 and (2.10), p.5. The new positive weight is

  w_s(y) = 2(2*pi)^(s-1)y^s/(s-1)! * sum_{l>=1} l^(s-1)e^(-2*pi*l*y).

For s=7 its prefactor is (2*pi)^6 y^7/360. Every term is positive for y>0.
The moment integral is

  integral_0^infinity y^(2e) w_s(y)dy
   = 2(2e+s)!/((s-1)!(2*pi)^(2e+2)) * zeta(2e+2)
   = mu_s(t^e),

using Euler's formula for the even zeta values. The first equality is a
nonnegative sum/integral interchange followed by a gamma integral.

There is a useful direct proof of the pole identity, avoiding a guess
based on the s=5 formula. Put

  Phi_s(a,c) = integral_0^infinity y^s e^(-cy)/(y^2+a^2)dy.

Changing y to av shows

  c^(s-1)Phi_s(a,c) = a^(s-1)Phi_s(c,a).

Use this identity with a=j, c=2*pi*l in the nonnegative sum for the pole
integral. The partial fraction expansion of coth gives

  sum_{l>=1} 1/(x^2+(2*pi*l)^2)
     = (coth(x/2)-2/x)/(4x).

Consequently the pole integral equals

  j^(s-1)/(2(s-1)!) * integral_0^infinity
       x^(s-1)e^(-jx)(coth(x/2)-2/x)dx
   = 1/(2j) + j^(s-1) sum_{n>=1}(j+n)^(-s) - 1/(s-1).

In the last step use coth(x/2)=1+2 sum_{n>=1}e^(-nx) and gamma integrals.
This is precisely the claimed mu_(s,zeta(s)) pole value. Although the
last expression has subtractions, all individual integrals are finite
for s>=3, so the split is legitimate. The original integral is positive.

Therefore

  mu_(7,zeta(7))(R) = integral_0^infinity R(y^2) w_7(y)dy

for the complete rational-function domain, by linearity. The weight
tends to 1/pi at zero and decays as a polynomial times e^(-2*pi*y) at
infinity. In particular all polynomial moments exist.

There is also a direct line-by-line transplant of the source's own
integration-by-parts proof. Put f(y)=(e^(2*pi*y)-1)^(-1). For odd s,

  w_s(y)=2 y^s f^(s-1)(y)/(s-1)!,
  (d/dy)^(s-1)[y^s/(y^2+a^2)]
      =(s-1)! a^(s-1) Im((a-iy)^(-s)).

For the derivative identity, divide y^s by y^2+a^2. The polynomial
quotient has degree s-2 and is annihilated. The remainder term is
(-a^2)^((s-1)/2)y/(y^2+a^2); use y/(y^2+a^2)=Im((a-iy)^(-1)).
The two signs from the even derivative and remainder cancel. Integrate
by parts s-1 times. A boundary product at zero has factors O(y^(k+2))
and O(y^(-k-1)), hence is O(y); at infinity exponential decay applies.
Hermite's formula, cited in the source as DLMF (25.11.29), gives

  integral_0^infinity w_s(y)/(y^2+a^2)dy
      =a^(s-1)zeta(s,a)-1/(2a)-1/(s-1).

At integer a=j, replace zeta(s,j) by zeta(s)-H_(j-1)^(s) to get the
pole formula above. In particular six integrations replace four for
zeta(7). The coth calculation provides an independent consistency check.

With the original D_m(t)=product_{j=1}^m(t+j^2), N=3K/40 and h=K-N, keep

  G_7,K(X)_(i,j) = mu_(7,X)(D_N(t)^6 t^(i+j)/D_K(t)).

For a nonzero real polynomial q of degree below h, its quadratic form is

  integral_0^infinity D_N(y^2)^6/D_K(y^2) * q(y^2)^2 * w_7(y)dy > 0.

The integrand is positive except at finitely many zeros of q. Thus the
matrix is positive definite and its determinant is strictly positive.
There is no unwanted zeta(3) or zeta(5) in any matrix entry.

3. The degree calculation also survives
---------------------------------------
Source: (2.9), p.4. Cancel D_N from numerator and denominator, leaving
D_N^5/D_tail. The coefficient matrix of X is a square Vandermonde matrix,
a nonzero diagonal matrix, and its transpose. The old diagonal factor
j^4 becomes j^6. Hence

  [X^h] det G_7,K(X)
    = (-1)^(h(h-1)/2) product_{j=N+1}^K j^6 D_N(-j^2)^5 != 0.

Thus the determinant has degree exactly h, not merely at most h.
This statement keeps the original power D_N^6. Increasing that power to
8 is another possible experiment, but it is not necessary for positivity
and changes the real external field and normalization.

4. A small, exact example
-------------------------
This is an illustrative K=2,N=0,h=2 example, outside the paper's large-K
parameter restrictions. It checks the mechanism, not its asymptotics.

  mu_(7,X)(1/(t+1)) = X-2/3,
  mu_(7,X)(1/(t+4)) = 64X-773/12.

The moment matrix is

  [ -21X+85/4       85X-257/3      ]
  [  85X-257/3     -341X+4127/12   ].

Its determinant is

  Delta(X) = -64X^2 + (569/6)X - 4399/144.

Thus Q(X)=144Delta(X)=-9216X^2+13656X-4399 has integer coefficients,
and Q(zeta(7)) is approximately 0.48139987525. A single small positive
value is not an irrationality criterion: one needs a whole sequence
whose decay beats the denominator of a hypothetical rational input.

5. The transformed functional
-----------------------------
Sources: (3.1)-(3.7), pp.5-8. Let L(x^k)=B_k. For odd s define

  tau_s(P)=L(P^(s-2))/(s-1)!,
  tau_(s,X)(1/(x-r))=H_(d(r))^(s)-X,
  d(r)=r if r>=0, and -r-1 otherwise.

Then

  mu_(s,X)(R)=tau_(s,X)(x^s R(-x^2)).

For monomials this is direct differentiation. For a pole, polynomial
division of x^s/(j^2-x^2) gives an odd polynomial whose top term is
-x^(s-2), plus -j^(s-1)/2 times (1/(x-j)+1/(x+j)). Applying tau gives
-1/(s-1) from the top term; the harmonic increment gives 1/(2j).

Reflection remains tau(g(-1-x))=-tau(g(x)). The difference identity is

  tau_(s,X)(g(x+1)-g(x))=g^(s-1)(0)/(s-1)!,

for g regular at zero. For s=7, five derivatives replace three, and the
sixth Taylor coefficient replaces the fourth. The distribution formula
has factor p^(-6), replacing p^(-4), with Y=p^7X+C_p. This change explains
why ordinary nonzero residue classes can lose two more powers of p.

A simple example explains that loss before the determinant machinery.
Let p>=11 be prime and j=p+1. In H_j^(s), exactly one summand has a
denominator divisible by p: 1/p^s. Since j^(s-1) is a p-adic unit, the
constant coefficient of mu_(s,X)(1/(t+j^2)) has valuation exactly -s.
The change s=5 to s=7 therefore costs exactly two powers at this pole.
At j=p, on the other hand, the factor p^(s-1) cancels all but one power
in 1/p^s, and the remaining leading term is -1/(2p). Its valuation is
-1 for every odd s under discussion. This is why the zero class can
behave better than the ordinary classes.

6. An actual exception at the prime 7
------------------------------------
The s=7 far-pole expansion is

  tau_7^an(1/(z-u))
     = -(1/6) sum_{k>=0} binom(k+5,5) B_k u^(-k-6),

when v_p(u)<0. Define C_p=sum_{a=1}^{p-1}tau_7^an(1/(z+a/p)).
For p>=11, C_p belongs to p^7 Z_p. Indeed, its k=0 term is

  -(p^6/6) sum_{a=1}^{p-1}a^(-6).

Since p-1 does not divide 6, the finite-field sum vanishes modulo p.
The k=1 term has valuation at least 7; the k>=2 terms have valuation
at least k+5>=7, by von Staudt-Clausen. These estimates also imply
convergence.

At p=7, however, the k=0 sum is -1 modulo 7. All later terms are still
divisible by 7^7. Therefore

  C_7/7^6 = 1/6 = 6 (mod 7), and v_7(C_7)=6 exactly.

So the literal assertion C_p in p^7 Z_p for every p>=7 is false.
This is a local bookkeeping exception, not the asymptotic obstruction:
the prime 7 can be handled in the small-prime range, and C_7 is still
integral. The large-prime argument eventually sees only p>=11.

7. A favorable change in polynomial denominators
------------------------------------------------
Source for the s=5 comparison: pp.11-12, immediately before (4.10).
For p>=7 the denominator 720 is a p-adic unit, and mu_7(t^e) has valuation
at least -1. A Bernoulli denominator can occur only if p-1 divides 2e+2.
Write 2e+2=k(p-1). For k=1,...,5 the product of five consecutive factors
(2e+3)...(2e+7) contains kp, cancelling the Bernoulli denominator.

The first uncancelled possibility is k=6, e=3p-4. Thus all polynomial
moments with e<3p-4 are p-integral. This improves the original cutoff
e<2p-3. With unchanged D_N^6, the polynomial correction rank is at most

  max(0,K+4N-3p+3),

in place of max(0,K+4N-2p+2). To check the rank, the polynomial quotient
for entry i,j has degree at most i+j+6N-K. The correction vanishes when
i+j<K-6N+3p-4, so its first 3p-5N-3 rows and columns vanish whenever
that number lies between 0 and h. This gives the claimed rank bound.

This improvement must not be omitted when diagnosing the arithmetic
loss. It is not correct to assert that every part of the prime estimates
uniformly worsens by two powers per row.

8. A rigorous real-bound transplant with the same quadratic constant
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Sources: Section 6, especially (6.9)-(6.16), pp.19-20. Keep D_N^6,
S_K, alpha=3/40 and lambda=37/40 exactly as in the source. Define
F_7,K=S_K det G_7,K. The original external field is unchanged:

  V(t)=2*pi*sqrt(t)+integral_0^1 log(t+u^2)du
                      -6 integral_0^alpha log(t+u^2)du.

The source's energy inequality and comparison measure therefore apply
without alteration. What must be checked is the larger polynomial
prefactor of w_7.

For q=e^(-2*pi*y),

  sum_{l>=1} l^6 q^l
    = q(1+57q+302q^2+302q^3+57q^4+q^5)/(1-q)^7.

The numerator coefficients sum to 720. Using 1-e^(-u)>=u/(1+u),

  w_7(y) <= (1/pi)(1+2*pi*y)^7 e^(-2*pi*y)
          <= 2(2*pi)^6(1+y)^7 e^(-2*pi*y)
           < 2^19(1+y)^7 e^(-2*pi*y).

The last simple bound uses pi<4. Andreief's identity remains valid,
because exponential decay gives absolute convergence. Set y_i=K sqrt(t_i).
Repeating (6.13), the prefactor becomes

  K^[2h(h+6N-K)+18h] (2^18 e^12)^h / h!,

and the remaining integral has integrand

  product_(i<j)(t_i-t_j)^2
     * product_i e^(-K V(t_i)) t_i^(-1/2)(1+sqrt(t_i))^7.

The extra exponent 2h of K, compared with the original 16h, comes only
from replacing the fifth power by the seventh power. The energy bound
(6.9) supplies e^(-sum_i sqrt(t_i)) for integrability, and

  integral_0^infinity t^(-1/2)(1+sqrt(t))^7 e^(-sqrt(t))dt
    = 2 sum_{k=0}^7 binom(7,k) k! = 27400.

The remaining linear constant is bounded by

  120+sqrt(2)+12+18log(2)+log(27400) < 160.

For instance sqrt(2)<1.5, log(2)<0.7 and log(27400)<10.3 suffice.
Discarding the nonpositive term -log(h!) gives

  log Delta_7,K(zeta(7))
    <= 2h(h+6N-K)log K + (lambda M_0-I(rho))K^2
       +20h log K+160h.

Adding unchanged (6.15), the K^2 log K terms cancel. The source's
verified potential inequality (6.4) bounds the quadratic constant by
U=-2733991/2000000. Therefore the following transplant is rigorous:

  0 < F_7,K(zeta(7)),
  log F_7,K(zeta(7)) <= U K^2 +26K log K+200K,

for K in the original positive multiples of 40. This establishes the
same leading real decay as for zeta(5). The unresolved/failed transfer
must therefore be sought in integrality and its normalization cost,
not in positivity, polynomial degree, or this real decay estimate.

9. Sharpening the same measure cannot close the arithmetic gap
------------------------------------------------------------
experiments/main_measure_certificate.py and its JSON output provide an
additional rigorous, narrowly scoped check. At t=3/4, compute
phi_6(t)=2U_rho(t)-V(t) for the original sixteen-component measure.
Every admissible constant M_6 in phi_6<=M_6 satisfies M_6>=phi_6(3/4).
Hence the certificate's real coefficient obeys

  lambda M_6-I(rho)+C_6* >= lambda phi_6(3/4)-I(rho)+C_6*.

Rational interval arithmetic encloses the right-hand side in
[-1.373916736595719552, -1.373916736595719257]. The arithmetic
coefficient can also be sharpened by exactly integrating the proposed
piecewise affine local-cost function on [3,200], rather than using the
source's conservative finite-interval/tail envelope. This uses 1430
rational intervals and gives A_7,200=2.323379348180366491... .

Their combined coefficient is bounded below by a number in
[0.949462611584646939, 0.949462611584647235], and thus exceeds 9/10.
Using the report's original A_7,200 instead gives a lower witness in
[0.953535544479317997, 0.953535544479318293].

This is not a bound on the true growth of the determinant. It shows
that improving only these numerical enclosures, while retaining the
same local valuation bounds, cutoff M=200, and global-potential method
with the same comparison measure, cannot create the negative coefficient
needed by the argument. Better valuation bounds or another measure are
not excluded. The interval implementation reuses the separately audited
sqrt/log/atan/Machin-pi routines in single_point_certificate.py and checks
the interval nesting and total measure mass directly.

Scope
-----
These notes establish successful algebraic and analytic transplants and
identify exact changes in local bookkeeping. They do not show that every
variant of the method fails, nor that the repaired integer polynomials
cannot have better arithmetic than the inherited lower bounds prove.
The parent report contains the arithmetic comparison and its limitations.
