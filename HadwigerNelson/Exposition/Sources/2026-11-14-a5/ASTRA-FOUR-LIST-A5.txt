RESULT

The full list field in the question is NOT list-colourable. More precisely,
there exists a finite unit-distance configuration F, with all its vertices
strictly inside the stated open cells, having no proper colouring from L.
It uses two odd walks in two forced-pair H lobes, together with the single
four-list vertex z0=i/2. The contacts and anchor edge are explicit below;
the finite connecting walks are established by an exact local four-edge
construction and connectedness. Thus this is a finite-existence proof,
not yet an enumerated coordinate certificate for every vertex of F.

The actual source consumer is finite_density_realization. Consequently,
for a measurable WeakColoring 6, the actual palette at one centre cannot
contain the specified P(t) for almost every t. Equality to P is unnecessary.
The same proof has a direct essential-binary-rigidity interpretation.

No uniqueness of the A4 colouring, no exhaustive 512-case reduction,
no palette-cardinality assumption, and no cross-centre palette transport
is used. In fact, the proof does not use A4's V--H separation factorizations,
its Reuleaux argument, or its full two-list colouring. It uses only the
two-list identity for H and the elementary geometry proved here.

LIMIT: This does not prove that arbitrary common-interface data produce
this exact paired field. That remains a separate producer obligation.
The two-list locus itself is colourable, consistently with A4: the new
obstruction essentially uses z0, a four-list vertex.

1. LISTS AND THE PARTICULAR H LOBES

Translate the centre to 0. Write e(t)=exp(it), s=sqrt(3)/2, and
  A0={1,4}, A1={2,5}, A2={0,3},
  P(t)=A_(floor((t+pi/6)/(pi/3)) mod 3).
Let D be 0<|z|<2 with the six circles |z-e(pi/6+k*pi/3)|=1 removed.
Its points have a unique unordered representation z=e(a)+e(b), with
the smaller angular separation strictly between 0 and pi. Define
  L(z)={0,1,2,3,4,5}\(P(a) union P(b)).

Set
  H={e(a)+e(b): pi/6<a<pi/2< b<5pi/6}.
Its directions belong to sectors 1 and 2, so L=A0 throughout H.
Every point of H lies in D: the directions are interior and nonparallel,
and an alternative boundary-direction representation is excluded by
uniqueness. Define the isometries
  R(z)=e(pi/3)z,   S(z)=-conj(z).
H is invariant under S. Therefore
  H1=R(H),     with L=A1;
  H2=S(H1)=R^(-1)(H), with L=A2.                     (1)
These statements follow by shifting sector indices by +1 and -1.

The central vertex is
  z0=i/2.
Put a=arcsin(1/4), so 0<a<pi/6. Then
  z0=e(a)+e(pi-a).
Both directions use A0 (sectors 0 and 3), so
  L(z0)=A1 union A2.                                 (2)
It lies strictly in D. For example its distances to the six boundary
centres are among 1/2, sqrt(3)/2, sqrt(7)/2, and 3/2, never 1.

2. EXACT VERTICAL SECTIONS OF H

For 0<=u<s define
  g(u)=1/2+sqrt(1-(s-u)^2),
  U(u)=1+sqrt(1-u^2).
Then the exact description is
  H={(u,v): |u|<s, g(|u|)<v<U(u)}.                    (3)
In particular,
  g(|u|)<3/2<U(u) for |u|<s,
so H is open and path connected: move vertically to v=3/2 and then
horizontally along that line.

Here is a verification of (3), including the converse.
Put C=(0,1), A=(s,1/2), Dleft=(-s,1/2).
For q=e(a)+e(b) in H, its coordinates satisfy |u|<s and 1<v<2.
Also
  |q-C|<1, |q-A|>1, |q-Dleft|>1.                     (4)
For the first inequality rotate by -pi/2 and write
  a'=a-pi/2 in (-pi/3,0), b'=b-pi/2 in (0,pi/3),
  delta=(b'-a')/2, m=(a'+b')/2.
Then
  |e(a')+e(b')-1|^2-1
      =4 cos(delta)(cos(delta)-cos(m))<0,
because |m|<delta<pi/3.
For |q-A|>1, the nonzero chord e(a)-A has direction in
(2pi/3,5pi/6), whose scalar product with e(b) is positive; expand
|e(b)+(e(a)-A)|^2. Reflection gives the other strict inequality.
For u>=0, (4) and v>1 give g(u)<v<U(u). Reflect for u<0.

Conversely, let 0<=u<s and g(u)<v<U(u), and write q=r e(theta).
Then pi/6<theta<=pi/2 and 0<r<2. Put alpha=acos(r/2).
The inequalities |q-C|<1 and |q-A|>1 give respectively
  alpha>pi/2-theta,   alpha<theta-pi/6.
Hence
  pi/6<theta-alpha<pi/2<theta+alpha<5pi/6,
and q=e(theta-alpha)+e(theta+alpha) belongs to H. Reflection proves
the converse for u<0 as well.

3. TWO CONNECTED ACTIVE LOBES; NO RIGIDITY ASSUMPTION ON THE CORE

Let Qplus=(s,3/2), Qminus=(-s,3/2), which belong to closure(H).
Define
  Bplus={p=(u,v) in H: u>0 and |p-Qminus|>1},
  Bminus=S(Bplus).
Both are nonempty open PATH CONNECTED sets. Every point in either
has an actual unit-distance neighbour inside H.

3a. Path connectedness, with all endpoint cases.
Put d=1-s, so 0<d<s. For 0<u<d set
  ell(u)=3/2-sqrt(1-(u+s)^2),
  h(u)=3/2+sqrt(1-(u+s)^2).
At such u the Bplus section is exactly
  (g(u),ell(u)) union (h(u),U(u)).                    (5)
Both intervals are nonempty. Indeed g(0)=ell(0)=1 and
U(0)=h(0)=2. The function f(t)=t/sqrt(1-t^2) is strictly increasing
on (0,1), and differentiation on (0,d) gives
  (ell-g)'=f(u+s)-f(s-u)>0,
  (U-h)'=f(u+s)-f(u)>0.

For d<u<s, the whole section (g(u),U(u)) belongs to Bplus, because
u+s>1. This right-hand region is path connected through v=3/2.
For u=d, all section points except (d,3/2) are in Bplus.

Any point in the lower interval of (5) can be written uniquely as
  (u, g(u)+lambda(ell(u)-g(u))), 0<lambda<1.
Keep lambda fixed and increase u continuously to d. Since ell(d)=3/2,
the endpoint has height strictly between g(d) and 3/2 and is in Bplus.
By openness, a short further horizontal step reaches u>d. The upper
interval is handled with h(u)+lambda(U(u)-h(u)); its endpoint at d
has height strictly greater than 3/2. Points already at u=d are
handled by the same short horizontal step. This connects every point
to the right-hand path-connected region. Reflection proves the claim
for Bminus.

3b. Actual unit neighbours, not an assumed colouring property.
For p in Bplus, the inequality |p-Qminus|>1 and Qminus in closure(H)
give q in H with |p-q|>1. A path in H from p to q, supplied by (3),
starts at distance 0 from p and ends above 1. The intermediate value
theorem gives r in H with |p-r|=1. The same holds for Bminus.
Since H is open, this unit direction can be perturbed slightly while
its endpoint stays in H, giving a second nonparallel unit direction.
This is exactly Rigidity.open_two_unit_directions.

Nothing here declares the region near the H axis rigid. Only Bplus
and Bminus, which possess actual unit neighbours, are used.

4. EXACT EVEN-PATH PROPAGATION ON A CONNECTED ACTIVE SET

Geometric lemma. Let W be open, and T be a connected subset of W.
Suppose every p in T has a unit neighbour in W. Then any two points
of T are joined by a finite EVEN-length unit-distance walk in W.

Proof. At p choose two nonparallel unit vectors v,w with p+v,p+w in W.
For each small displacement h uniquely write
  h=a*i*v+b*i*w,
where a,b are real and depend linearly on h. Define
  E(v,a)=(sqrt(1-a^2/4)+i*a/2)*v.
For |a|<2, both |E(v,a)| and |E(v,a)-a*i*v| equal 1; similarly for b.
Thus the five points
  p,
  p+E(v,a),
  p+a*i*v,
  p+a*i*v+E(w,b),
  p+h
form a four-unit-edge walk. At h=0 their limits are p,p+v,p,p+w,p,
all in W. Openness and continuity ensure all five remain in W for
every sufficiently small h, with |a|,|b|<2. This is precisely the
source's evenPath_edges and evenPath_local construction.

Partition T by the equivalence relation of even-walk reachability
through W. The preceding paragraph says each class is relatively open
in T. The union of all other classes is also relatively open, so every
class is relatively closed. Connectedness makes the nonempty partition
have one class. Reachability here means an actual finite walk, by its
definition; no infinite path or limiting graph edge is substituted. QED.

Apply this with W=H and T=Bplus or Bminus. The anchors
  aplus=(1/2,3/2), aminus=(-1/2,3/2)
are in the corresponding lobes by (3) and s+1/2>1. They form a unit
edge. Therefore ANY pminus in Bminus and pplus in Bplus are joined
by a finite ODD unit walk in H: an even walk to aminus, the anchor
edge, and an even walk from aplus to pplus.

5. EXACT CONTACTS FROM THE FOUR-LIST VERTEX

Rotate z0 back through pi/3:
  zprime=R^(-1)(z0)=(s/2,1/4).
Define the two explicit points
  qminus=(-sqrt(3)/24, (6+sqrt(429))/24),
  qplus= ( sqrt(3)/12, (3+2sqrt(33))/12).
Write their heights as vminus and vplus. Then
  1<vminus<9/8,  1<vplus<5/4,                        (6)
because 18<sqrt(429)<21 and 9/2<sqrt(33)<6.
Direct squares give
  |qminus-zprime|^2=(147+429)/576=1,
  |qplus-zprime|^2=(3+33)/36=1.                     (7)

Both contacts are STRICTLY inside H, and are in the indicated active
lobes. The following algebra checks all these claims without rounding.
For either q=(u,v), equation (7) gives
  u^2+v^2=s*u+v/2+3/4.                              (8)
For qminus, s*u=-1/16. Consequently
  |qminus-C|^2-1 = 11/16-(3/2)vminus <0,
  |qminus-A|^2-1 = 13/16-vminus/2 >0,
  |qminus-Dleft|^2-1 = 9/16-vminus/2 >0.
Together with -s<u<0, v>1, these imply membership in H by (3)-(4).
Moreover
  |qminus-Qplus|^2-1=(5/2)(9/8-vminus)>0,
so qminus belongs to Bminus.

For qplus, s*u=1/8, and
  |qplus-C|^2-1 = 7/8-(3/2)vplus <0,
  |qplus-A|^2-1 = 5/8-vplus/2 >0,
  |qplus-Dleft|^2-1 = 9/8-vplus/2 >0,
and
  |qplus-Qminus|^2-1=(5/2)(5/4-vplus)>0.
Thus qplus belongs to Bplus. All bounds are strict by (6), giving
positive margins from the H cell boundaries and active-lobe boundaries.

By Section 4, there is a finite odd unit walk Gamma in H with endpoints
qminus,qplus. Its two images
  Gamma1=R(Gamma), Gamma2=S(R(Gamma))
lie in H1,H2 respectively. All four endpoints are unit neighbours of
z0 by (7), because R is an isometry, R(zprime)=z0, and S(z0)=z0.

6. THE FINITE LIST OBSTRUCTION

Let F consist of z0 and all vertices in Gamma1 and Gamma2. This is a
finite subset of D. Repeated walk vertices are harmless; extra unit
edges among these vertices only add constraints.

Suppose F had a proper colouring from L. Every vertex of Gamma1 has
the same two-element list A1. Colours alternate on its odd-length walk,
so its two endpoints have the two DISTINCT colours of A1. Both are
adjacent to z0, so z0 cannot use either colour of A1. The same argument
on Gamma2 excludes both colours of A2. This contradicts (2).

This proves finite non-list-colourability, without assuming any particular
two-list colouring, measurability, or uniqueness of a bipartition.
Every vertex belongs to D, an open set, and F is finite. In particular
  min over p in F of min(|p|, 2-|p|,
      min_k abs(|p-e(pi/6+k*pi/3)|-1))
is strictly positive. Thus the finite obstruction has positive cell
margins. A numerical lower bound and a full coordinate list require
choosing the finite even walks explicitly; neither is claimed here.

7. ACTUAL SOURCE CONSUMER: EXCLUSION OF P AT ONE CENTRE

Let c:C->Fin 6 be measurable and satisfy WeakColoring 6. Suppose its
actual palette at x contains P(t) for almost every t. Translate x to 0
in the geometry above, or translate every displayed vertex by x.

On each open angular sector, the required label pair gives a RealState:
the sector lift is measurable and is almost everywhere contained in
each corresponding liftPalette. At every interior angle its complement
has line-density zero. States.states_densityOne therefore supplies
  DensityOne {q: c(q) in L(y)} at x+y
for every y in D, including every vertex of F. Nonparallelity is built
into the interior sum representation. Apply finite_density_realization
to the finite vertices x+F and lists L. It returns a proper L-colouring
of that finite unit graph, contradicting Section 6.

Thus the explicit paired P cannot occur even as an almost-everywhere
subpalette of an actual centre of a measurable weak six-colouring.
This is a source-licensed one-centre consequence. It does not require
the unproved exhaustiveness or "if and only if" claims in Claude05.

8. EQUIVALENT ESSENTIAL-RIGIDITY READING, AND NULL-SET AUDIT

For readers preferring the existing analytic consumer, the exact same
geometry works directly through Rigidity.binary_open_local_rigidity.

First, the density-one pair restriction at every point of H1 or H2
implies its almost-everywhere pair restriction on that open set. To see
this using source declarations, intersect ae_density_actual with the
restriction: at a density point of an actual colour outside the pair,
its actual-colour set and the pair set would both have density one,
although they are disjoint. DensityOne.inter and measure_ne_zero give
the contradiction. This is an a.e. statement, not pointwise properness.

At every point of either rotated active lobe, Section 3 gives genuine
nonparallel unit directions landing in the same open pair region.
Use U=V=that H-copy in binary_open_local_rigidity. It gives an ambient
essential-colour neighbourhood at every active point. The path-connected
lobes and connected_local_ae_constant yield one essential colour on
each lobe. Those colours belong to the prescribed pair, since each lobe
is a nonempty open set and the pair restriction holds almost everywhere.
The rotated anchor edge and ae_translated_unit_pair force the two lobe
colours to be different. They therefore exhaust that pair.

The four explicit contact points in Section 5 consequently have ambient
essential neighbourhoods carrying all four labels in A1 union A2.
For a unit contact p of essential colour i, ae_translated_unit_pair at
z0,p says c(t+z0)!=c(t+p) for almost every translation t. On a sufficiently
small translation ball, c(t+p)=i a.e.; hence c(t+z0)!=i a.e. Taking the
minimum of the four positive neighbourhood radii excludes all four
labels on a ball about z0. This contradicts its density-one list (2).

Only finitely many contact and anchor directions are used at the end;
there is no uncountable intersection of conull sets. The source's
connected_local_ae_constant already handles the local-to-global step.
The potentially flexible isolated core of H is never assumed constant.
