\begin{abstract}
The chromatic number of the plane, $\chi(\R^2)$, is the least number of colours needed so that no two points at distance exactly one share a colour. A manuscript supplied to this event (OpenAI, September 2026) proves that five colours never suffice, so $6\le\chi(\R^2)\le7$. That theorem is prior work and is not ours. During the event we did three things. First, we wrote a staged, figure-supported explanation of the manuscript's proof, checked step by step against the manuscript and its Lean development. Second, we proved local results about the manuscript's ``interfaces'' at a common centre. The main ones are a closed $60^\circ$ cone obstruction (CC60), which cannot be widened for arbitrary finite palettes, and a three-centre proof of the manuscript's strict-state separation lemma. The latter is integrated into a Lean build of the supplied proof that, together with an encoding refactor and a dead-code and step-ban pass, is 415 lines shorter. Both have archived Lean~4.34.1 checks. Third, we recorded further refereed local results, finite computations, negative results and corrections, each with its status in the team's results ledger. \textbf{No global chromatic result.} The bounds $6\le\chi(\R^2)\le7$ are inherited from the supplied proof and the classical seven-colouring; nothing we add changes them. Every claim below carries its status.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%======================================================================
Colour every point of the plane so that any two points at distance exactly $1$ get different colours. How many colours are needed? The question goes back to Nelson and to Hadwiger \cite{Hadwiger}.

\begin{itemize}
\item Upper bound. A tiling by regular hexagons of diameter slightly below $1$, coloured in a repeating pattern of seven, shows that seven colours suffice \st{cited}.
\item Lower bound four. The seven-point Moser spindle is a unit-distance graph that cannot be three-coloured \cite{MoserMoser} \st{cited}.
\item Lower bound five. De Grey found the first $5$-chromatic unit-distance graphs in 2018 \cite{deGrey}; see also \cite{Heule,ExooIsmailescu,Parts} \st{cited}.
\item Measurable colourings. When every colour class is Lebesgue measurable, Falconer had already shown in 1981 that five colours are needed \cite{Falconer} \st{cited}.
\end{itemize}

The supplied manuscript \cite{OAI} closes the gap from five to six. It proves that every five-colouring of the plane, with no regularity assumption, contains a monochromatic unit pair \st{cited}. Its proof has two bridges.
\begin{enumerate}
\item An analytic transfer turns a hypothetical proper colouring into a \emph{weak measurable} colouring (manuscript Theorem~1.3, Proposition~4.2).
\item A geometric and topological argument shows that no weak measurable five-colouring exists (manuscript Theorem~1.4, Sections~5--8).
\end{enumerate}
The manuscript is accompanied by a Lean development, pinned to OpenAI commit \texttt{fd4aeeb2}, Lean~4.34.1 and mathlib \texttt{d13f23b7} \st{cited}.

\section{The supplied proof, step by step \st{cited}}
%======================================================================
This section summarises manuscript Sections~4--8 at the level of our reader, Lessons~1--6. All results here are the manuscript's.

\subsection{Weak measurable colourings}
A colouring $c:\R^2\to\{1,\dots,k\}$ with measurable classes is \emph{weak} if almost every unit pair has distinct colours, with respect to the measure $dx\,d\sigma(u)$ on positions $x$ and unit directions $u$. Our reader explains this with a unit ruler: place it at random in a window $B$ and turn it uniformly. A placement is \emph{bad} if both ends get the same colour. Weakness says the probability $P_B$ of a bad placement is zero in every window. A proper colouring forbids bad placements outright. A weak one forbids them only up to a null set, so changing a colouring on a null set preserves weakness. Theorem~1.3 shows that a proper $k$-colouring yields a weak measurable $k$-colouring. The proof is spectral and uses a Haar-rigidity theorem (manuscript Theorem~2.3). We summarise it but do not re-derive it.

\paragraph{How the transfer works.} \st{cited} Restrict the assumed proper colouring to the countable algebraic plane, and average its translates and algebraic rotations to obtain a symmetry-invariant probability law on labelings; this gives symmetry but not yet measurability. Translations act on square-integrable random variables with a spectral description by characters. Manuscript Theorem~2.3 shows that a rotation-invariant law concentrated away from the ordinary characters $z\mapsto\exp(i\xi\cdot z)$ must be Haar probability, so the irregular part of the spectrum has zero correlation under every nonzero algebraic translation. Projecting each colour indicator onto the ordinary-character part is a conditional expectation: it keeps the coordinates nonnegative with sum one, and keeps the zero correlations at unit translations. The resulting fields extend continuously to the real plane. Choosing the least label with positive field value, and a fixed label on the remaining null set, gives a measurable colouring in which two equal labels at unit distance would force a positive correlation; so it is weak (manuscript Sections~2--4, Proposition~4.2).

\subsection{From ``almost never'' to ``never'' (manuscript Lemma 4.3)}
Call $a$ \emph{robust} in colour $i$ if the proportion of colour $i$ in small disks around $a$ tends to $1$; by Lebesgue's density theorem almost every point of colour $i$ is robust in $i$.

\begin{lemma}[manuscript Lemma 4.3]
In a weak measurable colouring, two points robust in the same colour are never exactly one unit apart.
\end{lemma}
\noindent\emph{Idea.} Suppose $b=a+u_0$ with both points robust in $i$. Start a ruler anywhere in $B(a,h)$ and turn it within angle $h$ of $u_0$; its far end lands in $B(b,2h)$. For each direction in this fan, at most $\varepsilon$ of the start disk misses colour $i$, and at most $4\varepsilon$ of it lands outside colour $i$ (the landing disk has four times the area). So for $\varepsilon<1/5$ a positive fraction of starts gives a bad ruler. The fan has positive angular measure, hence $P_{B(a,h)}>0$, contradicting weakness.

\subsection{Straddling a connected set}
For compact $K\subset\R^2$ the manuscript defines the open \emph{straddle region}
\[
\Delta(K)=\{z:\ \min_{q\in K}|z-q|<1<\max_{q\in K}|z-q|\}.
\]
If $K$ is connected, every $z\in\Delta(K)$ is at distance exactly $1$ from some point of $K$. This is the intermediate value theorem applied to $q\mapsto|z-q|$ \st{cited; classical}. Our reader's illustration: if every point of a connected $K$ is robust in colour $i$, then by Lemma~4.3 no point of $\Delta(K)$ is robust in $i$, so colour $i$ has zero area in $\Delta(K)$ \st{illustration}. Connectedness is essential: with $K$ the two endpoints of a segment of length 2 and $z$ one of them, $z$ straddles $K$ but has no unit contact.

\subsection{Palettes, transitions and states (manuscript Sections 5--6)}
\begin{itemize}
\item Traces and palettes. At a centre $x$, weak-$*$ limits of colour records on typical circles of radius tending to $1$ are \emph{traces}; at almost every direction a trace is a probability vector over labels. The \emph{palette} $P_x(e)$ is the essential union of the labels that traces make positive at direction $e$ (Definition~5.1).
\item Palettes are small. Palettes at directions $60^\circ$ apart are almost surely disjoint (Corollary~5.4). In the five-colour setting almost every palette has one or two labels (Proposition~5.6).
\item Transitions and states. Colour transitions near a centre give traces avoiding a pair of labels (Lemma~6.2). \emph{States}, angular sets on which the palette contains a given label group, obey a strict separation (Lemma~6.3) and cannot alternate in a singleton--pair pattern (Lemma~6.4). Centres whose transition graph contains a cycle form a closed, locally finite set (Theorem~6.5).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Interfaces and the cycle contradiction (manuscript Sections 7--8)}
Proposition~7.1 extracts, from a weak measurable five-colouring, three things:
\begin{itemize}
\item a simple cycle of $\ell\in\{3,4,5\}$ labels and a centre $x$;
\item for each edge $i_ji_{j+1}$ of the cycle, a compact connected $K_j\ni x$ and a direction $v_j$, such that points of $K_j$ approach $x$ from direction $v_j$;
\item the exclusion: both endpoint labels have closed essential supports disjoint from $\Delta(K_j)$.
\end{itemize}
Lemma~8.1 converts this into one-sided strips. For a direction $e$ not perpendicular to any $v_j$, a thin strip just \emph{outside} the unit circle about $x$ near direction $e$ uses only labels not excluded by the interfaces with $e\cdot v_j>0$. A thin strip just \emph{inside} uses only labels not excluded by those with $e\cdot v_j<0$. Propositions~8.2, 8.5 and~8.10 then exclude cycles of length $5$, $4$ and~$3$:
\begin{itemize}
\item length~5: palette rotation rules and the bound of two labels per palette;
\item length~4: state continuity and projective ray geometry;
\item length~3: placing the Moser spindle inside a region that uses only three labels, with a rational certificate in manuscript~\S8.6.
\end{itemize}
Hence no weak measurable five-colouring exists, and by the transfer no proper one.

\subsection{Statement}
At a centre $x$, a \emph{state} $(A,J)$ is a measurable set $A$ of directions and a label set $J$ with $J\subseteq P_x(t)$ for almost every $t\in A$ (Lean: \texttt{RealState}). The \emph{support} of $B$ is the set of directions every neighbourhood of which meets $B$ in positive measure.

\begin{theorem}[fixed-centre part of manuscript Lemma 6.3]\label{thm:three}
Let $c$ be a weak measurable $k$-colouring and $x$ a centre. Let $(A,J)$ and $(B,K)$ be states at $x$ with $|A|>0$. Suppose $X$ is the only label in neither $J$ nor $K$. Then no two directions $b,d$ in the support of $B$ satisfy $|e^{ib}-e^{id}|=1$, that is, no two are $60^\circ$ apart.
\end{theorem}
The manuscript also takes $J$ and $K$ disjoint; the Lean statement does not need this.

\subsection{Two supplied facts \st{cited}}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(F1)] \emph{Unit-separated centres} (Lean \texttt{palette\_\allowbreak{}neighbor\_\allowbreak{}disjoint}; manuscript Lemma~5.2 with Remark~5.3). If $|y-x|=1$ then $P_x(t)\cap P_y(t)=\varnothing$ for almost every $t$.
\item[(F2)] \emph{Avoiding traces at support points} (Lean \texttt{state\_\allowbreak{}support\_\allowbreak{}avoiding\_\allowbreak{}trace}, \texttt{avoiding\_\allowbreak{}trace\_\allowbreak{}palette}). If $(B,K)$ is a state at $x$ and $b$ is in the support of $B$, then $y=x+e^{ib}$ carries a trace that vanishes on $K$. Consequently $P_y(t)\not\subseteq K$ for almost every $t$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Counting lemma \st{proved}}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:count}
Let $P,Q,R$ be finite label sets and $J,K$ label sets such that every label outside $J\cup K$ equals $X$. If $J\subseteq P$, the sets $P,Q,R$ are pairwise disjoint, and $Q\not\subseteq K$, $R\not\subseteq K$, we reach a contradiction.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Pick $j\in Q\setminus K$. Since $Q\cap P=\varnothing$ and $J\subseteq P$, $j\notin J$. So $j\notin J\cup K$, hence $j=X$. Likewise $X\in R$. Then $X\in Q\cap R$, contradicting disjointness.
\end{proof}
Only ``at most one label outside $J\cup K$'' is used; with two leftover labels, $P=\{0,1\}$, $Q=\{3\}$, $R=\{4\}$ (with $J=\{0,1\}$, $K=\{2\}$) satisfies every hypothesis. An exhaustive check of all triples of subsets of $\{0,\dots,4\}$ confirms 0 solutions for $J=\{0,1\}$, $K=\{2,3\}$ and 8 for $K=\{2\}$ \st{computation}.

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:three} \st{proved}}
Suppose $b,d$ lie in the support of $B$ with $|e^{ib}-e^{id}|=1$. Put $y=x+e^{ib}$ and $z=x+e^{id}$; then $|x-y|=|x-z|=|y-z|=1$. These three centres are fixed before any direction $t$ is chosen.
\begin{itemize}
\item By (F1), applied to the three sides, $P_x(t),P_y(t),P_z(t)$ are pairwise disjoint for almost every $t$.
\item By (F2) at $b$ and at $d$, neither $P_y(t)$ nor $P_z(t)$ is contained in $K$, for almost every $t$.
\item Since $(A,J)$ is a state, $J\subseteq P_x(t)$ for almost every $t\in A$.
\end{itemize}
Finitely many null sets are removed and $|A|>0$, so some $t$ satisfies all of these. Lemma~\ref{lem:count} with $P=P_x(t)$, $Q=P_y(t)$, $R=P_z(t)$ gives the contradiction. \qed

\subsection{Comparison with the manuscript's proof}
The manuscript approximates support points by density-one directions $b',d'$ of $B$, whose chord is only close to $1$. For a density-one direction $a$ of $A$, the point $x+a+b'$ is robust in $X$. An implicit-function reparametrisation $a\mapsto c(a)$ makes $x+a+b'$ and $x+c(a)+d'$ exactly one unit apart, and an $L^1$ argument finds $a$ with $c(a)$ also a density direction. This contradicts Lemma~4.3. The three-centre proof adds no new analysis: it reuses (F1) and (F2), whose proofs already rest on manuscript Lemma~5.2. The forced-label step already appears in the manuscript's proof of Lemma~6.7, with one shifted centre and the $60^\circ$ exclusion in place of a third centre. What is new is using a third centre in place of the reparametrisation.

\section{A closed $60^\circ$ cone obstruction (CC60)}
\label{sec:cc60}

The manuscript's final contradiction concerns exclusion sets meeting at one
centre. CC60 shows that such a meeting leaves at least three labels
available whenever the approach directions fit in a closed $60^\circ$
cone. CC60 is a \emph{local obstruction}: it
forbids one configuration inside a hypothetical measurable weak colouring.
\textbf{No global chromatic result.} The bound remains
$6\le\chi(\mathbb R^2)\le7$ \emph{(cited: manuscript, Theorem~1.1)}.
% SOURCE: PAPER:56-60 (Theorem 1.1: 6 <= chi(R^2) <= 7; six vs seven unresolved)
% SOURCE: HN/PROGRESS.md:9 ("a local obstruction, not a plane-colouring solution")
% SOURCE: HN/RESULTS-LEDGER.md:5 (verbatim sentence "No global chromatic result."), :278 (CC60 "local obstruction only")

\subsection{Setting}

Identify the plane with $\mathbb C$ and use labels $[k]=\{0,\dots,k-1\}$.
A map $c:\mathbb C\to[k]$ is a \emph{weak colouring} if it is
almost-everywhere measurable and $c(z)\ne c(z+e^{i\theta})$ for almost
every $(z,\theta)$, for area times angular measure. The manuscript turns
any proper colouring into such a colouring \emph{(cited)}.
% SOURCE: SRC/Coloring.lean:27-29 (def WeakColoring)
% SOURCE: PAPER:64 (Definition 1.2); PAPER:60-62 (transfer from unrestricted proper colourings, every finite k); PAPER:89 (deduction of Theorem 1.1)
The \emph{spatial support} $\operatorname{supp}_c(j)$ is the support of area
measure restricted to $c^{-1}(j)$: the closed set of points whose every
neighbourhood meets colour $j$ in positive area.
% SOURCE: SRC/AngularBoundary.lean:307-308 (def spatialSupport); PAPER:2207-2208 ("closed essential supports H_i")

For $K\subseteq\mathbb C$, the \emph{straddle region} is the open set
\[
  \Delta(K)=\{z\in\mathbb C:\ |z-p|<1<|z-q|\ \text{for some }p,q\in K\}.
\]
For nonempty compact $K$ it is the manuscript's
$\min_{q\in K}|z-q|<1<\max_{q\in K}|z-q|$. Picture a tiny $K$ near $x$:
$\Delta(K)$ is a thin crescent just outside the unit circle about $x$, on
the side $K$ points towards, plus a thin crescent just inside it on the far
side.
% SOURCE: SRC/AngularBoundary.lean:146-155 (def Straddle, "equivalent witness form"; straddle_isOpen)
% SOURCE: PAPER:2214-2221 (equation (7.1), openness); WALK:542-555 (Section 22, shadow of a tiny interface)

We write $\mathrm{AC}(x,K,v)$, \emph{approaching-continuum data}, if
$x\in K$ and there are $r_n>0$ with $r_n\to0$ and unit vectors $w_n\to v$
with $x+r_nw_n\in K$. Despite the name, $K$ need not be connected, compact
or bounded. The vector $v$ is \emph{signed}: it records one way in which
$K$ approaches~$x$.
% SOURCE: SRC/AngularBoundary.lean:233-243 (structure ApproachingContinuum; no connectedness/compactness fields)
% SOURCE: HN/Exposition/SourceMap.md:42; OUT/ASTRA-CONE-REFEREE.txt:57-62

\paragraph{Why this setting.} At one centre $x$, Proposition~7.1 of the
manuscript gives a cycle of $3$--$5$ labels. For each edge
$\{i_j,i_{j+1}\}$ it gives a compact connected $K_j\ni x$ and points
$q_{j,n}\in K_j\setminus\{x\}$ tending to $x$ with directions tending to
$v_j$. Both endpoint supports miss $\Delta(K_j)$ \emph{(cited)}. With
$r_n=|q_{j,n}-x|$ this is $\mathrm{AC}(x,K_j,v_j)$ plus CC60's exclusion for
$F_j=\{i_j,i_{j+1}\}$. CC60 keeps these two ingredients only; it drops
compactness and connectedness and allows any label sets.
% SOURCE: PAPER:2225-2237 (Proposition 7.1, items 1-3)
% SOURCE: SRC/AngularBoundary.lean:233-234 (docstring: "approach data furnished by the manuscript interface proposition")
Lemma~8.1 signs each edge by $e\cdot v_j$. For a positive edge, the identity
$|x+e-q_{j,n}|^2=1+r_{j,n}^2-2r_{j,n}\,(e\cdot w_{j,n})$ puts a strip just
outside radius one, near $e$, inside $\Delta(K_j)$, and the labels allowed
there are nonempty \emph{(cited)}. In Lean this strip is
\texttt{Approaching\allowbreak{}Continuum.\allowbreak{}outer\_\allowbreak{}strip}, the only geometric fact about
$\Delta(K_i)$ that CC60 uses. CC60 strengthens ``nonempty'' to ``at least
three'' for families in a closed $60^\circ$ cone.
% SOURCE: PAPER:2683-2707 (Lemma 8.1 and proof)
% SOURCE: SRC/AngularBoundary.lean:244-250 (ApproachingContinuum.outer_strip); CONE/ConeObstruction.lean:108 (its use)

\begin{theorem}[CC60; \texttt{closed60\_\allowbreak{}exclusions\_\allowbreak{}three\_\allowbreak{}le}]\label{thm:cc60}
Let $k\in\mathbb N$ and let $c:\mathbb C\to[k]$ be a measurable weak
colouring. Let $\iota$ be a finite set, $x\in\mathbb C$ and
$\varphi\in\mathbb R$. For each $i\in\iota$, let $K_i\subseteq\mathbb C$ and
$\alpha_i\in[\varphi-\pi/6,\varphi+\pi/6]$ satisfy
$\mathrm{AC}(x,K_i,e^{i\alpha_i})$. Let $F_i\subseteq[k]$ be an arbitrary set
of labels with $\operatorname{supp}_c(j)\cap\Delta(K_i)=\emptyset$ for every
$j\in F_i$. Then the residual set
$R=\{j\in[k]: j\notin F_i\text{ for all }i\in\iota\}$ has $|R|\ge3$.
\end{theorem}
% SOURCE: CONE/ConeObstruction.lean:148-157 (exact statement); CONE/cone-audit-v3.log:18-25 (printed signature)
\noindent\emph{Status: proved (paper argument, independently reviewed);
checked in Lean 4.34.1 (archived logs).}
% SOURCE: OUT/ASTRA-CONE-REFEREE.txt:4-10 (ACCEPT at paper level, aperture exactly 60 degrees), :43-55 (contract)
% SOURCE: CONE/cone-obstruction-v3.log:1-4 (Lean 4.34.1, passed, exit 0)
% LEDGER: CC60 (HN/RESULTS-LEDGER.md:262-278; HN/results-ledger.json id "CC60").
%   Ledger status LEAN_VERIFIED is defined at HN/RESULTS-LEDGER.md:7 as a local

The directions are signed unit vectors in one closed arc of width $\pi/3$,
endpoints included. As $\Delta(K_i)$ is open, the exclusion says that
label $j$ occupies zero area of $\Delta(K_i)$.
% NOTE: the zero-area equivalence for an open set is a standard measure-theory
% fact, matching WALK:571 wording; it is not a separate Lean declaration.

\begin{corollary}[Not binary; \texttt{closed60\_\allowbreak{}exclusions\_\allowbreak{}not\_\allowbreak{}binary}]\label{cor:notbinary}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:cc60}, there are no labels
$a,b\in[k]$, equal or not, such that every residual label is $a$ or~$b$.
\end{corollary}
% SOURCE: CONE/ConeObstruction.lean:131-140 (hres : forall j, (forall i, j notin F i) -> j = a or j = b; conclusion False)
\noindent\emph{Status: checked in Lean 4.34.1 (archived logs).} In Lean the
corollary comes first; the theorem follows by choosing $a,b$ covering $R$
($a=b=c(x)$ if $R=\emptyset$). For six labels, two sets carrying disjoint
label pairs leave two labels, so their signed directions cannot lie in a
closed $60^\circ$ arc (an immediate specialisation, not a separate Lean
declaration).
% SOURCE: CONE/ConeObstruction.lean:158-174; WALK:575 (six-colour instance)

\subsection{Proof sketch}

The proof reduces CC60 to one open two-coloured region.

\begin{theorem}[OA120; \texttt{outer120\_\allowbreak{}binary\_\allowbreak{}impossible}]\label{thm:oa120}
Let $c$ be a measurable weak colouring, $a,b\in[k]$, and $W$ open with
$c(z)\in\{a,b\}$ for almost every $z\in W$. Suppose $W$ has \emph{outer
angular strips} about $x$ on $I=(\varphi-\pi/3,\varphi+\pi/3)$: for each
$\theta\in I$ there is $\delta>0$ with $x+se^{i\beta}\in W$ whenever
$|\beta-\theta|<\delta$ and $1<s<1+\delta$. This is impossible.
\end{theorem}
% SOURCE: CONE/ConeObstruction.lean:48-55; CONE/ConeGeometry.lean:9-12 (def OuterAngularStrips)
\noindent\emph{Status: proved (paper argument, independently reviewed);
checked in Lean 4.34.1 (archived logs).}
% SOURCE: OUT/ASTRA-CONE-REFEREE.txt:104-214; CONE/cone-audit-v3.log:14-17,37

\paragraph{Step 1: one pair on one open set.} Put $W=\bigcap_i\Delta(K_i)$,
open as $\iota$ is finite. Finitely many support exclusions hold off one
null set. So if $R\subseteq\{a,b\}$, then $c(z)\in\{a,b\}$ for almost every
$z\in W$, with one pair throughout $W$.
% SOURCE: CONE/ConeObstruction.lean:141-145; SRC/AngularBoundary.lean:317-327 (finite_support_exclusion_ae)

\paragraph{Step 2: the closed cone gives strips on an open $120^\circ$ arc.}
For $\theta\in I$, every $|\theta-\alpha_i|<\pi/2$, so
$\cos(\theta-\alpha_i)>0$; this holds at endpoint $\alpha_i$ because $I$ is
open. \texttt{outer\_\allowbreak{}strip} then gives $\delta_i>0$ such that points
$x+se'$, with unit $e'$ near $e^{i\theta}$ and $1<s<1+\delta_i$, lie in
$\Delta(K_i)$. Such points are farther than $1$ from $x\in K_i$ but closer
than $1$ to some $x+r_nw_n\in K_i$. The minimum of the finitely many
$\delta_i$ gives the strips of Theorem~\ref{thm:oa120}. Widths depend on
$\theta$ and on the $K_i$; no uniform width is claimed.
% SOURCE: CONE/ConeObstruction.lean:99-129 (closed60_continua_outer_strips); :90-97 (cone_exp_inner)
% SOURCE: SRC/AngularBoundary.lean:160-172 (approaching_direction_inside), :186-199 (outer_straddle_strip)
% SOURCE: OUT/MC120-ENDPOINT-CORRECTION.txt:22-25 (a universal width bound delta >= c<e,v> is false; not used here)

\paragraph{Step 3a: one radius on a closed $60^\circ$ subarc.} The arc
$J=[\varphi-\pi/6,\varphi+\pi/6]$ is compact in $I$. A finite subcover
gives one $r\in(1,2)$ with $x+re^{i\theta}\in W$ for all $\theta\in J$. The
arc $S=\{x+re^{i\theta}:\theta\in J\}$ is connected and lies in $W$.
% SOURCE: CONE/ConeGeometry.lean:135-152 (outerAngularStrips_compact_radius); CONE/ConeObstruction.lean:56-68

\paragraph{Step 3b: unit neighbours by rotation.} Let $p=x+re^{i\theta}\in S$
and $\gamma=\arccos(r/2)$. Then $0<\gamma<\pi/3$, strictly because $r>1$.
Rotate towards the axis: $\beta=\theta-\gamma$ if $\theta\ge\varphi$, else
$\beta=\theta+\gamma$. Then $\beta\in I$. As $x+e^{i\beta}$ need not lie in
$W$, take $q(s)=x+se^{i\beta(s)}$ with $\beta(s)=\theta\mp\arccos g(s)$ and
$g(s)=(r^2+s^2-1)/(2rs)$. The law of cosines gives $|p-q(s)|=1$, and for
$s>1$ near $1$, $q(s)$ lies in the strip at $\beta$.
% SOURCE: CONE/ConeGeometry.lean:107-133 (outer120_point_has_unit_neighbour; sign choice; arccos(r/2) < pi/3)
% SOURCE: CONE/ConeGeometry.lean:14-18, 31-33, 57-62, 76-105 (cosine law, coneRadialCos, coneNeighbourAngle_unit, outer_strip_actual_unit_neighbour)

\paragraph{Step 3c: rigidity spreads one colour over $S$.} At each $p\in S$,
openness of $W$ gives a second, non-parallel unit direction into $W$.
OpenAI's binary local rigidity, with $U=V=W$, then makes $c$ almost
everywhere constant near $p$. It uses four-edge unit walks from $q$ to $q+h$
whose labels must alternate between $a$ and $b$. As $S$ is connected, one
label $i$ is locally essentially constant at every point of $S$.
% SOURCE: CONE/ConeObstruction.lean:29-46 (binary_connected_unit_neighbour_obstruction)
% SOURCE: SRC/Rigidity.lean:177-206 (binary_open_local_rigidity, four-edge evenPath), :224-228 (open_two_unit_directions), :416-420 (connected_local_ae_constant)

\paragraph{Step 3d: a unit pair in $S$.} The ends of $S$ are $r>1$ apart:
a $60^\circ$ chord at radius $r$ has length $r$. The intermediate value
theorem on connected $S$ gives $p,q'\in S$ with $|p-q'|=1$, both of
essential colour $i$ nearby. A weak colouring separates almost every
translate of a fixed unit pair: contradiction.
% SOURCE: CONE/ConeObstruction.lean:72-88 (endpoints, cos(pi/3), intermediate_value); :9-27 (essentialLocalSet_no_unit_pair)
% SOURCE: SRC/Rigidity.lean:171-176 (ae_translated_unit_pair); SRC/WeakColoring.lean:73-74 (weak_all_directions)

\paragraph{Why a closed $60^\circ$ cone.} The width is used twice, without
slack. In Step~2, a cone of width $w$ leaves a common outer arc of width
$\pi-w$; $w=\pi/3$ gives exactly $2\pi/3$, and closed endpoints cost
nothing. In Step~3b, the central point $\theta=\varphi$ rotates to
$\varphi\mp\gamma$. This lies in $I$ only because $\gamma<\pi/3$, that is,
because $r>1$ strictly. Strip widths, hence $r-1$, may be arbitrarily small,
so $\gamma$ may approach $\pi/3$. With a wider cone the common outer arc $I$ would be shorter than $120^\circ$, and the rotated point could leave it.
Section~\ref{sec:cc60-sharp} shows that the theorem itself fails for every
wider cone.
% SOURCE: CONE/ConeGeometry.lean:107-108 (docstring: strict r>1 keeps the neighbour inside the OPEN 120 arc with closed cone endpoints)
% SOURCE: OUT/ASTRA-CONE-REFEREE.txt:100-102, 248-255; WALK:561 (arc of width 180 - w)
% NOTE: "rotation argument" is read here as the rotation by gamma = arccos(r/2)
% in Step 3b. "A shorter arc would break the argument" is a reading of the
% proof, not a theorem; the sharpness theorem is the rigorous statement.

\paragraph{Paper and Lean proofs.} The refereed paper proof uses a
$90^\circ$ subarc, an open sector and an explicit unit pair at angles
$\pm\arcsin(1/(2r_0))$. The Lean proof uses one radius on the closed
$60^\circ$ subarc and the intermediate value theorem. This works because the
source's connectedness lemma does not need $S$ to be open.
% SOURCE: OUT/ASTRA-CONE-REFEREE.txt:109-124, 194-214; CONE/REPORT.txt:112-122

\subsection{The equality case and a six-colour consumer}

\begin{corollary}[\texttt{cone\_\allowbreak{}survivor\_\allowbreak{}001334\_\allowbreak{}impossible}]\label{cor:survivor}
Let $c:\mathbb C\to[6]$ be a measurable weak colouring and $x\in\mathbb C$.
There are no sets $K_0,K_1,K_2$ with $\mathrm{AC}(x,K_0,1)$,
$\mathrm{AC}(x,K_1,1)$ and $\mathrm{AC}(x,K_2,e^{i\pi/3})$ such that the
spatial supports of the labels in $\{0,1\}$, $\{1,2\}$ and $\{2,3\}$ miss
$\Delta(K_0)$, $\Delta(K_1)$ and $\Delta(K_2)$ respectively.
\end{corollary}
% SOURCE: CONE/ConeSurvivor.lean:8-20 (angles ![0,0,pi/3], exclusions ![{0,1},{1,2},{2,3}], k = 6)
\noindent\emph{Status: checked in Lean 4.34.1 (archived logs); proved
(paper argument, independently reviewed).}
% SOURCE: CONE/cone-survivor-v4.log:1-4; CONE/cone-audit-v3.log:26-35,39; OUT/ASTRA-CONE-REFEREE.txt:17-18, 278-280

With $\varphi=\pi/6$, the directions $0,0,\pi/3$ sit at \emph{both}
endpoints of the closed arc: this is the equality case. The exclusions
leave $\{4,5\}$, so Corollary~\ref{cor:notbinary} applies. The sets are the
first three edges of the six-cycle with pairs $\{i,i+1\bmod6\}$ and
directions $q=(0,0,1,3,3,4)$ in sixth turns. The consumer is conditional:
nothing shows that a weak six-colouring produces such data.

\subsection{Sharpness above $60^\circ$}
\label{sec:cc60-sharp}

\begin{theorem}[Generic sharpness]\label{thm:sharp}
For every $\Delta\in(\pi/3,\pi)$ there are $\rho\in(0,1/2)$, segments
$K_\pm=[0,\rho e^{\pm i\Delta/2}]$ and a Borel, pointwise proper colouring
$c:\mathbb C\to[20]$ such that $\mathrm{AC}(0,K_\pm,e^{\pm i\Delta/2})$ holds,
the supports of the labels in $F_+=\{2,\dots,10\}$ miss $\Delta(K_+)$, those
in $F_-=\{11,\dots,19\}$ miss $\Delta(K_-)$, and the residual is exactly
$\{0,1\}$. Hence, for every $A>\pi/3$, CC60 fails for cones of width $A$,
already with $k=20$.
\end{theorem}
% SOURCE: SCALE/ULTRA-SHARPNESS-REFEREE.txt:15-39 (exact verified statement)
\noindent\emph{Status: proved (paper argument, independently reviewed). Not
checked in Lean.}
% SOURCE: SCALE/ULTRA-SHARPNESS-REFEREE.txt:4-8; SCALE/ULTRA-CONE-NOVELTY-AUDIT.txt:173-177 (construction first derived there); HN/PROGRESS.md:15
% LEDGER: SHARPNESS-20 (HN/RESULTS-LEDGER.md:408-421; HN/results-ledger.json id "SHARPNESS-20").
%   Status PROVED_PAPER_REVIEWED; 20 colours; not a six-colour statement; six-colour
%   sharpness and novelty priority not established. Artifact paths agree with SCALE above.

\emph{Construction.} $W=\Delta(K_+)\cap\Delta(K_-)$ lies in the band
$1-\rho<|z|<1+\rho$, in an outer horn around angle $0$ of half-width
$a=(\pi-\Delta)/2<\pi/3$ and an inner horn around $\pi$ of half-width
$a+\arcsin(\rho/(2(1-\rho)))$. For small $\rho$, each half-horn has diameter
below one and opposite horns are over one apart, so $c=0$ for
$\operatorname{Im}z\ge0$ and $c=1$ otherwise colours $W$ properly. Two
disjoint copies of a proper nine-colouring by squares of side $3/5$ colour
the rest: labels $2$--$10$ off $\Delta(K_+)$, labels $11$--$19$ on
$\Delta(K_+)\setminus\Delta(K_-)$. Each excluded class lies in a closed set
missing the relevant open straddle region, hence so does its support.
% SOURCE: SCALE/ULTRA-SHARPNESS-REFEREE.txt:41-85 (parameters, horns, inequalities (I),(II)), :124-151 (colouring), :153-168 (supports, AC data)

This mirrors the proof: the common outer arc has width $\pi-\Delta<2\pi/3$,
and bisecting it gives exactly the two-colouring that
Theorem~\ref{thm:oa120} forbids at full width. The theorem gives no
counterexample for each $k$. \textbf{Six-colour sharpness is not
established}, the interface-extraction hypotheses are not supplied, and
\textbf{novelty priority is not established}.
