The open-arc outer-strip hypothesis alone DOES NOT make the 120-degree monochromatic cone endpoint valid.
Let A=(sqrt(3)/2,-1/2), B=(sqrt(3)/2,1/2), C=(sqrt(3),0). They form a unit equilateral triangle.
Let U=B(A,1) intersect B(B,1) intersect B(C,1), using OPEN balls, and W=U intersect {z:|z|>1}.
The closed Reuleaux triangle has diameter1, and every pair in its interior is strictly closer than1. For the strict statement, extend the segment between any hypothetical unit-separated interior pair slightly within the open interior, contradicting diameter1. Thus W has no unit pair.
For every theta in(-pi/6,pi/6), e(theta) lies in U:
 |e(theta)-A|²=2-2cos(theta+pi/6)<1,
 |e(theta)-B|²=2-2cos(theta-pi/6)<1,
 |e(theta)-C|²=4-2sqrt(3)cos(theta)<1.
Openness and continuity therefore supply a JOINT angular/radial neighbourhood with all points r e(beta), 1<r<1+delta, |beta-theta|<delta, in W.
Consequently W has actual outer angular strips on an open60-degree arc, yet is unit-distance independent. It can be monochromatic.
This is a counterexample to the proposed outer-strip-only endpoint argument. It is NOT a counterexample to a stronger theorem using additional actual continuum/support/colouring constraints.
