The two accepted conclusions are:

1. EXACT AUDITED LIST DATA AND DOMAIN

Use e(t)=exp(it), s=sqrt(3)/2, and
  A0={1,4}, A1={2,5}, A2={0,3},
  P(t)=A_(floor((t+pi/6)/(pi/3)) mod3).
The sector of index m is ((m-1/2)pi/3,(m+1/2)pi/3).

Let D be 0<|z|<2 with the six circles
  |z-e(pi/6+k*pi/3)|=1
removed. The unordered unit-vector decomposition z=e(a)+e(b) is unique
for 0<|z|<2. Its vectors are distinct and nonantipodal. A vector on a
sector boundary occurs exactly on one of the removed circles, because
then the other summand is the unit vector z-e(boundary). Therefore every
decomposition used in D is strictly inside two sectors and has
sin(b-a) nonzero. This verifies the domain and Jacobian requirements.

For H=e((pi/6,pi/2))+e((pi/2,5pi/6)), the two sector palettes are A1,A2,
so its residual list is A0. Under R(z)=e(pi/3)z, sectors shift by +1;
hence H1=R(H) has list A1. Under S(z)=-conj(z), sector index m becomes
3-m modulo6; since S(H)=H and SR=R^(-1)S,
  H2=S(H1)=R^(-1)(H)
has list A2. This is the reflected H copy, not an incorrect use of
the same list after reflection.

For z0=i/2, put a=arcsin(1/4) in (0,pi/6). Then
  z0=e(a)+e(pi-a).
The two sectors are 0 and3, both with palette A0, giving the four-list
  L(z0)=A1 union A2.
Its six distances to boundary centres are among 1/2, sqrt(3)/2,
sqrt(7)/2, 3/2; none equals1. Thus the central vertex has strict margin.

2. H IS EXACTLY THE CLAIMED OPEN REGION

With C=(0,1), A=(s,1/2), and Dleft=(-s,1/2), define
  g(u)=1/2+sqrt(1-(s-u)^2) for 0<=u<s,
  U(u)=1+sqrt(1-u^2) for |u|<s.
The exact formula is
  H={(u,v): |u|<s, g(|u|)<v<U(u)}.                 (H)

The forward inequalities in A5 are correct. For a,b in the specified
arcs, |u|<s and 1<v<2. After rotation by -pi/2, put
delta=(b-a)/2 and m=(a+b)/2-pi/2. Then |m|<delta<pi/3 and
  |e(a-pi/2)+e(b-pi/2)-1|^2-1
    =4 cos(delta)(cos(delta)-cos(m))<0.
This proves |q-C|<1. The chord e(a)-A has argument between 2pi/3 and
5pi/6 and has positive scalar product with e(b), so |q-A|>1.
Reflection supplies |q-Dleft|>1. For u>=0 the first two circle
inequalities give the upper and lower bounds in (H); reflect for u<0.

I separately checked the converse, since one-way circle inequalities
would not suffice for the contact and connectivity argument. Suppose
0<=u<s and g(u)<v<U(u), and write q=r e(theta). Here
pi/6<theta<=pi/2 and 0<r<2. With alpha=acos(r/2),
  |q-C|<1 implies alpha>pi/2-theta,
  |q-A|>1 implies alpha<theta-pi/6.
The inequalities follow respectively by comparing cos(alpha) with
sin(theta) and cos(theta-pi/6); all comparison angles lie in the
monotonicity interval of cosine. They imply
  pi/6<theta-alpha<pi/2<theta+alpha<5pi/6.
For the last inequality, alpha<theta-pi/6 and theta<=pi/2 give
theta+alpha<2theta-pi/6<=5pi/6. Thus q is in H. Reflection completes
the converse. No omitted additional component is present.

For every |u|<s, g(|u|)<3/2<U(u). Vertical movement to height3/2,
followed by horizontal movement there, proves H path connected.
The strict continuous inequalities prove openness.

3. THE ACTIVE LOBES ARE CONNECTED AND HAVE UNIT NEIGHBOURS

Set Qplus=(s,3/2), Qminus=(-s,3/2), both in closure(H), and
  Bplus={p=(u,v) in H: u>0, |p-Qminus|>1},
  Bminus=S(Bplus).
These definitions need only be sufficient active regions. No claim
that every remaining point of H is inactive or flexible is used.

Let d=1-s, so 0<d<s. For 0<u<d, define
  ell(u)=3/2-sqrt(1-(u+s)^2),
  h(u)=3/2+sqrt(1-(u+s)^2).
The vertical section of Bplus is exactly
  (g(u),ell(u)) union (h(u),U(u)).
Both intervals are nonempty. The function f(t)=t/sqrt(1-t^2) is
strictly increasing on (0,1), and direct differentiation gives
  (ell-g)'=f(u+s)-f(s-u)>0,
  (U-h)'=f(u+s)-f(u)>0.
Their differences start from zero at u=0. These derivative signs and
the endpoint values were independently checked.

For d<u<s the complete interval (g(u),U(u)) is active, because u+s>1.
It is path connected through height3/2. At u=d, only the point of
height3/2 is removed. The two left horns really connect to the right
region: hold fixed 0<lambda<1 in
  v(u)=g(u)+lambda(ell(u)-g(u))
or
  v(u)=h(u)+lambda(U(u)-h(u)),
and increase u to d. At d the lower path ends strictly below3/2,
and the upper path strictly above3/2. Both endpoints remain inside
Bplus and admit a short horizontal step to u>d by openness. This
proves path connectedness without crossing the omitted tangent point.
Reflection proves the corresponding fact for Bminus.

For p in Bplus, approximation of Qminus by points q in H gives
|p-q|>1. A path in H from p to q starts with distance0 from p and
ends above1, so it contains an actual point at distance1. The same
argument applies to Bminus. The unit neighbour lies in the open H,
not merely its closure. Openness then supplies a second nonparallel
unit direction, exactly as in Rigidity.open_two_unit_directions.

4. FINITE EVEN WALKS: THE EXISTENCE CLAIM IS JUSTIFIED

The general geometric lemma in A5 is correct: if W is open and each
point of connected T subset W has a unit neighbour in W, any two
points of T are joined by a finite even unit walk lying in W.

At p choose nonparallel unit v,w landing in W. For small h write
  h=a*i*v+b*i*w.
The coefficients are continuous linear functions of h. With
  E(v,a)=(sqrt(1-a^2/4)+i*a/2)v,
the source identities give unit lengths for both wings when |a|<2.
The points
  p, p+E(v,a), p+a*i*v,
  p+a*i*v+E(w,b), p+h
therefore form a four-edge unit walk. At h=0 they are
  p, p+v, p, p+w, p,
all inside W. Continuity keeps the entire finite walk inside W for
all sufficiently small h, exactly matching evenPath_local/edges.

The relation used is reachability by ACTUAL FINITE even walks. It is
an equivalence relation, and the preceding construction makes every
class relatively open in T. The complement of a class is a union of
other relatively open classes. Connectedness therefore leaves one
class. This does not replace a finite walk by a limiting path or infer
finiteness from metric approximation. Finiteness is built into the
equivalence relation and survives concatenation and reversal.

The anchors aplus=(1/2,3/2), aminus=(-1/2,3/2) are in the respective
active lobes: (H) gives H membership and s+1/2>1 gives activity.
Their distance is1. Joining each active endpoint to its anchor by an
even walk therefore produces an odd H walk between opposite lobes.

5. EXACT CONTACT ARITHMETIC AND LIST ROTATIONS

Let zprime=R^(-1)z0=(s/2,1/4), and
  qminus=(-sqrt(3)/24,(6+sqrt(429))/24),
  qplus=(sqrt(3)/12,(3+2sqrt(33))/12).
Write their heights vm,vp. Exact squares show
  |qminus-zprime|^2=(147+429)/576=1,
  |qplus-zprime|^2=(3+33)/36=1.
The rational radical bounds imply
  1<vm<9/8, 1<vp<5/4.

The identity used to audit every circle test is
  u^2+v^2=s*u+v/2+3/4,
obtained by expanding either unit-distance equation. For qminus,
s*u=-1/16, and the three quantities |q-centre|^2-1 at C,A,Dleft are
  11/16-3vm/2, 13/16-vm/2, 9/16-vm/2,
with signs negative, positive, positive. Its active quantity is
  |qminus-Qplus|^2-1=(5/2)(9/8-vm)>0.
For qplus, s*u=1/8, the corresponding three quantities are
  7/8-3vp/2, 5/8-vp/2, 9/8-vp/2,
again negative, positive, positive, and
  |qplus-Qminus|^2-1=(5/2)(5/4-vp)>0.
All formulas and signs check exactly. Together with |u|<s and v>1,
the exact region formula proves qminus in Bminus and qplus in Bplus.
No rounded numerical position or boundary membership is being used.

An odd H walk Gamma joins these two contacts. R(Gamma) has constant
two-list A1. S(R(Gamma)) has constant two-list A2. Since R(zprime)=z0
and S(z0)=z0, every endpoint of the two walks is at distance1 from z0.

6. FINITE LIST CONTRADICTION AND ITS CERTIFICATE LIMIT

Take the finite set F consisting of z0 and the vertices of the two
walks. In any proper list colouring, colours alternate on the first
odd walk and its endpoints use both members of A1. Both are unit
neighbours of z0. The second odd walk similarly excludes both members
of A2. The central list is exactly A1 union A2, contradiction.

Repeated vertices cause no problem: any proper colouring of their
union restricts to a colouring along each walk. Extra unit edges only
add restrictions. The two H copies have different constant residual
lists and cannot overlap, by the uniqueness of the unit decomposition.
Every chosen vertex belongs to D. Because F is finite, its minimum
distance-margin expression from the removed boundaries is positive.

ACCEPTED: a finite configuration exists with strict list-cell margins.
NOT SUPPLIED: a chosen vertex count, complete coordinates, an enumerated
edge set, or a numerical lower bound for those margins. The author
states this distinction clearly. It is not a mathematical gap in the
finite-existence theorem, but it remains necessary if a fully explicit
machine-checkable finite certificate is wanted.

7. THE ACTUAL PALETTE/DENSITY CONSUMER IS VALID

Assume measurable c:C->Fin6 and WeakColoring6, and at one centre x
assume P(t) is contained in its ACTUAL palette for angular-a.e. t.
For each relevant open sector, its label pair defines RealState:
the sector is measurable, and its real lift is a.e. contained in
each relevant liftPalette. The angle quotient's quasi-measure-
preserving map transfers the given angular a.e. statement to real
angles. At every interior angle the sector complement has line
density zero. The sector endpoints themselves are not used.

For y=e(a)+e(b) in D, States.states_densityOne therefore gives
  DensityOne {q:c(q) in L(y)} at x+y.
The source theorem uses exactly the union of the two represented
label sets; it does not require that either be the entire actual
palette. Nonparallelity was checked in section1. Thus the assertion
holds at EVERY vertex of F, even if the original palette inclusion
fails at particular point representatives of a null exceptional set.
The RealState plus interior-density premise handles that issue.

Apply States.finite_density_realization to the vertices x+F with
their fixed lists L. Its source proof takes a finite intersection of
translated density-one restrictions and the conull properness
statements for all unit pairs in that finite set. It gives an actual
proper list colouring of F, contradicting section6.

This is not an unjustified invocation of pointwise proper colouring
from the product-a.e. definition of WeakColoring. The source theorem
explicitly invokes weak_all_directions at the finitely many fixed
unit directions and then intersects translated conull statements.
It is also not a selection of one exceptional-set complement over
uncountably many centres: the centre x is fixed, then F is finite.

8. THE ALTERNATIVE ESSENTIAL-RIGIDITY PROOF ALSO CHECKS

The pointwise density-one pair restriction on each open H copy implies
an a.e. pair restriction there. Indeed ae_density_actual gives density
one of the actual colour at almost every point. If that colour were
outside the pair, two disjoint measurable sets would both have density
one at that point; DensityOne.inter and measure_ne_zero exclude this.

At each active point there are two genuine nonparallel unit neighbours
inside the same open H copy. Apply binary_open_local_rigidity with
U=V equal to that full H copy, and its same fixed pair restriction.
The neighbours need not belong to the active lobe itself. The lemma
produces ambient essential constancy at every active point. Connected
local constancy gives one essential label per lobe; openness and the
a.e. pair restriction force that label into the pair. The anchor unit
edge forces the two labels to differ, so the lobes exhaust the pair.

The four contact points consequently have essential neighborhoods
carrying the four distinct labels of A1 union A2. For each fixed unit
contact, ae_translated_unit_pair excludes its label almost everywhere
on a sufficiently small ball about z0. The minimum of four positive
radii excludes the entire central list on a ball, contradicting its
density-one restriction. No local constancy is assumed on H's inactive
core, and no a.e. fact is evaluated at a single unlicensed point.

FINAL SCOPE

Both the purely geometric finite-existence proof and the independent
essential-rigidity reading are sound. The finite-existence route is
particularly direct for the source bridge and makes no regularity
assumption on a hypothetical list colouring.

No remaining mathematical gap was identified in the stated A5 theorem.
The separate producer from common-interface hypotheses to the canonical
P remains unverified by this audit. Consequently this report licenses
the exact canonical-subpalette exclusion and finite-obstruction
existence, and no broader interface or global colouring conclusion.
