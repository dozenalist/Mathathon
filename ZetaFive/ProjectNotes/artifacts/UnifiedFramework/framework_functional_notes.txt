FUNCTIONAL FRAMEWORK: EULER--MACLAURIN, HURWITZ ZETA, AND A RESIDUE DEFORMATION
Research notes for the parent agent, 14 November 2026.

Scope. These are exact reorganizations and derived identities for the functionals in
Fauzan's 17 September 2026 manuscript. They do not replace the difficult arithmetic
or potential-theoretic estimates. xi denotes zeta(5). References to the paper use
its equation numbers. The strongest three organizing objects are:

  (i) the Euler--Maclaurin symbol u^4/[24(e^u-1)];
 (ii) its Hurwitz-zeta remainder Phi(a), a Stieltjes transform after a -> sqrt(z);
(iii) the one-dimensional residue-at-infinity deformation that introduces X.

1. THE ONE EULER--MACLAURIN FUNCTIONAL

Let D=d/dx. On polynomials define

  T(P) = [ D^4/(24(e^D-1)) P ](0)
       = (1/24) [ (D/(e^D-1)) P''' ](0).

Here the operator is a formal power series that terminates when applied to a
polynomial. This is not inversion of an operator on arbitrary rational functions.
The Bernoulli generating identity u/(e^u-1)=sum B_k u^k/k! gives

  T(x^d)=d(d-1)(d-2) B_(d-3)/24   (d>=3),
  T(1)=T(x)=T(x^2)=0.

This is exactly the manuscript's tau; the Bernoulli functional L is merely the
intermediate operator [D/(e^D-1)] at zero. Its complete exponential symbol is

  T(e^(ux)) = u^4/[24(e^u-1)]  (formal generating identity).

Therefore the key translation relation is structural:

  T(P(x+1)-P(x)) = P^(4)(0)/24.

Multiplication by e^D-1 cancels the summation denominator. This explains why the
fourth derivative appears, and why fifth harmonic sums appear when evaluating a
simple pole. It also explains the Bernoulli multiplication/distribution formula.

The reflection relation follows from the symbol. Write A(u)=u^4/[24(e^u-1)].
Then exp(-u) A(-u)=-A(u), hence T(P(-1-x))=-T(P(x)).

2. AN AXIOMATIC DEFINITION THAT REMOVES THE DUPLICATE FORMULAS

Let R_Z consist of rational functions whose polynomial part is arbitrary and whose
poles are simple and at integers. There is exactly one Q-linear functional T_X
with values in Q[X] satisfying:

  T_X(g(x+1)-g(x)) = g^(4)(0)/24       if g is regular at zero,
  T_X(g(-1-x)) = -T_X(g(x)),
  T_X(1/x) = -X.

Proof of uniqueness and recovery of the paper's formulas:

* On polynomials, the forward-difference map is surjective. Thus the first identity
  fixes T_X(P) for every polynomial P; the Euler--Maclaurin operator above provides
  the solution.
* Put a_r=T_X(1/(x-r)). For r!=0, apply the difference relation to 1/(x-r):
      a_(r-1)-a_r = -r^(-5).
  For positive r this gives a_r=-X+H_r^(5).
* Reflection gives a_(-r-1)=a_r. Thus for negative r,
      a_r=H_(-r-1)^(5)-X.

Consequently the paper's polynomial and pole definitions are forced by a single
Euler summation rule and one boundary constant X. Conversely those formulas satisfy
all three axioms, so existence is immediate by partial fractions. The restriction
"g regular at zero" matters: otherwise the derivative defect is undefined and the
two half-lattices would be improperly joined across the pole.

This is an accurate sense in which X is the single free constant in the singular
extension of the Euler--Maclaurin functional. It is not a claim that the formal
infinite differential operator converges on every rational function.

3. THE REAL REALIZATION IS AN ABEL--PLANA BOUNDARY FUNCTIONAL

Let f(y)=1/(e^(2*pi*y)-1). For a real rational function g, polynomial part allowed,
with simple nonzero integer poles (so g is regular at zero), define

  A(g) = [g'''(0)-g''''(0)/2]/24
         - (i/24) integral_0^infinity
           [g''''(iy)-g''''(-iy)] f(y) dy.

This integral is absolutely convergent. Near zero the bracket is O(y), canceling
f(y)=O(1/y); at infinity exponential decay dominates polynomial growth. It is real
for real g. On this domain one has the exact identity

  A(g)=T_xi(g), xi=zeta(5).

Verification on a basis:

* On polynomials, Euler's formula for the even zeta values shows
      L(P)=P(0)-P'(0)/2
           -i integral [P'(iy)-P'(-iy)] f(y)dy.
  Apply this to P=g''' and divide by 24. Thus A agrees with the Bernoulli moments.
* Hermite's Hurwitz-zeta integral gives, for j>0,
      A(1/(x+j))=-zeta(5,j)=H_(j-1)^(5)-xi,
      A(1/(x-j))=-zeta(5,j+1)=H_j^(5)-xi.
  In the second identity the sign in the fourth derivative at zero changes, giving
  the difference of 1/j^5 between the two values.

No claim is made that this convergent integral evaluates 1/x: that function is
singular at the contour endpoint. Its value -xi is the specified extension datum.

Now put g(x)=x^5 R(-x^2), for R in the manuscript's rational-function domain, and
q(y)=y^5 R(y^2). This g vanishes to order at least five at zero. Since

  g''''(iy)=i q''''(y),   g''''(-iy)=-i q''''(y),

the boundary terms vanish and

  T_xi(x^5 R(-x^2)) = (1/12) integral q''''(y) f(y)dy
                    = (1/12) integral q(y) f''''(y)dy
                    = integral R(y^2) w(y)dy,

where

  w(y)=y^5 f''''(y)/12
      =(2*pi)^4 y^5/12 sum_(ell>=1) ell^4 e^(-2*pi*ell*y)>0.

All four integrations by parts are justified: every boundary product at zero is
O(y), and at infinity is exponentially small. This derives the positive weight
directly from the same Euler--Maclaurin/Abel--Plana kernel as the arithmetic
functional. The paper's weighted pullback mu_X(R)=T_X(x^5 R(-x^2)) is the bridge.

4. ONE HURWITZ-ZETA REMAINDER ENCODES BOTH KINDS OF mu VALUES

Define, for a>0,

  Phi(a)=a^4 zeta(5,a)-1/(2a)-1/4.

Equivalently, zeta(5,a)=-psi^(4)(a)/24, so Phi is a renormalized fourth
polygamma function. The subtraction removes the first two Euler--Maclaurin terms.
Hermite's integral and four integrations by parts give

  Phi(a)=integral_0^infinity w(y)/(a^2+y^2)dy.

Thus S(z)=Phi(sqrt(z)) is a positive Stieltjes transform:

  S(z)=integral_0^infinity dnu(t)/(z+t),
  dnu(t)=w(sqrt(t))/(2*sqrt(t))dt.

Its two manifestations are exactly the two clauses defining mu:

* At positive integers a=j, the Hurwitz recurrence gives
      Phi(j)=j^4(xi-H_j^(5))-1/4+1/(2j).
  Replacing xi with X is the paper's simple-pole value.
* Euler--Maclaurin at large a gives
      Phi(a) ~ sum_(e>=0) (-1)^e mu(t^e) a^(-2e-2),
  with
      mu(t^e)=(-1)^e B_(2e+2)(2e+3)(2e+4)(2e+5)/24.

The coefficients and integer samples come from ONE function. This seems the
clearest explanation of the previously disconnected definitions.

The expansion is asymptotic, generally divergent over R, not a convergent identity.
There is a particularly transparent exact remainder from the Stieltjes formula:

  Phi(a)=sum_(e=0)^(m-1) (-1)^e mu(t^e)/a^(2e+2)
       + (-1)^m a^(-2m) integral y^(2m)w(y)/(a^2+y^2)dy.

The remainder has alternating sign and absolute value at most
mu(t^m)/a^(2m+2). This is a rigorous Euler--Maclaurin interpretation without invoking
an infinite divergent sum as an equality.

5. THE SAME FORMAL SERIES HAS A p-ADIC REALIZATION

For a variable a, the manuscript's far-pole series (3.5), after s=-a, is

  T^an(1/(x+a))
    = -(1/4) sum_(k>=0) (-1)^k binom(k+3,3) B_k a^(-k-4).

This is exactly the Euler--Maclaurin Laurent expansion of -zeta(5,a):

  -1/(4a^4)-1/(2a^5)-(5/12)a^(-6)+(7/24)a^(-8)-...

Over the reals it is an asymptotic expansion at infinity. Over Q_p it converges
when |a|_p>1, because the Bernoulli valuations are bounded below and powers of
a^(-1) tend to zero. Therefore the real functional and the p-adic analytic
functional are different realizations of the same formal Euler summation data.
They are not literally values in one common numerical field.

The residue-class distribution formula is also forced by the Euler symbol:

  p^(-4) sum_(a=0)^(p-1) T(e^(u(a+px)))
  =p^(-4) [sum exp(au)] (pu)^4/[24(e^(pu)-1)]
  =u^4/[24(e^u-1)].

For singular functions the extension parameter changes to Y=p^5 X+C_p. The
factor p^5 has two sources: the p^(-4) operator scaling and an additional p^(-1)
from rescaling a simple pole. The additive C_p is the sum of the analytic far-pole
contributions. This is a forced change of boundary datum, not an unrelated new
mapping or an arbitrary adjustment.

6. THE X-PARAMETER IS A RESIDUE AT INFINITY

For g=P+sum c_r/(x-r), directly from the definition,

  T_X(g)=T_0(g)-X sum c_r
        =T_xi(g)-(X-xi)[x^(-1)]g.

Here [x^(-1)] means the coefficient in the Laurent expansion at infinity. The
first equality uses T_0 and rational coefficients; the second is the useful real
interpretation. Therefore X changes one residue functional only.

For R=P+sum c_j/(t+j^2),

  mu_X(R)=mu_0(R)+X sum j^4 c_j
         =mu_xi(R)+(X-xi)[t^(-3)]R.

Indeed 1/(t+j^2)=t^(-1)-j^2 t^(-2)+j^4 t^(-3)-..., and polynomial parts do not
contribute. Equivalently [t^(-3)]R=-Res_(t=infinity)(t^2 R(t)dt).

This has a useful consequence for the determinant construction. Set

  D=D_tail, W=D_N^5, B=t^2 W, and E=Q[t]/(D).

Then the coefficient of X in the Gram bilinear form is

  beta(p,q)=[t^(-1)] B(t)p(t)q(t)/D(t)
           =sum_(D(r)=0) B(r)p(r)q(r)/D'(r).

This pairing descends to E since changing p by a multiple of D adds a polynomial
inside the coefficient extraction. It is the standard residue pairing with a
unit multiplier B. Since D has simple roots -j^2 for N<j<=K, and B is nonzero
at all those roots, beta is nondegenerate. In the monomial basis, its determinant
is

  (-1)^(h(h-1)/2) product_(N<j<=K) j^4 D_N(-j^2)^5.

This recovers (2.9) as a residue-pairing/resultant formula. It also identifies the
matrix M_K of Appendix C as the same pairing. This observation was sent to the
determinant subagent for integration with the other matrix constructions.

7. AN OPTIONAL SINGLE REGULARIZED HALF-LATTICE SUM

There is a rigorous way to express the full real functional by one shifted zeta
sum, including its polynomial part. This makes the relation to classical
summation particularly explicit.

For g in R_Z, define

  Z_g(z)=sum_(n>=0)^(zeta-regularized) g'''(n+z).

This notation has a precise definition using the unique polynomial-plus-simple-
partial-fraction decomposition of g. Sum a monomial (n+z)^k as zeta(-k,z), and sum
the third derivative of each proper pole term by its absolutely convergent
Hurwitz series, continued meromorphically in z. More explicitly:

  If g(x)=P(x)+sum_r c_r/(x-r), and P'''(x)=sum_k a_k x^k, then
  Z_g(z)=sum_k a_k zeta(-k,z)-6 sum_r c_r zeta(4,z-r).

Then

  T_xi(g)=-(1/24) FP_(z=0) Z_g'(z).

Here FP means the constant Laurent coefficient after removing polar terms.
The verification uses only standard Hurwitz identities:

* For g=x^d, d>=3,
    Z_g=d(d-1)(d-2) zeta(3-d,z).
  Since zeta(-k,z)=-B_(k+1)(z)/(k+1), its derivative is -B_k(z), giving exactly
  T(x^d)=d(d-1)(d-2)B_(d-3)/24.
* For g=1/(x-r), Z_g=-6 zeta(4,z-r), so Z_g'=24 zeta(5,z-r).
  For r>=0,
    zeta(5,z-r)=z^(-5)+zeta(5,z+1)+sum_(v=1)^r (z-v)^(-5),
  with finite part xi-H_r^(5). For r=-j<0 the value is zeta(5,j).
  Negating gives precisely H_(d(r))^(5)-xi.

This prescription also handles the endpoint pole r=0, which the convergent
Abel--Plana boundary integral in section 3 does not directly handle.

On proper rational functions, it reduces to the elementary finite-part lattice
formula

  T_xi(g)=-(1/24) sum_(n>=0) FP_(x=n) g''''(x),

where the finite part at each actual simple pole discards the single fifth-order
polar term and retains contributions from all other terms of g. The sum converges.
For a single pole at r>=0 this is the sum with n=r omitted.

Crucial point: the derivative in FP Z_g'(z) is taken AFTER zeta regularization.
For g=x^3, Z_g(z)=6 zeta(0,z)=3-6z, so T(x^3)=1/4. If one differentiated before
regularization, one would get g''''=0 and incorrectly lose this term. Thus the
boundary anomaly is encoded canonically by the shifted Hurwitz continuation.

The manuscript's X-extension can consequently be described as replacing the one
Hurwitz finite-part constant xi by the indeterminate X; all Bernoulli polynomial
terms and finite harmonic sums remain rational. This regularized half-lattice
view is exact, but it is more machinery than the elementary axiomatic definition
in section 2. It is best used to explain the origin, not necessarily to make the
core proof depend on regularization terminology.

8. THE EXTENT AND LIMITS OF THE SIMPLIFICATION

The functional family can be presented with one definition, one free constant,
and two realizations:

  Euler--Maclaurin symbol -> rational extension T_X
     -> weighted pullback mu_X
     -> real positive Stieltjes realization at X=xi
     -> p-adic convergent Laurent realization and local distribution.

The Bernoulli numbers, fifth harmonic sums, positive weight, and far-pole series
are consequences of this framework. They need not appear as independent ideas.

The fourth derivative and the x^5 prefactor are dictated by the desired value
zeta(5), and the positive weight comes from four integrations by parts. Similar
functionals exist for every odd s>=3:

  T_s(P)=[D^(s-1)/((s-1)!(e^D-1)) P](0),
  T_(s,X)(1/(x-r))=H_(d(r))^(s)-X,
  mu_(s,X)(R)=T_(s,X)(x^s R(-x^2)),
  w_s(y)=2 y^s f^(s-1)(y)/(s-1)! >0.

This explains common odd-zeta constructions but does not make their local
denominator bounds or final numerical inequalities identical. The fourth-order
arithmetic cancellations in the source remain substantive.

Do not present T as an ordinary real sum -sum g''''(n)/24 on the full rational
domain. This happens to hold for decaying functions with poles only on the
negative side, but fails already for g=x^3: the proposed sum would be zero,
whereas T(x^3)=1/4. One must retain the Euler--Maclaurin boundary term or specify
a suitable regularization. Similarly, the formal differential operator should
not be treated as convergent at rational functions with nearby poles.
