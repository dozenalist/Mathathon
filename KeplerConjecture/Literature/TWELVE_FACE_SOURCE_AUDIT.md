# Twelve-face premise: source and scope

Checked November 14, 2026, 07:38 Pacific.

Hales and McLaughlin, *The dodecahedral conjecture*, author manuscript,
Section 4, Theorem 4.1, printed/PDF page 16 (zero-based page 15), gives a
volume lower bound for a polyhedron with at most twelve faces containing a
unit sphere, even after concentric truncation at the dodecahedral circumradius.
The bound is the regular dodecahedron's unit-inradius volume; equality is rigid.
The surrounding text attributes this to Fejes Toth, *Regular Figures*,
Sections 33 and 41, including the note on page 265.
[Primary author manuscript](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~seanmcl/papers/McLaughlin-Dodec.pdf).

This is the appropriate source for E's contracted-polyhedron premise; it does
not require that the contracted constraints arise from a legal sphere packing.
Calling the premise simply “Lindelof 1869” obscures the needed theorem. It does
not validate the remaining ring-face area estimates, simultaneous movement
argument or numeric rounding. Those are separate project claims under review.
It does not assert the same bound for arbitrary polyhedra with more faces.
