INDEPENDENT REFEREE: CLAUDE'S VARIABLE-TRIANGLE OBSTRUCTION
14 November 2026

VERDICT

ACCEPT the main geometric theorem, including its stronger closure version,
for the SPECIFIED half-open four-state angular palette. I give a complete
analytic proof below using conservative rational bounds; the large proposed
grid is unnecessary and was not run.

Several subsidiary statements need correction:
  * the unrestricted oriented-difference list omits (270 degrees,330 degrees);
  * the exact inner-spike condition is half-open, not a strict absolute-value
    inequality;
  * the half-open good set itself need not be reflection-invariant on its
    boundary, though its closure is;
  * arbitrary null-set changes can create isolated pointwise good triangles.
    They cannot create the density-point/open-margin data needed by this
    argument. The raw closure theorem is representative-specific;
  * using different base centres does not require a new general density
    lemma: the existing source already permits the three density arguments
    to have different centres. What is missing is a producer of compatible
    actual state data and triangle geometry across those centres.

No plane-colouring theorem or numerical chromatic bound follows. This is
an obstruction to a particular fixed-base, partner-pair triangle method.

1. EXACT OBJECT BEING AUDITED

I read both outputs/CLAUDE-VARIABLE-TRIANGLE-2026-11-14.txt and the preceding
outputs/CLAUDE-FIRST-RESEARCH-2026-11-14.txt to recover the actual palette.
Put h=pi/6 and
  f(t)=floor(t/h) modulo 4,
  P_0={0,3}, P_1={0,4}, P_2={1,4}, P_3={1,3}.
The rule is well-defined on the angular circle because 2pi=12h.
Two palette values are disjoint exactly when their indices differ by 2
modulo 4. Both partner pairs have union {0,1,3,4}, leaving {2,5}.

For base centre zero, let G be the set of z=e(a)+e(b) for which P(a),P(b)
are partners, using exactly these half-open fibres. For every nonzero z
with |z|<=2, its unordered pair of unit summands is unique:
  a=psi-gamma, b=psi+gamma,
  psi=arg(z), gamma=arccos(|z|/2) in [0,pi/2).
For |z|>2 there is no such pair. At z=0 antipodal summands are not unique;
they are all partners for this word. Including zero is conservative,
since these antipodal pairs fail the nonparallelism needed by the source
density lemma.

The theorem is: no triple of points in closure(G) has all three pairwise
distances equal to one. It allows EVERY translation and rotation of the
unit equilateral triangle relative to this single fixed base centre.

2. GOOD-SET DERIVATION AND BOUNDARY CORRECTIONS

Use a representative psi in [n h,(n+1)h) and set
  delta=psi-(n+1/2)h, so -h/2<=delta<h/2.
The slot-index difference is
  N=floor((psi+gamma)/h)-floor((psi-gamma)/h).
For 0<|z|<=2, one has 0<=N<=6. Goodness means N=2 or N=6.

The exact nonzero cases are:

OUTER: h/2<gamma<3h/2 and
  -w<=delta<w,  w=min(gamma-h/2,3h/2-gamma).

INNER: 5h/2<gamma<3h and
  -(gamma-5h/2)<=delta<gamma-5h/2.

These follow directly from the two floor inequalities. In particular the
inner lower endpoint is INCLUDED. For example gamma=80 degrees,
psi=10 degrees gives summands -70 and 90 degrees, whose slot indices are
-3 and 3, a difference of six. Its delta is -5 degrees, so Claude's
asserted strict |delta|<gamma-75 degrees wrongly excludes this point.

Reflection also exposes the boundary asymmetry. Reflect that point to
psi=-10 degrees with the same gamma. The summands are -90 and 70 degrees,
with indices -3 and 2, a difference of five; it is not good. The familiar
reflection formula floor(-t/h)=-floor(t/h)-1 fails when t/h is an integer.
Rotation by h does preserve G exactly. Reflection and twelve-fold rotation
both preserve its closure.

For a raw oriented separation d modulo 360 degrees the possible projected
windows are (30,90), (150,210), AND (270,330) degrees. Equivalently, its
shortest circular separation belongs to (30,90) union (150,180] degrees.
The latter convention is appropriate for the unique pair with separation
2gamma. The radial ranges quoted by Claude remain correct despite the
omitted raw window:
  |z| in [0,R) union (sqrt(2),U),
  R=2sin(15 degrees)=sqrt(2-sqrt(3)),
  U=2cos(15 degrees)=sqrt(2+sqrt(3)).

For the theorem it suffices to use the following CLOSED SUPERSET H of G:
  inner points: |z|<=R, with no angular restriction at all;
  outer points: sqrt(2)<=r<=U and, for some half-slot centre c_n,
    circular_distance(psi,c_n)<=45 degrees-arccos(r/2).
This set is closed: its inner disk is closed, and its outer part is a finite
union of closed angular/radial constraints away from zero. Thus it contains
closure(G). It deliberately discards the inner-star constraint and the
other outer-width bound gamma-15 degrees. Proving that H contains no unit
triangle proves the advertised closure result with room to spare.

3. THREE OUTER VERTICES: EXACT PROOF

Let g be the centroid, D=|g|. A unit equilateral triangle has circumradius
1/sqrt(3), so for suitable tau
  r_j^2=D^2+1/3+(2D/sqrt(3))cos(tau+2pi*j/3).

For these three cosines, the minimum is at most -1/2 and the spread is at
least 3/2. For the spread, rotation/permutation and reflection reduce tau
to [0,pi/3]; then the spread equals
  cos(tau)-cos(tau+2pi/3)=sqrt(3)sin(tau+pi/3)>=3/2.

The squared outer radii lie in [2,2+sqrt(3)], of width sqrt(3).
Their spread is at least sqrt(3)D, so D<=1. But then
  min_j r_j^2 <= D^2-D/sqrt(3)+1/3 <= 4/3 < 2,
contradicting the outer lower bound. This avoids every decimal and the
unnecessary convex-quadratic numerical maximum in Claude's version.

4. AT LEAST TWO INNER VERTICES: EXACT PROOF

Let p_1,p_2 be inner vertices a unit distance apart and m their midpoint.
The parallelogram identity gives
  |m|^2=(|p_1|^2+|p_2|^2)/2-1/4
        <=7/4-sqrt(3)=(1-sqrt(3)/2)^2.
The third vertex q has |q-m|=sqrt(3)/2. Consequently
  sqrt(3)-1 <= |q| <= 1.
The lower endpoint equals sqrt(2)R, which is strictly greater than R;
the upper endpoint is strictly less than sqrt(2). Thus q belongs to
neither permitted radial range. This also excludes three inner vertices.

5. ONE INNER AND TWO OUTER VERTICES: CONSERVATIVE EXACT PROOF

Write the inner vertex as p, rho=|p|<=R, and the outer vertices as
q_i=p+u_i, |u_i|=1. Since |q_1-q_2|=1, |u_1+u_2|=sqrt(3).
If rho=0, both outer radii would be one, already impossible. Otherwise
the smaller real projection of u_1,u_2 in the direction of p is at most
sqrt(3)/2, by taking the average and using |u_1+u_2|. Ordering the outer
radii as r_1<=r_2 gives
  r_1^2 <=1+rho^2+sqrt(3)rho,
  r_2 <=1+rho.
This derivation avoids any choice of wrapped relative angles.

The simple exact bounds
  173/100<sqrt(3)<7/4,
  R^2<27/100 and R<13/25
give
  r_1^2 <1+27/100+(7/4)(13/25)=109/50<(37/25)^2,
  r_2 <38/25.
Thus r_1<1.48 and r_2<1.52, while both radii are at least sqrt(2).

The elementary trigonometric certificate in section 6 proves
  cos(42 degrees)>37/50,
  cos(40 degrees)>19/25.
Hence gamma_1=arccos(r_1/2)>42 degrees and gamma_2>40 degrees.
The CLOSED outer lobe constraint still yields STRICT bounds
  |delta_1|<3 degrees, |delta_2|<5 degrees.
Their principal angular separation theta therefore satisfies
  distance(theta,30 degrees*Z)<8 degrees.                 (A)

The cosine law for |q_1-q_2|=1 is
  cos(theta)=(r_1^2+r_2^2-1)/(2r_1r_2).
Its derivative in r_1 has numerator r_1^2+1-r_2^2, and similarly in r_2.
On [sqrt(2),38/25]^2 these are positive, since
  r_i^2+1-r_j^2 >=3-(38/25)^2>0.
Both the UPPER and LOWER radius bounds are used here; an upper bound on
r_i^2 alone would not justify Claude's monotonicity sentence.

Therefore
  3/4 <= cos(theta) <=
  ((37/25)^2+(38/25)^2-1)/(2*(37/25)*(38/25))
  =547/703 <39/50.
The certificate also gives cos(38 degrees)>39/50 and cos(42 degrees)<3/4.
Since cosine decreases on [0,pi],
  38 degrees<theta<42 degrees.
Its closest multiple of 30 degrees is 30 degrees, at distance greater
than eight degrees. This contradicts (A).

These three cases prove the theorem for the closed superset H and hence
for closure(G). No inner angular constraint or floating-point estimate
has been used.

7. NULL SETS, DENSITY POINTS AND THE MEANING OF FIXED BASE

The closure theorem concerns the specified representative of P. It is
NOT invariant under arbitrary pointwise changes on null sets. For a
concrete illustration, take the unit equilateral triangle
  z_j=(1/sqrt(3))*exp(2pi*i*j/3).
Its six decomposition angles are 2pi*j/3 plus or minus
gamma_0=arccos(1/(2sqrt(3))). They are distinct. Reassign P at those six
angles, three to P_0 and their partners to P_2. This changes P on a finite
null set and makes the three vertices pointwise good, although their
radius 1/sqrt(3) lies in the original forbidden radial gap.

This does NOT defeat the density obstruction. Null changes do not alter
the density of any fibre at any angular point. A newly assigned isolated
value is not a density-one occurrence of that state. For the original
step-function palette, density-one occurrences of each state lie inside
its sector intervals; sector endpoints have the wrong densities. Thus
every pair usable in states_densityOne remains governed by the same
partner-pair geometry, regardless of null-set edits. Such edits also
cannot fill a new open two-dimensional region of good sums: pairs using
an exceptional angle form a product-null set, and the smooth/Lipschitz
sum map sends that set to a planar null set.

For any single fixed base centre x, the good-vertex set is x+G. Translating
the whole configuration by -x reduces it to the audited case. Changing
the one common base centre while retaining the same angular rule, rotating
the whole rule, or consistently relabelling its colours therefore supplies
no escape. The theorem already quantifies over all triangle locations and
orientations relative to that centre. It does not constrain a family of
genuinely different palettes at different centres.

The exact method ruled out is this: all three vertices are produced from
two unit directions at ONE common base, each pair is one of this word's
two partner pairs, and the density triangle is forced into their common
residual pair {2,5}. The theorem does not rule out other finite unit graphs,
other residual-list arguments, or other constructions of density data.

8. SMALLEST MEANINGFUL ESCAPES

Different actual base centres are the most direct change to the producer.
One could seek
  z_j=x_j+direction(a_j)+direction(b_j),
with the z_j forming a unit triangle and the represented palette pairs at
x_j all forcing the same residual colour set of size at most two.
This needs actual compatibility between centres, not freely assigned
angular lists. But no new general density lemma is needed:
States.states_densityOne already accepts an arbitrary centre on each
application, and States.two_label_triangle_impossible already accepts an
arbitrary map Fin 3->C with verified unit distances. The missing result
is the geometric/analytic producer of those three compatible instances.

Alternatively, change the angular rule genuinely. The two-valued rule
P=J={0,3} on [0,60 degrees)+120 degrees*Z and P=K={1,4} on the complementary
60-degree sectors admits the standard triangle: for any
0<t<60 degrees-gamma, a_j=t+120 degrees*j lies in a J-sector while
b_j=a_j+180 degrees+gamma lies in a K-sector. Their sums are the source
unit triangle. This is a different angular word, not a colour relabelling
or phase shift of the four-state countermodel. The source already excludes
its actual realization via two_state_impossible. It illustrates why
Claude's obstruction cannot be promoted to a general triangle impossibility
for finite palettes.

The previously accepted aligned-continuum argument is another independent
source constraint on Countermodel C's coincident directions; this geometric
triangle obstruction does not invalidate that argument or make the model
an actual weak colouring.
