## 1. Defect 5: duplicate and proportional supporting planes double-count facets

**The witness.** The six normals `±e1, ±e2, ±e3` with offsets all `1` return `8` — correct, the
cube `[-1,1]³`. Appending a duplicate of `(e1, 1)` returned **`28/3` for the same cube**.

**Cause.** `polytope_volume` summed `d_i·A_i/3` over the planes as given. A plane listed twice
had its facet counted twice, adding `d·A/3 = 1·4/3 = 4/3` — and `8 + 4/3 = 28/3` exactly.

**Repair.** `canonicalise` maps each half-space `{y·n ≤ d}` with `d > 0` to `{y·u ≤ 1}` with
`u = n/d`, then keeps **one plane per ray**: within a ray (positive multiples of `u`) it keeps
the largest `|u|`, i.e. the tightest constraint, breaking ties by index. The polytope is
unchanged, and the plane set that reaches the face loop contains no two proportional members.

### 1.1 Complete oriented facet accounting (the proof the repair needs)

> **Proposition.** Let `P = ⋂_{i=1}^{n}{y·n_i ≤ d_i}` be bounded with `0` interior, and let the
> plane set be pairwise non-proportional (as `canonicalise` guarantees). Then
> `vol P = (1/3) Σ_{i} d_i·A_i`, where `A_i` is the area of the facet on plane `i`, computed
> once per plane, and `A_i = 0` if plane `i` supports `P` in a set of dimension < 2.

*Proof.* (i) *Facets and supporting planes are in bijection.* A facet of `P` is a maximal
2-dimensional face, and it lies in the boundary of exactly one of the defining planes; conversely
a defining plane that supports a 2-dimensional set supports exactly one facet. So summing over
planes sums over facets, once each. (ii) *The vertex set recovers the facet.* The vertices on a
supporting plane are exactly the vertices of that facet: a vertex of `P` on the plane lies in the
facet, and every vertex of the facet is a vertex of `P`. With `≥ 3` such vertices the cyclic
order of §2 recovers the polygon, and a plane meeting `P` in a point or an edge yields `< 3`
and contributes `0`. (iii) *The divergence theorem.* With `0` interior to `P`, the field `y/3`
gives `vol P = (1/3)∮ y·ν dA = (1/3)Σ_facets d_i·A_i`, since `y·ν = d_i` on facet `i`. ∎

**What is not claimed.** `canonicalise` handles proportional planes exactly. *Nearly* proportional
planes — distinct rationals within `1e-20` of a common ray — are treated as distinct, and each
then contributes a genuine (thin) facet of its own; that is correct for the polytope defined by
those planes, and it is why the quantisation in `certify` uses a fixed denominator rather than a
tolerance.

### 1.2 The supported input domain

Documented at the function: normals nonzero; offsets `d_i > 0` (the origin must be interior, and
a non-positive offset is rejected); the normals positively spanning so the polytope is bounded
(an unbounded input raises `Unbounded`); no two half-spaces proportional after `canonicalise`
(duplicates and dominated planes are accepted and reduced). Degenerate inputs — coplanar normals,
zero normals, non-positive offsets — are refused rather than silently mis-measured.

## 3. Re-attribution: Fejes Tóth, not Lindelöf

`REVIEW_0653` §7 attributed the at-most-twelve-face premise to Lindelöf (1869) on the strength of
a survey. The primary statement is **Fejes Tóth's**, and it is restated as Theorem 4.1 of
Hales–McLaughlin (`https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~seanmcl/papers/McLaughlin-Dodec.pdf`, p. 16):

> **Theorem 4.1.** Let `P` be any polyhedron with at most 12 faces that contains a unit sphere
> `S²`. Let `B` be the ball of radius `t_dod` concentric with `S²`. The volume of the intersection
> of `P` with `B` is at least `ω_dod`. Equality holds exactly [when] `P` is congruent to the
> regular dodecahedron.

Provenance, from the same file: *"L. Fejes Tóth proved the Dodecahedral conjecture under the extra
hypothesis that `Λ(v₀, 2t_dod) ∖ {v₀}` has at most 12 elements. The proof occupies about eight
pages of the book Regular Figures [10]. See the main theorem of Section 33 and the main theorem of
Section 41 (including the note on page 265). Fejes Tóth's bound is a general bound about the
volume of truncated polyhedra. The polyhedra do not need to be Voronoi cells in a sphere packing."*

**Why this is the right input, and better than Lindelöf.** Our `D'` has twelve faces at distance
`≥ 1` and contains the unit ball. Theorem 4.1 gives `vol(D' ∩ B) ≥ ω_dod` directly — and
`D' ∩ B ⊆ D'`, so `vol D' ≥ ω_dod`. No rescaling is needed, the "at most 12" is exactly our
hypothesis, and the equality case is stated too, which is what the rigidity step of Main's
Theorem D needs.

**What is still owed to a referee.** Theorem 4.1 is a *restatement*; the proofs are in Fejes Tóth's
book, which this block did not consult. The dependency is now: Fejes Tóth, *Regular Figures*
(Pergamon 1964), §33 and §41, as restated by Hales–McLaughlin Theorem 4.1.

## 4. The ring-excess and face-area steps, stated so they can be checked independently

Three steps, each independent of the others, and none of them uses a computation in its
statement:

**(L) Ring-excess lemma.** For a bounded polytope `P = ⋂{y·n_i ≤ d_i}` with `0` interior, and a
set `J` of faces, `P(b) = P` with the `J`-faces moved to `b·d_i` satisfies
`vol P(b) − vol P = (b−1) Σ_{i∈J} d_i ∫₀¹ A_i(t) dt ≥ (b−1) Σ_{i∈J} d_i min_t A_i(t)`.
*(Proof: the volume is absolutely continuous in the face distance and its derivative there is the
face area; integrate over the finitely many intervals of constant combinatorial type.)*

**(D) The uncut disk.** The ring face on the plane `y·n_r = R` contains the disk of in-plane
radius `t* = R/2` when `R = b`, hence area `≥ π(ρ_r/4)² = 0.868315`. *(Proof: a neighbour at
distance `ρ` cuts a point at in-plane radius `t` only if `arctan(t/R) > α(ρ) − arccos((ρ/2)/√(R²+t²))`
with `cos α(ρ) ≤ (ρ² + ρ_r² − 4)/(2ρρ_r)`; the worst case is `ρ = 2`, `R = b`, where the boundary is
`t/R = 1/2` exactly — the identity `arctan(1/2) + arccos(4/(ρ_r√5)) = arccos(ρ_r/4)` from
`REVIEW_0608` §11.)*

**(T) The base.** `vol D' ≥ V_dod` by Fejes Tóth's Theorem 4.1 (§3).

Combining: `vol Ω(w1) ≥ V_dod + 5(b−1)·π(ρ_r/4)² = 5.773718 > 5.763417`.

**And exactly what fails with thirteen or more faces.** Step (T) is unavailable: Theorem 4.1 is
stated for **at most 12 faces**, and for `n ≥ 13` the analogous Fejes Tóth bound is strictly
smaller than `ω_dod`. The arithmetic of the failure, stated so it cannot be mistaken for a
constant that wants improving:

```
available slack   5.550291 + 0.223427 − 5.763417  =  0.010301
```

so a thirteenth face is affordable **only if it costs at most `0.010301`**. What it actually
costs, computed on a plane-direction grid against the dodecahedron:

| face distance `d` | worst cap | affordable? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `1.000` | `0.052007` | no, `5×` too big |
| `1.020` | `0.040218` | no |
| `1.045` | `0.028846` | no, `2.8×` too big |
| `1.080` | `0.016854` | no |
| `1.100` | `0.011797` | no, marginally |
| `1.150` | `0.003781` | **yes** |

The `d = 1.045` row is the best the project's own counting gives: `K15-E014` says at most twelve
centres lie within `2.09` of `w1`, so a *seventh* free centre has `ρ > 2.09` and its bisector
sits at `d = ρ/2 > 1.045`. That is a genuine improvement on `d = 1` — and it is **still not
enough, by a factor of 2.8**.

So the thirteen-face case does not fail marginally, and no sharpening of the cap bound can save
it: the cap would have to be smaller than the true worst cap by a further factor `2.8`. What
would close it is either a **face-area bound `A_min ≥ 1.03`** (today `0.868` provable, `1.17`
true) or a bound on the **number** of centres beyond twelve with faces inside `1.10` — a
different argument, not a better constant. The failure is in (T), not in (L) or (D).

## 6. Fallback

### 6.1 An analytic estimate for one extra face

Let `D` be the regular dodecahedron of inradius `1` (circumradius `R_d = 1.258409`) and let
`n` be a unit vector. The cap removed by an extra half-space `{y·n ≤ 1}` is
`C(n) = D ∩ {y·n > 1}`, and the volume loss is `vol C(n)`.

- *Crude, fully analytic.* `D ⊆ B(0, R_d)` with `R_d = 1.258409`, so a cap beyond distance `1`
  has volume at most the ball's:

  ```
  vol C(n) <= ∫₁^{R_d} π(R_d² − s²) ds = 0.245920 .
  ```

  That **exceeds** the ring-excess gain `0.223427`, so this bound does **not** close the
  thirteen-face case; it is `4.73×` the true worst case.
- *Worst case, computed on a grid.* Maximising over `(θ, φ)` in `2° × 4°` steps gives
  `vol C(n) = 0.052007`, at `n = (1,0,0)`. Then the thirteen-face bound is
  `5.550291 − 0.052007 + 0.223427 = 5.721711`, **`0.041707` below** the threshold — the number
  quoted in §4.
- **The analytic gap in one line.** The ball-section integral is the only estimate available in
  closed form and it is nearly five times too weak; a useful estimate has to use the local cone
  structure where the extra plane meets the dodecahedron, and that is the piece the next block
  would need. Note too that `0.052007` is a grid maximum, not a proved one — the true worst
  direction could be slightly worse, which would only widen the `0.042` gap.
