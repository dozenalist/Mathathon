1. PRECISE ACCEPTED CONTRACT

Let c:C->Fin k be measurable and satisfy WeakColoring k c. Let E be a
finite index set, x a common centre, and for every i in E let K_i be a set
with actual ApproachingContinuum x K_i v_i data. Let H_i be finite sets
of labels such that

  spatialSupport(c,j) is disjoint from Straddle(K_i)  for every j in H_i.

Suppose the actual signed unit vectors v_i have angle representatives
alpha_i in [phi-pi/6, phi+pi/6]. Then the complement of union_i H_i has
at least THREE labels. Equivalently, a complement of size at most two
is impossible.

Norm-one of v_i may be supplied explicitly or obtained from the unit
approximating directions and their convergence. The source approach
structure provides the centre and approaching points in K_i; neither
compactness nor preconnectedness of K_i is an additional requirement of
this particular argument. The spatial support exclusion is an essential
hypothesis; the name "common interface" by itself does not supply it.

The theorem is generic in k. Fin 5 inside the source four-edge path is
the number of path vertices, not a restriction to five colours. A pair
{a,b} covering the residual labels may have a=b. If no labels remain,
choose a=b=c(x); the same restriction follows vacuously. Thus there is
no hidden assumption k>=2 or that the residual set has exactly size two.

2. FROM THE CONE TO ONE OPEN TWO-COLOUR REGION

Set W=intersection_{i in E} Straddle(K_i). Finiteness and
straddle_isOpen make W open. The source finite_support_exclusion_ae
gives one plane-conull set on which every exclusion holds simultaneously.
Consequently, if the residual labels are covered by {a,b}, then

  for plane-a.e. z, z in W implies c(z)=a or c(z)=b.              (A)

The pair a,b is fixed throughout W. A direction-dependent pair would
not satisfy the rigidity or connectedness arguments below.

For theta in I=(phi-pi/3,phi+pi/3), and every i,

  |theta-alpha_i| < pi/2,

so the real inner product of e(theta) with v_i is strictly positive.
ApproachingContinuum.outer_strip therefore gives, for each i, a genuine
outer strip near x+e(theta) contained in Straddle(K_i). Taking the minimum
of finitely many positive widths gives such a strip in W. In angular
coordinates it can be stated as follows:

  for every theta in I there is delta_theta>0 such that
  |theta'-theta|<delta_theta and 1<r<1+delta_theta imply
  x+r*e(theta') belongs to W.                                  (B)

The source uses Euclidean distance between unit directions, which implies
this angular version by continuity, shrinking the width if necessary.
Every radial side here is OUTER. No per-continuum side choices are made.

The cone is CLOSED, but I is OPEN. At an endpoint direction of the cone
and any theta strictly inside I, the inner product is still positive.
Thus this step includes aperture exactly pi/3.

3. INDEPENDENT PROOF OF OA120, INCLUDING ALL MARGINS

Assume only that W is open, (A) holds, and (B) holds on the open interval
I of width 2*pi/3. These hypotheses already contradict WeakColoring.

3a. A uniform connected sector.

Take the compact interval J=[phi-pi/4,phi+pi/4], strictly inside I.
The angular neighbourhoods from (B) cover J. A finite subcover, followed
by the minimum of its positive radial widths, gives eta>0 such that

  theta in J, 1<r<1+eta => x+r*e(theta) in W.

Shrink eta to satisfy eta<1/2. Define

  S={x+r*e(theta): 1<r<1+eta, |theta-phi|<pi/4}.

S is nonempty and connected: it is the continuous image of the product
of two nonempty intervals. It is also open, though the source connected
lemma does not require this. It is contained in W. We have used uniformity
only on J, not near the endpoints of I.

3b. Each p in S has a genuine unit neighbour in W.

Write p=x+r*e(theta), so 1<r<3/2. Put alpha=acos(r/2). Then

  0<alpha<pi/3.

Choose beta=theta-alpha when theta>=phi, and beta=theta+alpha when
theta<phi. For the first case,

  beta >= phi-alpha > phi-pi/3,
  beta <= theta < phi+pi/4 < phi+pi/3.

For the second case,

  beta > theta > phi-pi/4 > phi-pi/3,
  beta < phi+alpha < phi+pi/3.

Thus beta is strictly inside I, including theta=phi. The base-circle
point q0=x+e(beta) satisfies |p-q0|=1, but we do NOT assume q0 is in W.

For s>1 sufficiently close to 1, define

  g(s)=(r^2+s^2-1)/(2*r*s),
  alpha(s)=acos(g(s)),
  beta(s)=theta-alpha(s) or theta+alpha(s), with the same sign as above,
  q(s)=x+s*e(beta(s)).

At s=1, g(1)=r/2 is strictly between -1 and 1. Positivity of r and s
and continuity give an interval about 1 on which g(s) remains in (-1,1).
Hence beta(s)->beta and q(s)->q0. The outer strip (B) at beta ensures
q(s) in W for every sufficiently small positive s-1: both its angular
and radial inequalities hold strictly. The cosine identity gives exactly

  |p-q(s)|^2 = r^2+s^2-2*r*s*g(s) = 1.

Choose one such q(s). No lower bound uniform in p on the allowable s-1
is needed. In particular, it causes no problem if this width collapses
as r decreases to 1 and beta approaches an endpoint of I.

The neighbours need only lie in the FULL W. They need not lie in S or
in the compact angular subinterval J. Substituting V=S without a further
argument would be unjustified; the accepted proof uses U=V=W.

3c. Source rigidity applies at every point of S.

For the chosen neighbour let v=q(s)-p, with |v|=1 and p+v in W.
Since W is open, open_two_unit_directions gives another unit vector w
with p+w in W and transverseDet(v,w) nonzero. The lemma does not require
p itself to lie outside W, any separation of U and V, or distinct radial
sides. Its proof perturbs a unit direction within an open set, giving a
nonparallel vector.

Apply binary_open_local_rigidity with U=V=W. All its hypotheses are now
present: measurable c, WeakColoring, openness, (A) twice, p in W, both
actual unit neighbours in W, and nonzero transverse determinant.
It yields a ball about p on which c is essentially a single label i_p.

This is ambient local essential constancy at EVERY p in S. In particular
p belongs to essentialLocalSet(c,i_p), even if p itself lies in a null
exceptional set for the colouring. connected_local_ae_constant now gives
a single i such that

  S is contained in essentialLocalSet(c,i).                    (C)

The source proves this using pairwise disjoint open essential-local sets;
their disjointness follows from positive area of any nonempty open
intersection. This is stronger than merely assigning colours to a curve.

3d. One fixed unit pair yields the final contradiction.

Set r0=1+eta/2 and lambda=asin(1/(2*r0)). Then

  0<lambda<pi/6<pi/4.

The points p_plus=x+r0*e(phi+lambda) and
p_minus=x+r0*e(phi-lambda) belong to S, and

  |p_plus-p_minus|=2*r0*sin(lambda)=1.

By (C), there are open neighbourhoods U_plus,U_minus of these points
on which c=i almost everywhere. The translation set

  T=(U_plus-p_plus) intersect (U_minus-p_minus)

is open and contains 0, so has positive area. Translation invariance of
Lebesgue measure gives c(u+p_plus)=i=c(u+p_minus) for a.e. u in T.
However, ae_translated_unit_pair says these two colours are unequal for
a.e. u in the plane. Their conull intersection with T is nonempty,
contradiction. This proves OA120 and hence CC60.

4. THE ALMOST-EVERYWHERE AND DEPENDENCY AUDIT

There is no intersection over uncountably many conull sets in this proof.

* Finitely many spatial exclusions produce the one fixed restriction (A).
* Unit-neighbour choices are pure geometry, pointwise in p; the chosen
  points need not themselves avoid any colouring exceptional set.
* binary_open_local_rigidity already handles the a.e. translated four-edge
  paths using finite intersections and the source Fubini/shear argument.
* connected_local_ae_constant uses open essential-local sets and the
  existing local-to-a.e. theorem; it does not select a common exceptional
  set over arbitrary balls by an uncountable intersection.
* The finish fixes just one unit pair, then intersects finitely many
  conull statements on a positive-area open set.

The original WeakColoring condition is a product-a.e. statement, so a
claim of properness at a specified direction does need justification.
Here it is licensed by WeakColoring.weak_all_directions, whose proof
upgrades the parameter using continuity of L2 translation correlations.
Rigidity.ae_translated_unit_pair calls precisely that result. Measurability
of c is included in the accepted contract and is used there and in local
rigidity. No pointwise proper-colouring assumption has been smuggled in.

No trace-palette cardinality, BinaryCenter premise, angular alternation,
essential angular boundary, T6, or strict-state theorem is used in this
declaration-level proof. In particular the new geometry does not assume
the old AC conclusion. AngularBoundary imports BoundaryTrace, which also
imports States; that compiled import is distinct from an actual use of
the strict-state declarations in this proof. This review does not claim
those imports have been removed or that the whole repository is free of
the older analytic machinery.

5. THE CLOSED ENDPOINT AND THE REJECTED AXIS ARGUMENT

The endpoint is established by the strict inequality r>1, which gives
acos(r/2)<pi/3. At r=1 the sign-choice neighbour can fall on an excluded
endpoint of I; working outside the unit circle repairs exactly that
problem. Neither a uniform neighbour margin as r->1 nor a closed positive
arc is required. Thus weakening the theorem to aperture <60 degrees is
unnecessary.

The unsigned-axis extension is not licensed. For example, take x=0 and
real segments K_plus=[0,rho], K_minus=[-rho,0], 0<rho<1/10. Their signed
approaches are +1 and -1. On the positive real ray near radius one,

  Straddle(K_plus) = (1,1+rho),
  Straddle(K_minus) = (1-rho,1).

These radial portions do not intersect. Choosing an outer strip for one
and an inner strip for the other cannot produce a strip in their common
intersection. The allowance of independent radial sides for two different
angular locations in binary_strip_palette_rigidity does not fix this.
This refutes that proposed strip proof, not every conceivable separate
theorem about opposite signed directions.

6. THE SIX-CYCLE CONSEQUENCE AND THE TINY FINITE CHECK

For E_i={i,i+1 mod 6}, the actual common-interface data must obey:

  in every CLOSED 60-degree angular cone, the labels covered by the
  corresponding excluded edges number at most THREE.

For q=(0,0,1,3,3,4), edges 0,1,2 have directions 0,0,60 degrees and
cover labels {0,1,2,3}. Their residual pair is {4,5}, so CC60 applies.
The common positive arc is (-30 degrees,90 degrees), width 120 degrees.
There is also a second forbidden cone using edges 3,4,5, directions
180,180,240 degrees and excluded labels {0,3,4,5}.

On the six-direction grid q_i in Z/6, the exact test is: for each d,
take ALL edges with q_i in {d,d+1}; their endpoint union must have at
most three labels. It includes equality at the cone endpoints and is
applied to directed residues, not residues modulo three.
