ACTUAL MC120 ENDPOINT: EXPLICIT PROPER 19-COLOUR COUNTEREXAMPLE
14 November 2026

RESULT AND SCOPE

The generic strict-aperture MC120 theorem CANNOT be extended to a closed
120-degree cone. There are two compact connected embedded arcs K_+, K_-
approaching the SAME centre 0 in directions +60 and -60 degrees, a Borel
POINTWISE PROPER 19-colouring c of the whole plane, and exact essential
spatial-support exclusions H_+, H_-, whose union excludes 18 of the 19
labels. Thus the residual palette is exactly one label.

This is an actual continuum/colouring counterexample, not only an angular
strip model. It handles every point of the plane and all support boundaries.
It is NOT a six-colour counterexample and makes no novelty-priority claim.
It does not refute a special six-colour endpoint theorem using additional
six-cycle combinatorics. It does refute the palette-independent closed-MC120
statement with precisely the hypotheses in section 4 of A4.

Grade: complete elementary paper proof, not Lean verified. Original source
files were read only. No builds, installs, heavy computation, or subagents.

AngularBoundary.lean defines
  Straddle(K) = {z : exists p,q in K, |z-p|<1 and |z-q|>1},
and proves it open. Its ApproachingContinuum structure records 0 in K and
points r_n w_n in K with r_n>0, r_n ->0, |w_n|=1, w_n ->v. The construction
below additionally makes K compact, connected, and an embedded arc.

spatialSupport(c,j) is the support of plane Lebesgue measure restricted to
the colour-j set. Coloring.lean defines ProperColoring by pointwise
inequality at every unit pair, and WeakColoring by AEMeasurability plus
almost-everywhere inequality for plane point/angular direction pairs.
A Borel ProperColoring therefore satisfies the source WeakColoring.

All angles in the proof are in radians. Identify C with R^2, write
  e(theta)=(cos theta,sin theta), rho=1/100,
  alpha(t)=pi/3+2t,
  q_+(t)=t e(alpha(t)), q_-(t)=t e(-alpha(t)), 0<=t<=rho,
  K_+={q_+(t):0<=t<=rho}, K_-={q_-(t):0<=t<=rho}.
Let S_+=Straddle(K_+), S_-=Straddle(K_-), and W=S_+ intersect S_-.

1. ACTUAL APPROACHING CONTINUA

Both parameter maps are continuous on [0,rho], so their images are compact
and connected. They are injective because |q_+(t)|=|q_-(t)|=t. Each is an
embedded arc; the two arcs meet only at 0, since their nonzero arguments lie
in disjoint intervals near +pi/3 and -pi/3.

For n>=0 set r_n=rho/(n+1) and w_n=e(+(pi/3+2r_n)) for K_+, or use the
minus sign for K_-. These give the exact ApproachingContinuum data at 0,
with signed limiting directions v_+=e(pi/3), v_-=e(-pi/3). Their circular
separation is exactly 2pi/3, and they lie in the closed cone [-pi/3,pi/3].

2. THE ENTIRE COMMON STRADDLING REGION IS UNIT-INDEPENDENT

First, every z in either S_+ or S_- satisfies
                         1-rho < |z| < 1+rho.                 (1)
Indeed a witness |z-q|<1 gives |z|<1+|q|<=1+rho, and a witness |z-q|>1
gives |z|>1-|q|>=1-rho. Strict inequalities are preserved.

Define the open disks of radius 1/2
  D_+=B((sqrt(3)/2,0),1/2), D_-=B((-sqrt(3)/2,0),1/2).
We prove
  W intersect {|z|>1} is a subset of D_+,
  W intersect {|z|<1} is a subset of D_-,
  W intersect {|z|=1} is empty,                            (2)
and any point in the first part has distance >1 from any point in the
second part. Each disk itself has all pairwise distances strictly <1.

2A. OUTER ESTIMATE, INCLUDING r=1 UNTIL IT IS EXCLUDED

Let z in W have r=|z|>=1 and write z=r e(theta), with theta in (-pi,pi].
For each sign choose the close witness q_+(t_+) or q_-(t_-), respectively.
Both t_+,t_->0, since q(0)=0 is not strictly closer than 1 when r>=1.
The squared-distance inequalities give
  cos(theta-alpha(t_+)) > (r^2-1+t_+^2)/(2rt_+) >=0,
  cos(theta+alpha(t_-)) > (r^2-1+t_-^2)/(2rt_-) >=0.

There is no hidden angular-wrap choice here: each relevant open
semicircle is an interval contained in (-pi,pi), since
  alpha(t)+pi/2 <= 5pi/6+1/50 < pi.
Their intersection therefore gives the ordinary real inequalities
         -pi/6+2t_+ < theta < pi/6-2t_-.                    (3)
Put h=r-1. The close witnesses also imply r<1+t_+ and r<1+t_-, so
  0<=h<rho, t_+,t_->h, and |theta|<pi/6-2h.                (4)

For 0<=s<=1/50, the 1-Lipschitz property of sine gives
  sin(pi/6-u) >= 1/2-u >= 12/25 > 1/3  (0<=u<=s).
Integrating gives
  cos(pi/6-s) >= sqrt(3)/2+s/3.
Consequently (4), with s=2h, gives the STRICT estimate
                  cos(theta) > sqrt(3)/2+2h/3.             (5)
The strictness also holds at h=0 because the angular interval is open.

The condition z in D_+ is equivalent to
                  r^2-sqrt(3) r cos(theta)+1/2 < 0.
By (5), its left side is strictly less than
  r^2-(3/2)r+1/2-(2sqrt(3)/3)rh
    = h(1/2+h)-(2sqrt(3)/3)(1+h)h
    <= h(1/2+h)-(1+h)h = -h/2 <=0,
using 2sqrt(3)/3>1. Thus z in D_+. Equation (3) also gives Re z>0.

2B. INNER ESTIMATE, INCLUDING r=1 UNTIL IT IS EXCLUDED

Let z in W have r=|z|<=1 and write z=-r e(beta), beta in (-pi,pi].
Here r>99/100 by (1). For each sign choose a far witness q_+(t_+) or
q_-(t_-). Again t_+,t_->0, since 0 is not strictly farther than 1.
The far inequalities give
  cos(beta-alpha(t_+)) > (1-r^2-t_+^2)/(2rt_+) >= -t_+/(2r),
  cos(beta+alpha(t_-)) > (1-r^2-t_-^2)/(2rt_-) >= -t_-/(2r).
Set delta(t)=arcsin(t/(2r)). Since 0<t<=1/100 and r>99/100,
  0<t/(2r)<1/198<1/2,
  0<delta(t)<=2(t/(2r))=t/r<(100/99)t<2t.                 (6)
The bound arcsin(u)<=2u for 0<=u<=1/2 follows directly by integrating its
derivative, which is at most 2 on that interval.

The two angular intervals of radius pi/2+delta(t) around +alpha(t_+) and
-alpha(t_-) are again contained in (-pi,pi): indeed
  alpha(t)+pi/2+delta(t)<5pi/6+4t<=5pi/6+1/25<pi.
Hence their intersection yields
  -pi/6+2t_+-delta(t_+) < beta < pi/6-2t_-+delta(t_-),
and (6) implies
                              |beta|<pi/6.                 (7)

Thus Re z<0. Moreover
  r^2-sqrt(3)r cos(beta)+1/2
    < r^2-(3/2)r+1/2 = (r-1)(r-1/2) <=0,
because 99/100<r<=1. This is exactly z in D_-.

At r=1, 2A gives Re z>0 and 2B gives Re z<0, an impossibility.
This proves every assertion of (2), including the missing unit-circle
boundary. No almost-everywhere exception is used.

2C. DISTANCES WITHIN AND BETWEEN THE TWO PARTS

Two points in D_+ have distance <1 by the triangle inequality through its
centre, and the same is true for D_-. If z is in the outer part of W,
(3) and r>1 give Re z>sqrt(3)/2. If w is in the inner part, (7) and
|w|>99/100 give Re w<-(99/100)sqrt(3)/2. Therefore
                 |z-w| >= Re z-Re w > 199sqrt(3)/200 >1.
This proves that W contains NO unit-distance pair.

For completeness W has both parts nonempty. Take t=rho and points
z_out=(1+rho/4,0), z_in=(-(1-rho/4),0). At both signs,
cos alpha(rho)>=cos(pi/3)-2rho=12/25. For z_out, q(0) is the far witness
and q(rho) is close; for z_in, q(0) is close and q(rho) is far. Substitution
in r^2+rho^2 minus/plus 2r rho cos alpha(rho) verifies both strict
inequalities. Nonemptiness is not needed for the counterexample logic.

3. AN EXPLICIT BOREL PROPER NINE-COLOUR BACKGROUND

For z=(x,y) put
  m=floor(5x/3), n=floor(5y/3),
  b(z)=3(m mod 3)+(n mod 3), with residues in {0,1,2}.
This is a Borel map to {0,...,8}. Its grid cells have side 3/5.
If two points of the same b-colour are in one cell, their distance is
strictly less than its diameter 3sqrt(2)/5<1.
If they are in distinct cells, one coordinate index differs by a nonzero
multiple of 3, hence by at least 3. Their corresponding coordinate
separation is at least 6/5 (in fact strictly greater for the half-open
cells), so their distance is >1. Thus b is pointwise proper everywhere,
including grid boundaries and negative-coordinate cells.

4. WHOLE-PLANE PROPER 19-COLOURING AND EXACT EXCLUSIONS

Use labels P_+={0,...,8}, P_-={9,...,17}, and a=18. Define c everywhere by

  c(z)=18,                 if z in W;
  c(z)=b(z),               if z not in W and z not in S_+;
  c(z)=9+b(z),             otherwise.

The last case means precisely z in S_+ but z not in S_-. The sets S_+,
S_- and W are open, so c is Borel.

To verify pointwise properness, equal colours in P_+ or P_- imply equal
background colours b, and hence cannot occur at unit distance by section
3. Equal colour 18 forces both points into W, which has no unit pair by
section 2. The two palettes and colour 18 are disjoint. This exhausts all
equal-colour cases, so ProperColoring 19 c holds pointwise and consequently
WeakColoring 19 c holds in the exact source sense.

Every point of S_+ has colour in P_- union {18}, so no label in P_+ occurs
anywhere on S_+. Every point of S_- has colour in P_+ union {18}, so no
label in P_- occurs anywhere on S_-. Set H_+=P_+ and H_-=P_-.

These pointwise absences imply the REQUIRED SUPPORT EXCLUSIONS, not just
almost-everywhere absences: if U is open and the colour-j class is disjoint
from U, every z in U has an open neighbourhood within U whose restricted
colour-j measure is zero. Such z is outside spatialSupport(c,j). Therefore
  Disjoint(spatialSupport(c,j), Straddle(K_+)) for every j in H_+,
  Disjoint(spatialSupport(c,j), Straddle(K_-)) for every j in H_-.
The open-set condition is crucial and has been checked. Colour-class
support may meet the boundary of S_+ or S_-, which is harmless because
Straddle uses strict distance inequalities. All such boundary points were
already assigned a colour in the total piecewise definition.

Finally {0,...,18} minus (H_+ union H_-) is exactly {18}. Thus every
hypothesis of generic closed-aperture MC120 is satisfied, while its claimed
contradiction is false. The original strict <120 theorem is unaffected:
the limiting directions here are exactly 120 degrees apart.

5. AUDIT OF THE STRAIGHT-NEEDLE CANDIDATE

The straight-needle claim that motivated this search is TRUE and does not
extend to the curved arcs above. Here is a direct exact unit-pair witness.
Take straight K_+=[0,L_+ e(pi/3)] and K_-=[0,L_- e(-pi/3)] with L_+,L_->0.
Choose 0<t<=min(L_+,L_-,1/10), and put
  d=t^2/(2(t+sqrt(3))), a=sqrt(3)/2-d,
  z_+=(-a,1/2), z_-=(-a,-1/2).
Then |z_+-z_-|=1 and |z_+|^2=|z_-|^2=1-sqrt(3)d+d^2<1, so 0 is the
close witness for each point and each segment. The smallest of their four
squared distances to the two witnesses t e(+-pi/3), minus 1, is
  t^2-(t+sqrt(3))d+d^2=t^2/2+d^2>0.
Thus both z_+,z_- lie in Straddle(K_+) intersect Straddle(K_-).
The radial deficit is O(t^2), while their angular excess past the limiting
inner endpoints is O(t^2) for this deliberately conservative exact choice.
Larger choices of the far witness can give more bulge; no such asymptotic
claim is needed. The exact common unit pair is the relevant conclusion.

The curved counterexample uses outward angular deviation 2t, already
linear in radius. The suggested larger deviation sqrt(t) is unnecessary.
Its inner far inequality is overpowered by the outward angular deviation,
as quantified by arcsin(t/(2r))<2t. This identifies exactly why the needle
argument does not prove an arbitrary-continuum endpoint theorem.
