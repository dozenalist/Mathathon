Hadwiger--Nelson Continuation Prompt

Current task statement

Let $\chi(\mathbb{R}^{2})$ be the chromatic number of the plane: the least $k$ for which there is a colouring $c : \mathbb{R}^{2} \to \{1, \dots, k\}$ with $|x - y| = 1 \Rightarrow c(x) \neq c(y)$. The known state is $5 \le \chi(\mathbb{R}^{2}) \le 7$: the upper bound is the classical hexagonal $7$-colouring, the lower bound rests on explicit finite unit-distance graphs produced since 2018 in the de Grey--Polymath16 line, machine-verified to have chromatic number $5$. No explicitly realised $6$-chromatic unit-distance graph is known, and no $6$-colouring of the plane is known.

Determine $\chi(\mathbb{R}^{2})$, or failing that, close one half of it, or failing that, reduce one half of it to a finite decision problem that a checker can settle. The problem is settled exactly when one finite unit-distance graph $G \subset \mathbb{R}^{2}$ with $\chi(G) = k$ and one proper colouring $c : \mathbb{R}^{2} \to \{1, \dots, k\}$ exist for the same explicit $k$.

Why I1--I4 are not terminal

Repair of I3. The sentence "$\chi \le 6$ needs an actual 6-colouring" misstates the obligation. For finite $k$, a graph $G$ satisfies $\chi(G) \le k$ if and only if every finite subgraph of $G$ does. Applied to the unit-distance graph of the plane,

  $\chi(\mathbb{R}^{2}) \le 6$   if and only if   no finite unit-distance graph in the plane has chromatic number $\ge 7$.

The upper half of the problem is therefore a finitary statement about finite point configurations, containing no colouring at all. Every no-go theorem you cite for lattice, periodic, radial, o-minimal and finite-component colourings constrains the shape of a colouring, and so cannot bear on a statement that quantifies over no colouring. Note also that R1 is a corollary of this same equivalence, not an independent tool; the structural no-gos are therefore not merely weakened by R1, they are irrelevant to the question of existence. Rewrite the upper-bound programme in the finitary form and attack that.

Repair of I4. By the same equivalence, a proof of $\chi(\mathbb{R}^{2}) \ge 6$ is, contrapositively, the existence of a finite $6$-chromatic unit-distance graph in the plane. There is no such thing as a proof of $\chi \ge 6$ from which no finite witness can be extracted; there are only proofs from which nobody has extracted one yet. Non-explicitness is an extraction problem with a definite shape: finitely many vertices, algebraic coordinates by R1, so for each vertex count $n$ and height bound $H$ the search space is finite and enumerable. Either carry out the extraction, or prove the infeasibility of the extraction at the sizes involved by a lower bound on witness size; and if the compactness argument yields neither, then what it proves is not $\chi \ge 6$, and the claim must be restated with its hypothesis displayed. Resolve this first: as written, I4 asserts a proof of $\chi \ge 6$ while I1 sets out to reach $\chi \ge 7$. One of the two is being misreported, and the misreport sits exactly where the value of the work lies.

Repair of I1. The constraint you dismiss is not a constraint on the circle. $P_x(e)$ is a palette at a centre $x$ inside a colouring of a neighbourhood of $x$, not a colouring of $S^{1}(x)$: it must be simultaneously compatible with the palettes at every centre $y$ with $|x - y| = 1$. The $2$-colourability of the unit-distance graph on a circle -- true, since that graph is a disjoint union of $6$-cycles -- says only that no obstruction lives on one circle. It says nothing about a ball. The claim "ternary centres are consistent" must therefore be tested thus: exhibit a finite $S \subset B(x, 2)$, containing three points of $S^{1}(x)$ carrying three distinct colours, whose unit-distance graph admits a proper $6$-colouring. If such an $S$ exists, the local obstruction survives to radius $2$ and you have a real global problem, stated. If no such $S$ exists, the obstruction is already dead at radius $2$ and you must produce the contradiction. Both outcomes are results. The report asserts the first without testing either.

Repair of I2. "A length-six interface cycle is untreated" and "states become set-valued" are not mathematical statements. A state is an object; write its definition, the state set attached to each site, the transition relation, the interface cycle with its six sites and the constraint each carries, and the resulting finite constraint problem with its exact size. If the state set is astronomically large, that is the finding, and the deliverable is the exact count together with a symmetry-reduced quotient. Note further that set-valued states are a loss, not a fact: the standard remedy, and the one that produced the known lower bounds, is to enlarge the configuration until the state is forced to be a singleton. Design configurations that force singleton palettes; do not accept a set-valued state and stop.

Adversarial audit requirements

Every claimed result must survive the following, and the audit report is part of the deliverable.

- Every use of compactness names the finitary object it yields: the finite set of points, the finite subgraph, the finite constraint set. An unnamed finite object is an unfinished proof.
- Every no-go theorem states its class precisely and is checked for vacuity: what is the largest class the proof actually excludes, and is anything outside it also excluded?
- Every invariance or blindness claim is proved for all $k$, not exhibited at two values.
- Every palette or state claim is a statement about a ball, not a circle; verify that the constraint set used includes the cross-constraints between centres at distance $1$.
- Every satisfiable or unsatisfiable claim carries a checkable certificate: a resolution proof, a DRAT or LRAT file, or a verified exhaustive enumeration; report instance size, encoding, solver, version, and the verification of the certificate itself.
- Every unit length is verified exactly, in exact arithmetic or in Lean, never numerically; a near-unit length with an unstated tolerance is an error.
- Every structural assumption on a colouring -- measurability, periodicity, bounded variation, regularity of colour classes -- is either the hypothesis under study or a gap.
- Every statement equivalent in strength to the problem is flagged as such and is not used as a step.
- Every sentence of the form "this requires a new mechanism" is replaced by a theorem, a construction, or an explicit falsification test that was run and failed.
- Simplification is audited too: a shorter proof that no longer reaches the same conclusion is not a simplification.

What does not count

Reported as progress, the following are insufficient: the status of record R1--R6 restated; module counts, port sizes, axiom lists, or toolchain versions; five errata shown to be extraction artifacts; a no-go theorem for one class of colourings presented as a no-go for the problem; a compactness-derived witness presented as though it were explicit; prose descriptions of the $k = 6$ subproblem; improvements to constants that do not change $k$; numerical or SAT claims without certificates; reductions to another open statement; measurable or finite-field results transplanted; any argument resting on $|\mathbb{R}^{2}| = \mathfrak{c}$; and the observation that a circle of radius one is $2$-colourable, which is a fact about a circle.
