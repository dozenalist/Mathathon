# Referee-checkable certificate: no 6-colouring constant on honeycomb cells, for `a` in `(1/√28, 1/√19)`

Restricted honeycomb family only. Nothing here constrains `chi(R^2)`; a hypothetical 6-colouring may be
non-constant on cells. Grades: **[A]** machine-checked in this project with the hash/log named;
**[P]** paper-level derivation written here, checkable by hand; **[S]** read from source.

## 0. The theorem

**Theorem (cell-constant honeycomb no-go).** Let `a ∈ (1/√28, 1/√19) = (0.1889822…, 0.2294157…)`
(this contains `[19/100, 22/100]` and `a = 1/5`). Let `Λ = a√3·(ℤ + ℤω)` be the triangular lattice, `H_a`
the Voronoi hexagon of `Λ` (circumradius `a`, inradius `a√3/2 = √3/10` at `a = 1/5`), and let `c` be any
colouring of the plane with **at most 6 colours** that is constant on the interior of every cell
`H_a + λ`, `λ ∈ Λ`. Then `c` is not proper: some two points at distance exactly 1 share a colour.

*Equivalently:* the infinite graph `Cay(ℤ², F)` with `F = {v : N(v) ∈ {4,7,9,12,13}}`,
`N(p,q) = p²+pq+q²`, is not 6-colourable — because the 73-cell induced patch `P = {v : N(v) ≤ 19}`
is already not, and a proper 6-colouring of the infinite graph would restrict to one of the patch.

*Scope.* One cell-constant family, one interval of one parameter, six colours. No statement about
`k ≥ 7` (PATCH19 reports `chi(G[P]) = 8` without a certificate — not accepted here), none about other
cell shapes, tilings, or `a`, and none about the plane.

## 2. The geometry, exactly, and the tangency cases

A cell `H = H_a` is the Voronoi hexagon of `Λ`, with vertices in the six directions
`30° + 60°k`, circumradius `a`, inradius `a√3/2`, nearest-neighbour distance `a√3`. For `H` centrally
symmetric, `H − H = 2H`, so the set of distances between `H` and `H + v` is
`{|v + w| : w ∈ 2H}`, an interval because `2H` is convex and `|·|` is continuous, with

* `dmin(v) = dist(0, 2H+v)` = `0` if `0 ∈ 2H+v` (cells overlap or touch), else the minimum of the
  point-to-segment distances from `0` to the six edges — the minimiser is an **edge interior point** when
  the projection falls inside an edge, otherwise a **vertex**;
* `dmax(v) = max_{w ∈ 2H} |v+w|`, attained at a vertex of `2H+v` (convexity).

The pair is forced iff `dmin < 1 < dmax` **strictly**: the realisable distances are the *open* interval,
so a contact at distance exactly 1 lying only on boundaries constrains nothing for an
interior-constant colouring.

**Exact numbers at `a = 1`** (verified [A], `checks/interval_a1.py`, agreement with
`interval_cert.json`: 0 of 42 mismatches):

| `N(v)` | `dmin²` | `dmax²` | `dmin, dmax` | forced window in `a` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 3 | 28 | 1.7321, 5.2915 | `(1/√28, 1/√3) = (0.18898, 0.57735)` |
| 7 | 7 | 43 | 2.6458, 6.5574 | `(0.15250, 0.37796)` |
| 9 | 12 | 49 | 3.4641, 7.0000 | `(0.14286, 0.28868)` |
| 12 | 16 | 64 | 4.0000, 8.0000 | `(0.12500, 0.25000)` |
| 13 | 19 | 67 | 4.3589, 8.1854 | `(0.12218, 0.22942)` |

Intersecting: all five forced iff `a ∈ (1/√28, 1/√19)`. **The endpoint contacts are of both types**:
at `a = 1`, 12 of the 42 minima are attained at an edge interior and 30 at a vertex [A] — this is not a
vertex-to-vertex phenomenon.

**Correction to an earlier phrasing of mine (and to the natural shortcut).** `|v| + 2h(v̂)` is the
**maximum projection onto `v̂`**: `max_{w ∈ 2H} ⟨v + w, v̂⟩ = |v| + h_{2H}(v̂) = |v| + 2h(v̂)`. Since
`|x| ≥ ⟨x, v̂⟩` for every `x`, that quantity is a **lower bound** for `max_{w} |v + w|`. Being a lower
bound, it is *useful* in the direction needed here: a lower bound `> 1` certifies `dmax > 1`. What it
does not give is an upper bound on `dmax` (for that, `|v| + 2a` from the circumradius is available).
The certificate still computes `dmin` and `dmax` exactly from the polygon — edge by edge and vertex by
vertex — so no estimate is load-bearing.

**The immediately adjacent excluded norms, and why strictness matters.**
* `N = 3` (`|v| = 3a`): `dmin² = 1`, `dmax² = 25` at `a = 1`, so at `a = 1/5` the window is `(0.2, 1)` and
  `dmax = 1` **exactly** — the only distance-1 pairs are vertices, so no constraint. For any `a > 1/5`
  this offset *becomes* forced and the graph gains 6 edges; that only strengthens the conclusion
  (UNSAT is inherited by supergraphs), which is why the certified interval can extend above `1/5`.
* `N = 1` (`|v| = a√3`): `dmax = 0.7211 < 1` at `a = 1/5`, so the two cells are simply too close to
  contain a distance-1 pair. It becomes forced only for `a > 1/√13 = 0.27735`, which is *outside* the
  certified interval — that is the mechanism which produced the earlier `N ∈ {1,3,4}` family at
  `a ≥ 1/√13`.
* `N = 16` (`v = (4,0)`): `dmin = 1.0392 > 1` at `a = 1/5`; the crude bound `|v| − 2a` would wrongly
  include it, and it first becomes forced at a larger `a`.

## 4. What a referee must still decide

1. Nothing in the chain above depends on the producer's checker, on any solver's SAT/UNSAT *claim*, or on
   the claimed `chi(G[P]) = 8`.
2. The one link resting on external (supervising-audit) work is L6/L7's clause-multiset identity; if a
   referee wants it from within this directory, regenerate the CNF from `patch19_cert.json` and diff —
   a few lines of code, not yet stored here.
3. `chi(G[P]) ≥ 8` is **not** certified. Only `≥ 7` (i.e. no 6-colouring) is.
4. The theorem is about cell-constant colourings. It does not say that six colours fail for the plane, and
   it does not combine with other parameters into a bound on `chi(R^2)`.
