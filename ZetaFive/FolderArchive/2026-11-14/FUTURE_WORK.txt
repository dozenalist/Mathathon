FUTURE WORK: UNDERSTAND THE BOUNDS, THEN TEST HIGHER ODD ZETA VALUES
14 November 2026 (America/Los_Angeles)

Purpose
-------
Find better expositions of the arithmetic and analytic bounds in the new,
simplified zeta(5) argument. Use that understanding to determine precisely
which steps do and do not extend to zeta(7), zeta(9), and higher odd values.
The aim is a better explanation and a defensible diagnosis of the method's
limitations. Do not assume in advance that every possible extension fails.

This file is a research handoff, based on the saved workspace. It does not
certify the manuscripts or add a new irrationality result.

2. Find and develop better expositions of the arithmetic bounds
--------------------------------------------------------------
Read the revised local proofs first. Explain one common rational bilinear
form in three coordinate systems: integer-valued bases for small primes,
residue-class/jet bases for inner primes, and actual pole-factor bases with
a polynomial correction for outer primes. Use a small worked example for
each, then state the uniform lemma and explain every hypothesis.

The exposition should answer the following questions explicitly:

* Why must the bound control the minimum p-adic valuation of ALL polynomial
  coefficients, with X still indeterminate? Evaluation at one X is not a
  substitute for coefficientwise integrality.
* Why does a basis change multiply the determinant by det(C)^2, and when
  is det(C) a p-adic unit? How do entry bounds w_i+w_j yield the determinant
  bound 2 sum_i w_i?
* How does the pullback mu_X(R)=tau_X(x^5 R(-x^2)) connect the rational
  pairing to tau(P)=L(P''')/24 and Bernoulli moments? Which denominator
  losses arise from derivatives, harmonic sums, and partial fractions?
* Why must inner weights be capped so that each row works in every class?
  Why does the zero class need a separate allocation?
* Why do polynomial parts remain after pole cancellation? Explain the
  correction A+p^(-1)UV and pay its full rank loss. The residue algebra
  alone does not account for the entire bilinear form.
* How do the factorial scalar and signed local bounds combine? The
  normalizer m_K=product p^(-L_p) is positive rational, not necessarily an
  integer. Positive L_p permit division by common factors and save cost.
* How do finite class counts become profiles E(K/p), and why does summing
  p log(p) E(K/p) give K^2 integral E(x)/x^3 dx? Keep the cutoff fixed before
  K tends to infinity and account for uniform error terms.
* What do the periodic Bernoulli primitives accomplish in the inner tail?
  Separate exact rational table integration from the asymptotic prime sum.

Use framework_arithmetic_notes.txt and audit_simplification.txt as aids to
interpretation, while checking all estimates against the revised baseline.
The latter preserves the user's earlier preference for a better argument,
not merely a shorter one. A dictionary of related objects is insufficient:
give precise bridge identities and explain where each is used.

Deliverable: an arithmetic reading guide with a dependency map, worked
examples, a prime-range budget table, and exact pointers to the general
proofs and check scripts. A reader should be able to identify the loss
caused by changing the order of the zeta value before running any optimizer.

3. Find and develop better expositions of the analytic bounds
-----------------------------------------------------------
Organize the explanation around the chain

  positive Gram determinant -> Andreief integral -> logarithmic energy
  -> comparison-measure certificate -> normalized small-value bound.

Explain the following details rather than hiding them in a constant:

* Where positivity and nonvanishing come from, and why the squared
  Vandermonde is the pair interaction in the determinant integral.
* How y_i=K sqrt(t_i) and the products D_N^6/D_K produce the external field
  V(t), with a uniform finite-product error estimate.
* The different roles of the original moment measure, a configuration
  measure, and the auxiliary comparison measure rho. They are not the
  same measure. The configuration and comparison measures have equal
  mass lambda=h/K.
* Why I(eta-rho)<=0 for a zero-mass signed measure leads to a bound using
  2U^rho-V <= M_0. State the integrability assumptions and sign convention.
* Why atomic configurations cannot be substituted directly into finite
  logarithmic self-energy, how circle regularization controls this, and
  why a decaying tail must remain after the uniform configuration bound.
* How the factorial normalization cancels the K^2 log K term exactly,
  leaving a K^2 coefficient and controlled O(K log K) errors.
* What the 684 interval certificates actually prove, including complete
  interval coverage and the unbounded tail. A sampled numerical plot is
  not a uniform potential inequality. Explain why the chosen measure
  need only certify a useful bound; exact equilibrium is unnecessary.

Deliverable: an analytic reading guide with the energy argument written
in full, a transparent normalization calculation, and an annotated route
through the exact certificate. Illustrations may aid intuition, but every
inequality used in the conclusion must have a proof or certified source.

For both guides, search for clearer primary-source treatments and record
exact versions and theorem locations. Begin with the sources already in
full argument with attribution/source_ledger.json: Persiflage for the
organizing narrative; Prevost for the transform and moments; Fauzan for
the specific determinant and comparison measure; Firsching for revised
arithmetic and analytic implementations; Zudilin and Brown for the broader
determinant/normalization perspective. Follow cited classical sources on
logarithmic energy, integer-valued polynomials, Bernoulli summation, and
rational modifications of orthogonality where they explain an actual step.
Do not present these starting points as newly surveyed or as interchangeable
proofs of the particular estimates. Assess each exposition by what it
explains, its prerequisites, and what it leaves to imported certificates.

4. Test the NEW simplified argument at higher odd orders
------------------------------------------------------
First specify s=2r+1 and derive the honest order-s functional. Keep a table
for s=5,7,9 and then general s. For each order, record:

  (a) polynomial moments, pole values, pullback and distribution identities;
  (b) positive measure, integrability, affine dependence on zeta(s), and
      whether any other zeta values actually occur;
  (c) matrix size, degree bound, positivity/nonvanishing, and normalizer;
  (d) small-prime losses, exceptional primes, inner caps/allocations, outer
      polynomial degree thresholds and full rank correction;
  (e) resulting global arithmetic profile and its certified integral;
  (f) analytic external field, comparison certificate, normalization, and
      error terms for that SAME determinant.

Hold K=40n, N=3n, h=37n and D_N^6 fixed in the first comparison. Separate
changes caused solely by the order-s kernel from changes to the modifier
or parameters. Re-derive the revised capped-weight and full-rank estimates;
do not just transplant old bounds or replace a 5 by a 7 in final formulas.

Use the existing transfer work as a starting point:

  output/zeta7_argument_audit.pdf
  algebra_notes.txt; arithmetic_notes.txt; arithmetic_variant_notes.txt
  experiments/exact_checks.py and exact_checks.json
  experiments/main_measure_certificate.py
  experiments/single_point_certificate.py
  zeta9_draft/README.txt, zeta9_formal_analogue.tex, parts/, and checks/
  output/2026-11-14_research_chat_summary.md

Those records report that the tested order-7 functional retains positivity,
nonvanishing, and a single zeta value. Thus neither loss of positivity nor
unavoidable contamination by lower odd zeta values is an established
explanation of failure here. Test these properties for the chosen extension
rather than asserting a generic obstruction. If a different construction
does introduce several zeta values, state the independent elimination and
nonvanishing obligations it creates.

At K=40, N=3, p=29, the saved exact constant-coefficient valuations are -60,
-82, and -104 for orders 5, 7, and 9. The order-7 value refutes a copied
lower bound of -60. Retain this as a regression test of the underlying
functional and determinant; one finite example does not prove the general
asymptotic cost. Check the precise normalization before comparing numbers.

The older order-7 audit reports an added arithmetic coefficient 751/768
and a combined upper-bound coefficient about +0.960456781075 for its
specified original setup. Its D_N^8 repair has a certified obstruction for
the fixed original comparison measure. The order-9 draft reports the
increment 116581/57600 and explicitly labels unresolved/formal steps.
These are historical results for those setups, not conclusions already
established for the revised capped-weight argument.

For the revised setup, write the final comparison as

  log F_(s,K)(zeta(s)) <= A_s K^2 + o(K^2),
  log m_(s,K)          <= B_s K^2 + o(K^2),
  Q_(s,K)=m_(s,K)F_(s,K) in Z[X],  Q_(s,K)(zeta(s))>0.

The needed certificate is A_s+B_s<0 with degree O(K). Give exact or
rigorously enclosed constants and identify the first lemma that changes
or fails. A positive upper-bound coefficient means this certificate does
not establish decay; it does not prove that the actual values grow or that
every determinant approach to zeta(s) is impossible.

Only after this fixed-setup diagnosis should repairs be explored: different
N/K, numerator powers, local bases/allocations, sharper signed arithmetic
savings, or a new comparison measure. Every change must be charged on both
the arithmetic and analytic sides. Fixed rational rescaling cannot be
assumed to remove a quadratic asymptotic gap. Distinguish a failure of a
chosen comparison measure from an obstruction to the best possible bound.

Deliverable: a side-by-side transfer audit for orders 5, 7, 9, and general
odd s, with status labels (proved, imported, finite check, conditional,
open), exact counterexamples where available, and a short statement of
what genuinely prevents the current certificate from concluding.
