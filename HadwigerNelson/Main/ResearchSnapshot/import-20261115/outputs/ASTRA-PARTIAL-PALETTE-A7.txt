2. COMPLETE DESCRIPTION OF THE TWO-LIST LOCUS

Translate x to 0. Write s=sqrt(3)/2 and e(t)=exp(it). Define
  U=e((pi/3,pi/2)), V=e((2pi/3,5pi/6)),
  K=U+V, M0=U-V.
(The subscript distinguishes M0 from the different subcell M in Section 5.)

The entire locus with L=A0 is exactly
  R0=K union (-K) union M0 union (-M0).               (2)
To leave only A0, the excluded union must have four labels. Since F has
size at most two, this requires two FULL distinct pairs A1 and A2.
Their full-pair direction arcs are U,-U and V,-V. The four combinations
give exactly (2). No singleton endpoint, including a conservatively
completed nested boundary, can produce a two-list.
Rotation and the label permutation tau give
  R_j=e(j*pi/3)R0, j=1,2.                             (3)
There are no other two-lists.

3. EACH OF THE FOUR PIECES HAS ALL DISTANCES <1

Let d=2 sin(pi/12)=sqrt(2-sqrt(3)). A chord between two points of U has
length <d, and, up to reversal, its direction lies in (5pi/6,pi).
A chord between two points of V has length <d and direction, up to
reversal, in (7pi/6,4pi/3). The angle between the two chosen chord
directions lies between pi/6 and pi/2. Consequently, for any such
chords h,k (zero chords included),
  |h dot k| <= s |h| |k|.
For either sign,
  |h +/- k|^2 <= |h|^2+|k|^2+2s|h||k|
                 < (2+sqrt(3))*d^2 = 1.              (4)
Thus both K=U+V and M0=U-V have every pairwise distance strictly below
one. Their negatives do too. The strict bound follows from the open
30-degree arcs; no sampled grid or endpoint approximation is involved.

4. EVERY CROSS-PIECE DISTANCE IS >1

The source intervals immediately give the exact coordinate bounds
  (u,v) in K:   -s<u<0,        s+1/2<v<1+s;
  (x,y) in M0:   1/2<x<s+1/2, 0<y<1/2.              (5)
Hence:
  K versus M0: horizontal gap >1/2, vertical gap >s, so distance >1;
  K versus -M0: vertical gap >s+1/2>1;
  K versus -K: vertical gap >2s+1>1;
  M0 versus -M0: horizontal gap >1;
  M0 versus -K: vertical gap >s+1/2>1;
  -K versus -M0: follows by negating the first case.
These are all six unordered pairs of distinct pieces.

Combining (4) and (5), NO TWO POINTS OF R0 ARE AT UNIT DISTANCE.
Rotations prove the same for R1 and R2. In particular, arbitrary choices
of one label from each two-list give a proper colouring of the entire
two-list locus: within a fixed R_j there are no edges, and labels from
different A_j are disjoint. One especially simple choice is the constant
label a_j throughout R_j.

Precise consequence for A5: the directly forced open binary regions
cannot supply even one unit neighbour carrying the SAME forced pair.
Thus binary_open_local_rigidity with binary restrictions obtained solely
by these direct two-list inclusions has no geometric starting point.
This does not forbid a later argument from proving stronger restrictions
on some 3/4/5-list region and then applying rigidity there.

5. AN EXPLICIT RECOLOURING OF THE ENTIRE OLD A5 SUPPORT

Use the same full H lobe as in A5:
  H=e((pi/6,pi/2))+e((pi/2,5pi/6)).
Its NEW lists all contain A0, because its two source sectors never
contain a label in A0. Define the smaller open subcell
  M=e((pi/6,pi/3))+e((2pi/3,5pi/6)).                 (6)
On M the first source palette is the singleton {2} and the second is
A2={0,3}, so the NEW list is exactly
  L=A0 union {5} on M.                                (7)

M has diameter strictly below one. Its two 30-degree source arcs have
chord directions (up to reversal) in (2pi/3,5pi/6) and (7pi/6,4pi/3).
Their separation is between pi/3 and 2pi/3, so the absolute scalar
product is at most half the product of chord lengths. With the same
d as Section 3,
  diam(M)^2 <= 3*d^2=6-3sqrt(3)<1.                    (8)
The weak inequality on the diameter allows closure endpoints; every
actual pair has the corresponding strict bound.

Recall the accepted elementary H half-lobe fact (A4, Section 4c):
each of H intersect {Re>=0} and H intersect {Re<=0} has all distances
strictly below one. A short verification is as follows. The right half
lies in the three open unit disks centred at
  C=(0,1), Q=(s,3/2), E=(0,2),
whose centres form a unit equilateral triangle. For C this is the H
defining circle inequality; for Q use 0<=u<s and 1<v<2. For E use
|q-E|^2=|q-C|^2+3-2v when v>=3/2, and when v<=3/2 use
  u<sqrt(3)(v-1),
  |q-E|^2<3(v-1)^2+(v-2)^2
           =1+2(2v-3)(v-1)<=1.
The Reuleaux triangle has diameter one; membership in all three OPEN
disks makes the distances strict. Reflect for the left half.

Consequently the following is a proper NEW-list colouring of ALL H:
  colour 5 on M;
  colour 1 on H\M with Re>=0;
  colour 4 on H\M with Re<0.                          (9)
The two old half-lobes remain independent, M is independent by (8),
and its new label is distinct from both old labels. This is a pointwise
proper colouring, not merely an a.e. assignment.

6. ALL OLD CENTRAL CONTACTS LAND IN THE RECOLOURED SUBCELL

Let A=e(pi/6)=(s,1/2), zprime=A/2=(s/2,1/4).
The key exact contact-localization statement is
  q in H and |q-zprime|=1  ==> q in M.                (10)
It concerns the ENTIRE unit-circle intersection with H, not just the
two explicit contacts formerly chosen in A5.

Proof. Write q=(u,v). The unit equation gives
  u^2+v^2=s*u+v/2+3/4.                               (11)
From the exact H geometry (A5, Section 2),
  |u|<s, 1<v<2, |q-A|>1,
  |u|<sqrt(3)(v-1).                                  (12)
The last inequality follows from the lower boundary arc lying above
its chord from (0,1) to (+/-s,3/2).

First v>21/20. Otherwise (12) gives |u|<sqrt(3)/20 and hence
  |q-zprime|^2
     <= (3sqrt(3)/10)^2+(4/5)^2=91/100<1,
a contradiction.

Let B=e(pi/3)=(1/2,s), D=e(2pi/3)=(-1/2,s). Using (11),
  |q-B|^2-1=(s-1)u+(1/2-2s)v+3/4
              < (s-s^2)+(1/2-2s)+3/4
              =1/2-s<0,                             (13)
where u>-s, v>1, and s^2=3/4 were used with the correct negative
coefficient signs.
Also |q-A|>1 and (11) imply
  u<(3/2-v)/(2s).
Therefore
  |q-D|^2-1=(s+1)u+(1/2-2s)v+3/4
              <3/2+3/(4s)-(2/s)v
              <3/2-9sqrt(3)/10<0,                   (14)
using v>21/20 and sqrt(3)>5/3.

Finally write the unique H representation as q=e(a)+e(b), where
  pi/6<a<pi/2<b<5pi/6.
Put theta=(a+b)/2 and delta=(b-a)/2. For gamma=pi/3 or 2pi/3,
the inequality |q-e(gamma)|<1 is equivalent to
  delta>|theta-gamma|,
by q=2cos(delta)e(theta). It says gamma lies strictly between a and b.
Equations (13)-(14) thus give a<pi/3 and b>2pi/3, proving (6) and (10).

7. COLOURING BOTH H COPIES AND THE FOUR-LIST VERTEX

Put R=e(pi/3) and z0=i/2. The two original A5 lobes are
  H1=R H, H2=R^(-1)H.
Their NEW lists always contain A1 and A2 respectively. On their
rotated subcells the extra labels are
  L=A1 union {0} on R M,
  L=A2 union {4} on R^(-1) M.                         (15)
These follow directly from the source intervals in (6), shifted by
+pi/3 and -pi/3. In particular, the second line uses the singleton {1}
on (-pi/6,0), together with the full A1 on (pi/3,pi/2).

Define a colouring of H1 union H2 union {z0}:
  On H1, write w=R^(-1)z:
    if w in M use 0;
    otherwise use 2 if Re(w)>=0, and 5 if Re(w)<0.
  On H2, write w=R z:
    if w in M use 4;
    otherwise use 0 if Re(w)>=0, and 3 if Re(w)<0.
  At z0 use 2.                                       (16)
Each H-copy is properly list-coloured by precisely the argument for (9).

There are NO unit edges between H1 and H2. Their angular ranges are
(2pi/3,pi) and (0,pi/3), so their angular separation is strictly greater
than pi/3 and less than pi. Both radii are greater than one. Therefore
  |p-q|^2 > r^2+t^2-r*t=(r-t)^2+r*t>1.

For contacts with z0, R^(-1)z0=zprime, so (10) places every H1 unit
neighbour in R M, where (16) assigns label 0. Reflection in the imaginary
axis fixes H and M and sends zprime to R z0. Hence every H2 unit
neighbour lies in R^(-1)M, where (16) assigns label 4. Neither equals
the chosen label 2 at z0. Its list is still A1 union A2: its source
angles are arcsin(1/4) and pi-arcsin(1/4), whose forced sets are A0
and {1}, respectively.

Thus (16) is a concrete proper colouring from the NEW lists on the
ENTIRE support H1 union H2 union {z0}. Restriction gives a valid
colouring for every finite configuration inside that support, including
every possible choice of A5's connecting odd walks and all extra unit
edges among their vertices.
