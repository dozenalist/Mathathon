1. THE AC-AXIS ERROR, EXHIBITED GEOMETRICALLY

Take x=0, K+=[0,rho], K-=[-rho,0], 0<rho<1/10, on the real axis.
They approach x in directions +1 and -1 respectively.
On the positive real ray near radius one, the exact Straddle definition
gives
  Straddle(K+) intersect R_positive = (1,1+rho),
  Straddle(K-) intersect R_positive = (1-rho,1).
Their intersection contains neither radial side. Choosing OUTER for K+
and INNER for K- does not put any common strip in their intersection.

BoundaryTrace.binary_strip_palette_rigidity permits independent sides
bu,bv at two DIFFERENT angular locations, for its sets U and V. It does
not permit per-continuum side choices inside U=intersection Straddle(K_i).
Thus replacing equal directed vectors by a common unoriented axis is
not a valid extension of AC. This observation does not itself disprove
some other axis theorem; it disproves the stated strip-producing proof.

2. WHAT EXCEPTIONAL-SET COUNTING REALLY SAYS

Suppose a global binary palette cover has ALREADY been validly produced,
with Boolean state s flipping at tau=pi/3. Let B be a finite antipodal set,
and suppose s is essentially locally constant whenever t and t+tau both
avoid B. The essential boundary D then lies in B union (B-tau).

Since D is nonempty and tau-invariant, it contains a whole six-point orbit
O. More precisely, B intersect O must be an antipodal vertex cover of the
cycle C6, hence has at least FOUR points. This is exactly the finite step
six_antipodal_vertex_cover used inside AngularBoundary.angleBoundary_one_orbit
at lines 448-477. The conclusion is stronger than |B|>=3 or |D|<=2|B|:
four exceptional points must belong to ONE common sixty-degree orbit.

For B formed from perpendiculars to approach axes, this requires at least
two distinct axes in the same sixty-degree orbit (an angle difference of
60 or 120 degrees modulo 180). Generic unrelated axes would not suffice.
But this argument requires the global binary cover and local-rigidity
hypotheses first. Arbitrary nonaligned continua do not automatically supply
them; mixed radial sides cannot be used to invent them.

Four exceptional points CAN suffice at the angular level. For example,
B={30,90,210,270 degrees} is antipodal and
B union (B-60 degrees) is the entire orbit {30+60*j:j=0,...,5}.
The Boolean function alternating on consecutive 60-degree sectors with
those orbit boundaries flips at 60 degrees and is locally constant
outside that union. Therefore the count |B|<=2m cannot rule out all m>=2.

3. NEW GEOMETRIC LEMMA: AN OUTER 120-DEGREE ARC CANNOT BE TWO-COLOURED

Let c:C-plane->Fin k be measurable with WeakColoring k c. Fix x and an
open set W which is two-coloured almost everywhere:
  for plane-a.e. z, z in W implies c(z) in {a,b}.
Fix a real angle phi. Assume that for EVERY theta in
  I=(phi-pi/3, phi+pi/3)
there are positive angular/radial widths such that W contains an OUTER
strip near x+e(theta). One convenient exact formulation is
  for every theta in I, there exists delta>0 such that
  |theta'-theta|<delta and 1<r<1+delta imply x+r*e(theta') in W.
The source's Euclidean-direction-neighbourhood formulation implies this
by continuity of e. Widths may depend on theta and need not be bounded
away from zero near the two endpoints of I.

THEOREM OA120: these hypotheses imply False.

Proof step 1: produce a genuine connected sector with uniform width.
The closed angular interval
  I0=[phi-pi/4, phi+pi/4]
lies strictly inside I. Its compactness gives a finite cover by the angular
neighbourhoods in the outer-strip assumption. Taking a positive minimum
of their radial widths gives eta>0 such that
  1<r<1+eta, theta in I0 => x+r*e(theta) in W.
Decrease eta so eta<1/2. Put
  S={x+r*e(theta):1<r<1+eta, phi-pi/4<theta<phi+pi/4}.
This is a nonempty connected open polar sector contained in W. It has
angular width 90 degrees, not a limiting zero-width curve. No unproved
uniformity near the endpoints of the original 120-degree arc is used.

Proof step 2: every p in S has an actual unit-distance neighbour in W.
Write p=x+r*e(theta), 1<r<1+eta<2, and put
  alpha=arccos(r/2).
Then 0<alpha<pi/3, STRICTLY, because r>1. Choose
  beta=theta-alpha if theta>=phi,
  beta=theta+alpha if theta<phi.
In either case beta belongs to I. The point q0=x+e(beta) obeys
  |p-q0|^2=r^2+1-2*r*cos(alpha)=1.
Although q0 itself need not belong to W, its nearby unit neighbours on the
OUTER side do. Here is an explicit construction, avoiding a tangency guess.
For s>1 sufficiently close to 1 define
  g(s)=(r^2+s^2-1)/(2*r*s),
  beta(s)=theta-alpha(s) or theta+alpha(s),
  alpha(s)=arccos(g(s)),
using the same sign as above. At s=1, g(1)=r/2 is strictly between -1 and 1,
so beta(s) is continuous near 1 and tends to beta. Therefore the assumed
outer strip at beta contains q(s)=x+s*e(beta(s)) for all sufficiently
small s-1>0. Direct substitution gives |p-q(s)|=1 exactly. Choose one such
q in W. The construction is pointwise in p; no uniform neighbour radius
or derivative lower bound is being asserted.

Proof step 3: apply the ACTUAL generic binary rigidity theorem.
Let v=q-p, a genuine unit vector. Since W is open and p+v belongs to W,
Rigidity.open_two_unit_directions gives a second unit w with p+w in W and
transverseDet(v,w) nonzero. Now apply binary_open_local_rigidity with
  U=V=W, hp:p in W, hpv:p+v in W, hpw:p+w in W,
  the same a.e. two-colour restriction for U and V.
Every required hypothesis is present. It follows that p belongs to
essentialLocalSet(c,i_p) for some label i_p.

This holds at EVERY p in S. Rigidity.connected_local_ae_constant applies
to the connected set S, yielding one i with
  S subset essentialLocalSet(c,i).
In particular this is ambient local essential constancy, not just a colour
assignment on a zero-area arc.

Proof step 4: S contains a unit pair, contradicting weak colouring.
Take r0=1+eta/2 and lambda=arcsin(1/(2*r0)). Then 0<lambda<pi/6<pi/4.
Both
  p+=x+r0*e(phi+lambda), p-=x+r0*e(phi-lambda)
belong to S, and |p+-p-|=2*r0*sin(lambda)=1. They have the same essential
local colour i. Choose open neighbourhoods of p+ and p- on which c=i a.e.
Their common translation pullback contains an open neighbourhood of zero,
hence has positive area. But ae_translated_unit_pair says that for a.e.
translation u, c(u+p+) differs from c(u+p-). Intersecting these conull
statements on that positive-area neighbourhood is a contradiction.
This proves OA120.

WHY THE ENDPOINT CASE WORKS.
At radius r=1 the neighbour angle would be exactly 60 degrees and could
land on a missing endpoint of I. At every r>1 it is STRICTLY less than
60 degrees. The pointwise outer strip at the now-interior beta then supplies
a real unit neighbour. This is the additional geometric coupling absent
from a boundary-cardinality argument on the unit circle alone.

4. CLOSED SIXTY-DEGREE CONE EXCLUSION (CC60)

Assume a finite family K_i, one centre x, unit approach vectors v_i, and
actual ApproachingContinuum x K_i v_i data. Let H_i be finite excluded
label sets with
  Disjoint(spatialSupport c j, Straddle(K_i)) for every j in H_i.
Suppose ALL v_i have real angle representatives in
  [phi-pi/6, phi+pi/6],
and the complement of union_i H_i has at most two labels.
Then False under Measurable c and WeakColoring k c.

Proof. Set W=intersection_i Straddle(K_i), which is open. By the generic
finite_support_exclusion_ae, for a.e. z in W the colour is outside every
H_i, so it lies in some pair {a,b} covering the complement. If fewer than
two colours remain, the same conclusion holds; the argument does not
need a and b distinct.

For every theta in (phi-pi/3,phi+pi/3) and every i,
  |theta-arg(v_i)|<pi/2,
so <e(theta),v_i>>0. AngularBoundary.ApproachingContinuum.outer_strip
gives an actual OUTER strip at that direction inside Straddle(K_i).
All these sides agree. Finiteness allows taking their minimum widths,
giving an outer strip inside W. Thus OA120 applies.

No palette-size premise is used. No inner/outer signs are mixed inside W.
The excluded label sets may belong to different continua, but every
continuum approaches the SAME centre, and their SIGNED directions must
lie in the indicated closed 60-degree cone. Exact boundary directions are
permitted because the common positive arc is open.

EQUIVALENT NECESSARY CONDITION.
For any actual finite common-interface configuration at one centre and
any closed angular arc J of length 60 degrees,
  |union {H_i : arg(v_i) in J}| <= k-3.
This is a geometric label-union constraint with a direct source consumer.
It strictly strengthens AC, which uses only an arc of length zero.

5. SIX-COLOUR CYCLE CONSUMER AND THE GIVEN SURVIVOR

For the six-cycle E_i={i,i+1 mod 6}, if its approach directions in some
closed 60-degree cone cover at least four labels, CC60 forbids the actual
common interfaces. In particular:
  * two nonadjacent cycle edges cannot have approach directions separated
    by at most 60 degrees, since their four endpoints are distinct;
  * any three distinct cycle edges cannot all lie in one such cone.
Adjacent edges may share a direction or a small cone: they exclude only
three labels, so this theorem does not apply to them alone.

For q=(0,0,1,3,3,4), directions are q_i*pi/3. The selected edges 0,1,2
have directions 0,0,pi/3 and union {0,1,2,3}. Their straddling intersection
is a.e. {4,5}-coloured and has outer strips on the 120-degree common
positive arc (-pi/6,pi/2). OA120 yields a contradiction. This excludes
the DIRECTION configuration itself under actual source hypotheses; it
does not depend on which finite palette word the search selected.
