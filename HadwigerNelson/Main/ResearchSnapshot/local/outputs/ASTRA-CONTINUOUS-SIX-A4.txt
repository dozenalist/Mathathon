RESULT

There are two distinct results, with different hypotheses.

1. The CLOSED-60-degree necessary condition CC60 has a complete continuous
   classification. It is equivalent to requiring circular distance >60
   degrees between every two NONADJACENT cycle-edge indices. Among six
   distinct directions, 57 of the 60 unoriented cyclic orders are feasible.
   Every partition into adjacent coincident pairs and remaining singletons
   is feasible; with 3, 4, or 5 distinct directions, every order of its
   blocks is feasible. Exact gap inequalities below describe every angle,
   including all equalities and coincidences. Thus the six grid survivors
   do NOT classify the continuous CC60 problem.

2. Actual measurable weak colouring supplies an additional elementary
   obstruction: directions in a cone of aperture STRICTLY LESS THAN 120
   degrees cannot jointly exclude all but ONE label. Applying this to two
   coincident adjacent-edge pairs proves that any three-paired-direction
   configuration must be EXACTLY equilateral: its three rays are separated
   by 120 degrees. This collapses that entire continuous stratum to the
   already known paired grid geometry, without a palette cardinality bound.
   The equilateral paired geometry is not excluded by this proof.

Topology alone does not discretize the remaining directions. An explicit
measurable NONweak colouring below has exact common-interface support
exclusions on six compact radial segments, pairwise disjoint off their
centre, for a full open family of perturbations of the regular six rays.
It satisfies every CommonInterface requirement except WeakColoring. This
is a precise limitation of a topological shortcut, not a colouring model.

1. EXACT REFORMULATION OF CC60

Indices are i in Z/6; E_i={i,i+1} is the fixed cycle edge. Write alpha_i
for its assigned direction on R/(360 degrees Z). Let d be circular angular
distance, taking values in [0,180 degrees]. The following are equivalent:

  (C) In every CLOSED 60-degree arc, the union of the endpoints of the
      assigned cycle edges contained in that arc has at most three labels.

  (D) d(alpha_i,alpha_j)>60 degrees whenever
      j is not i, i-1, or i+1 modulo six.

Proof C=>D. Nonadjacent cycle edges have four distinct endpoints. If their
directions have distance <=60, some closed 60-degree arc contains both,
violating C. The strict inequality includes the endpoint case.

Proof D=>C. Every three distinct vertices in the edge-index cycle C6
include a nonadjacent pair, because C6 has no triangle. Thus an arc of
length 60 cannot contain three assigned edges. If it contains two, their
indices must be adjacent by D; their endpoint union then has size three.
One or zero edges is harmless. This proves the equivalence.

In particular, the separate condition 'no three directions in a closed
60-degree cone' is redundant once all nine nonadjacent-pair inequalities
are imposed. The directions are directed rays, not axes.

2. AN EXACT GAP THEOREM, INCLUDING ALL DEGENERACIES

Normalize 60 degrees to one, so the circle has circumference six. Choose
a cyclic listing sigma_0,...,sigma_5 of the six distinct edge INDICES in
nondecreasing angular order. Indices at the same direction can be listed
in either order. Let g_r>=0 be the forward angular gap from sigma_r to
sigma_(r+1), with cyclic indices; sum_r g_r=6.

THEOREM G. This angle assignment satisfies CC60 if and only if:

  G1. g_r>1 whenever sigma_r and sigma_(r+1) are nonadjacent in C6;
  G2. g_r+g_(r+1)>1 for every r;
  G3. g_r>=0 for every r and sum_r g_r=6.

Proof. G1 follows from (D). For G2, the three indices at the endpoints of
two consecutive gaps include a nonadjacent pair. A two-gap sum <=1 would
put that pair in a forbidden arc. Conversely, consider any nonadjacent
index pair. If it is consecutive in the spatial cyclic listing, its short
listing arc has length >1 by G1, and its other arc contains a consecutive
two-gap sum >1 by G2. If it is not consecutive, both arcs between it contain
at least two consecutive gaps and so have length >1. Thus its circular
distance is >1, proving (D). G3 only records the circle geometry.

This is a complete classification by explicit linear inequalities: for
each cyclic order, it is a convex relatively open region of the gap simplex,
with permitted zero gaps recorded by G3. No unmentioned generic-position
assumption or numerical angular mesh is used.

Consequences for ties:
  * At most two indices have one direction; if two do, they are adjacent
    in C6. Thus tied pairs form a matching in the edge-index cycle.
  * Three or more indices cannot coincide. Two zero gaps cannot be adjacent.
  * Each direction containing a tied pair is more than 60 degrees from
    EVERY other direction: one member of the tied pair is nonadjacent to
    any specified outside index.
  * There are at least three distinct directions.

3. WHICH CYCLIC ORDERS AND COINCIDENCE PARTITIONS EXIST?

THEOREM O, SIX DISTINCT DIRECTIONS. A cyclic order of the six indices is
CC60-feasible if and only if at least one of its consecutive pairs is an
edge of the index cycle C6.

Necessity. If no consecutive pair is adjacent in C6, G1 gives six gaps
strictly larger than one, contradicting their sum six.

Sufficiency, explicitly. Select one consecutive C6-neighbor pair. Give
its gap length 1-5 epsilon, and every other gap length 1+epsilon, where
0<epsilon<1/5. The sum is six and every gap is positive. For any
nonadjacent index pair, each of the two arcs joining it contains at least
one of the long gaps; the only arc without a long gap joins the selected
adjacent pair. Therefore all nonadjacent circular distances exceed one.
Equivalently G1-G3 hold. Small perturbations preserve the strict
inequalities, so every such order has a full-dimensional feasible region.

There are (6-1)!/2=60 cyclic orders modulo spatial rotation and reflection,
with labels fixed. Exactly THREE have no C6 adjacency, leaving FIFTY-SEVEN.
For a paper count, the complement of C6 is the triangular prism: triangles
{0,2,4} and {1,3,5}, with matching edges 03,25,41. A Hamiltonian cycle in
this prism crosses between the triangles an even number of times; with
only three matching edges it uses exactly two. Choosing those two edges
determines the Hamiltonian paths through both triangles, hence one cycle.
There are three choices. Canonical representatives of the forbidden orders:
  (0,2,4,1,5,3), (0,2,5,3,1,4), (0,3,1,5,2,4).
A tiny enumeration of the 60 orders independently checked this count.

Example far from the two grid geometries. In spatial order
  (0,1,3,5,2,4),
put the directions at
  (0,30,96,162,228,294) degrees respectively.
The selected gap is 30; the other five are 66. Every nonadjacent pair of
cycle indices is farther than 60 degrees. This is a CC60 model only: for
example edges 0,2,4 fit in a 132-degree arc and cover all six labels, so
the elementary empty-residual actual-interface obstruction in section 5
already forbids its realization by a weak colouring.

THEOREM O, COINCIDENT DIRECTIONS. For m=3,4,5 distinct directions, first
partition the six indices into m blocks of size one or two, requiring that
every two-element block be an adjacent pair in C6. EVERY cyclic order of
these blocks is feasible. Place the blocks at the vertices of a regular
m-gon: its minimum circular separation is 360/m>60 degrees. All forbidden
index pairs therefore have distance >60, and the only equal directions
are licensed adjacent pairs. Conversely every coincident CC60 assignment
has exactly such a matching partition by section 2. Actual angles are
then classified by Theorem G, or directly by (D).

In particular, the three-direction stratum has exactly the two perfect
matchings of C6 as its blocks. After choosing the matching (01),(23),(45),
it consists of
  alpha=(a,a,b,b,c,c),
where the three successive circular gaps are ALL >60 degrees and sum to
360 degrees. For example gaps 100,130,130 satisfy CC60. There is no
equilateral conclusion from CC60 alone.

4. A NEW ACTUAL-INTERFACE CONSUMER: ONE RESIDUAL COLOUR

THEOREM MC120 (strict aperture). Let c:C->Fin k be measurable and satisfy
WeakColoring k c. Suppose finitely many K_i approach one centre x in unit
directions v_i, with actual ApproachingContinuum data, and have finite
excluded label sets H_i satisfying
  Disjoint(spatialSupport(c,j), Straddle(K_i)) for j in H_i.
If the complement of union_i H_i has at most ONE label, their directions
cannot all lie in a cone of aperture STRICTLY LESS THAN 120 degrees.

Proof. If the complement is empty, the same argument can use any label
c(x) as a vacuous singleton cover. Otherwise call the residual label a.
Set W=intersection_i Straddle(K_i). This is open, and finite support
exclusion makes c=a almost everywhere on W. Suppose the angular span of
the directions is L<120 degrees. The common positive directions form an
open arc I of length 180-L>60 degrees. At EVERY angle of I, the source
outer_strip theorem supplies an outer strip in each Straddle(K_i); the
finite minimum supplies an outer strip in W.

Choose a compact angular interval strictly inside I with length B>60
degrees. A finite strip cover gives eta>0 such that W contains its open
polar sector with 1<r<1+eta. At r0=1+eta/2, take two angles symmetric
about the sector centre, with half-separation
  lambda=arcsin(1/(2*r0))<30 degrees<B/2.
Their two points p,q are in W and satisfy |p-q|=1. Since W is open and
c=a almost everywhere there, fixed small neighborhoods of p and q have
the same essential colour. The common translation pullback of those
neighborhoods is open and has positive area. Translation invariance gives
c(p+u)=a=c(q+u) for almost every u there, contradicting
Rigidity.ae_translated_unit_pair. This proves MC120.

The aperture inequality is STRICT. At aperture exactly 120, the common
positive arc has length exactly 60; pointwise outer-strip widths can
collapse at its endpoints. One cannot select a compact subarc wider than
60 or assume uniform radial width up to those endpoints. The accepted
OA120 proof repairs a different binary situation using local rigidity;
it does not license closing MC120's endpoint. No closed-120 claim is made.

No palette cardinality premise, same-direction axis reversal, limiting
pointwise colouring assertion, or uncountable intersection of conull
sets occurs in this proof.

5. CONSEQUENCES FOR ACTUAL SIX-EDGE CONFIGURATIONS

Together with the accepted CC60 and the trivial empty-residual argument,
actual same-centre interfaces obey the following table for any subfamily:

  Excluded endpoint union       Forbidden containing cone aperture
  at least four labels         <=60 degrees                 (CC60)
  at least five labels          <120 degrees                 (MC120)
  all six labels                <180 degrees                 (empty residual)

For the last row, a containing cone of width <180 gives a common positive
direction and hence a nonempty open straddling intersection. Exclusion of
all colours almost everywhere on a nonempty open set is impossible.

COROLLARY P, COMPLETE THREE-DIRECTION RIGIDITY. If actual six-cycle
interfaces have only three distinct directions, their pairs are one of
the two perfect matchings of the edge-index C6, and their three directions
are exactly 120 degrees apart.

Proof. The matching form follows from CC60. Use (01),(23),(45), the other
matching being a cyclic relabeling. The three blocks exclude respectively
  A={0,1,2}, B={2,3,4}, C={4,5,0}.
The union of any two blocks contains five labels. If any two block
directions had circular separation <120, those four actual continua would
contradict MC120. Thus all three pairwise circular distances are >=120.
List the three directions around the circle. Each successive gap is at
least120 (because its circular distance is at least120), and their sum is

6. WHAT THE SOURCE TOPOLOGY DOES AND DOES NOT SAY

The definition CommonInterface at ContinuumLimits:228 packages one K,
one unit direction, ApproachingContinuum, and two support exclusions.
For a family of interfaces it does not say that the K_i are faces of a
cell decomposition, that they bound colour regions, that there is only
one branch of each interface, or that their cyclic order agrees with the
label cycle. It does not even require distinct K_i to be disjoint.
The actual compact-sequence producer supplies compact connected continua
reaching the centre and a fixed outer sphere. It supplies their support
exclusions analytically. It does not add a simultaneous cyclic-order rule.

One can see that the missing order rule is substantive, rather than a
minor topological omission, in the following exact construction.

NONWEAK TOPOLOGICAL/SUPPORT MODEL. Fix phi, 0<eta<30 degrees, and independent
perturbations |delta_i|<eta. Put
  alpha_i=phi+60*i+delta_i,
  v_i=e(alpha_i),
  kappa=cos(60 degrees+eta)>0.
These directions satisfy CC60: nonadjacent regular rays are at least120
degrees apart, so after perturbation their separation is >120-2eta>60.
They retain the cyclic index order and form a full open five-dimensional
family modulo common rotation.

For every unit e, there is an index j such that BOTH
  <e,v_(j-1)> >= kappa and <e,v_j> >= kappa.              (positive pair)
Indeed take the two regular adjacent rays bracketing e; each is at most
60 degrees from e before perturbation. Apply the same assertion to -e to
obtain an adjacent pair with both inner products <=-kappa. (negative pair)

Choose 0<rho<min(1/4,kappa), let x=0, and set
  K_i={t*v_i:0<=t<=rho}.
These are compact connected arcs, pairwise disjoint except at0. They have
the required approach data with r_n=rho/(n+1), w_n=v_i. Define a Borel
colouring c:C->Fin6 by the least qualifying index in the finite choices:
  - if |z|<=1-rho or |z|>=2, set c(z)=0;
  - if 1-rho<|z|<=1, choose j from the positive-pair rule for e=z/|z|;
  - if 1<|z|<2, choose j from the negative-pair rule for e=z/|z|.
All selection conditions are Borel; choices exist as just proved.

Claim: c(z)=j implies z is in neither Straddle(K_(j-1)) nor Straddle(K_j).
For |z|<=1-rho, all distances to all K_i are <=|z|+rho<=1; for |z|>=2,
all are >=|z|-rho>1. Neither regime can straddle.
In the middle inner regime write z=r*e. For either selected ray and
0<=t<=rho,
  |r*e-t*v_i|^2=r^2+t^2-2*r*t*<e,v_i>
                <=r^2+t*(t-2*r*kappa)<=r^2<=1,
since r>1-rho>3/4 and rho<kappa<2*r*kappa.
Thus neither selected segment has a point farther than1.
In the middle outer regime, its negative-pair selection gives
  |r*e-t*v_i|^2>=r^2>=1,
so neither segment has a point closer than1. The claim follows.

Consequently each open Straddle(K_i) contains NO point of colour i or
i+1. An open set on which a colour is absent is disjoint from that colour's
essential spatial support (source support_disjoint_open_of_ae). Hence ALL
six exact common-interface support exclusions hold, not merely formal
direction signs or invented palettes.

This c is emphatically NOT a WeakColoring: it is constantly0 on the open
exterior |z|>2, which has a positive-measure family of monochromatic unit
edges. The construction proves only that compactness, connectedness,
disjoint branches, measurability, and the exact support-exclusion topology
do not discretize the directions or by themselves exclude this continuous
family. Any exclusion of these configurations must use the global weak
colouring hypothesis (or another additional analytic condition). It does
not refute F6 or construct an actual weak six-colouring.
