Focused audit: coset support and the remaining weak-mixing obstacle

Hypotheses. F is the field of real algebraic numbers; E=F(i), with its discrete additive topology; K={u in E: u conjugate(u)=1}; D=dual(E); T_u d(z)=d(uz). Let mu be a K-invariant Borel probability. Write psi_z(d)=d(z), a_z=integral psi_z dmu, and d_0 for the trivial character. Ergodicity and mu(j(R^2))=0 are needed below only where expressly stated. No Haar-classification theorem is used.

1. Exact lemma: for every nonzero z in E, |a_z|=1 implies mu=delta_{d_0}.

First, the additive subgroup generated by K is all of E. For t in F choose an integer n>=1 with |t|<=2n. Put a=t/(2n), b=sqrt(1-a^2), and u=a+ib, v=a-ib. Then u,v belong to K and t=n(u+v). Thus F lies in the integer span of K; because iK=K, so does iF. Consequently span_Z(K)=E, and, for any nonzero z, span_Z(Kz)=E.

If |a_z|=1, then psi_z=c almost everywhere, where c=a_z: indeed integral |psi_z-a_z|^2 dmu=1-|a_z|^2. K-invariance implies psi_{uz}=c almost everywhere for every u in K. Countability of K gives a common conull set on which all these equalities hold.

Take r=(1+i sqrt(3))/2 and s=conjugate(r). Both belong to K and r+s=1. On that conull set,
  c=psi_z=psi_{rz+sz}=psi_{rz} psi_{sz}=c^2.
Since |c|=1, c=1. Every d in this conull set annihilates Kz, hence its additive span E. Therefore d=d_0 and mu=delta_{d_0}. Conversely delta_{d_0} has a_z=1 for all z.

This proves more than the requested exclusion: among all invariant probabilities, without ergodicity, modulus one at a single nonzero frequency occurs exactly for delta_{d_0}. Under wildness, d_0=j(0) belongs to C, so this is impossible. Hence |a_z|<1 for every nonzero z under the full wild hypotheses. This is pointwise strictness, not a uniform spectral gap.

2. Corrections to the coset discussion.

For nonzero z, H_z={d:d(z)=1}. Any probability carried by a coset of H_z has |a_z|=1. Thus a fully K-invariant coset-supported probability must be delta_{d_0}; there is no wild example.

H_z is not K-stable. Its annihilator in the discrete dual group E is the cyclic subgroup Zz, and multiplication by i does not preserve Zz when z is nonzero. Therefore the stated K-action does not descend to D/H_z. The constant-value claim cannot be analyzed by treating that quotient as a K-factor.

For a coset with character value c, its intersection with C pulls back under j to
  {xi in R^2: <xi,z>=theta+2 pi k for some integer k},
where exp(i theta)=c. This is a union of parallel affine lines, not one affine line. This geometric fact does not rescue the coset construction from the invariance argument above.

3. A correct, centered weak-mixing criterion.

Now also assume mu is ergodic. Since K is countable abelian, choose a sequence of finite nonempty Folner sets Phi_n in K: |v Phi_n symmetric_difference Phi_n|/|Phi_n| tends to zero for every v in K. Define h_z=psi_z-a_z. Then diagonal ergodicity (M3) is equivalent to the following condition, for every z,w in E:

  (1/|Phi_n|) sum_{u in Phi_n}
    |a_{uz-w} - a_z conjugate(a_w)|^2  --> 0.             (*)

The same equivalence holds for any chosen Folner sequence. In particular, if M3 holds, (*) holds along every such sequence. There is no uniformity requirement over z,w. When z=0 or w=0, the integrand in (*) is identically zero.

Justification. With U_u f=f composed with T_u and inner product linear in the first entry,
  <U_u h_z,h_w>=a_{uz-w}-a_z conjugate(a_w).
The characters separate points of compact D, contain constants, and are closed under products and conjugation. Stone-Weierstrass and density of continuous functions in L^2(mu) imply that the h_z span a dense subspace of L^2_0(mu). This density does not assume Haar measure or character orthogonality.

If the diagonal action is ergodic, apply the Hilbert-space mean ergodic theorem to h_z(x) conjugate(h_z(y)) and pair with h_w(x) conjugate(h_w(y)); this yields (*). Conversely, (*) and Cauchy-Schwarz show that averages of all matrix coefficients between tensors h_z tensor conjugate(h_p) and h_w tensor conjugate(h_q) vanish. These tensors span a dense subspace of the centered-by-centered tensor product. The remaining two nonconstant summands of L^2(mu x mu) have no invariants by ergodicity of mu. Thus constants are the only diagonal invariants, proving M3.

The worker's uncentered diagonal expression is not the general weak-mixing criterion. Under M3 its limit for any z is
  average_{u in Phi_n} |a_{uz-z}|^2 --> |a_z|^4,
not necessarily zero; at z=0 it is identically one. Indeed imposing zero for every nonzero z would force a_z=0 at all nonzero frequencies and therefore force mu itself to be Haar, by density of characters and uniqueness of measures. This is a stronger assertion than the correctly centered criterion unless additional input already forces these Fourier means to vanish.

4. What remains unresolved.

The lemma rules out deterministic nontrivial ambient characters, not arbitrary nonconstant Koopman eigenfunctions. For example, any joint eigenfunction f with U_u f=lambda(u)f has constant modulus by ergodicity; if nonzero and nonconstant, f(x) conjugate(f(y)) is a nonconstant diagonal invariant. Nothing proved above excludes such an f, establishes (*), or proves M3 independently of the classification theorem.

M3 and terminal tower propriety H2 must not be called equivalent. In the proposed strategy M3 is only a sufficient route, subject to the actual tower hypotheses. This packet does not define that tower, so it supplies no proof of terminal propriety. Initial-stage propriety alone does not establish terminal propriety. The existing conditional argument therefore still needs its stated hypotheses; this audit removes one false branch, not the original proof sections.
