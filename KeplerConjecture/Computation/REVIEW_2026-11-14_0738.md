## 1. `exact_volume.py` — search and check are separate

`certify_volume` **searches**; `check_certificate` **checks** a certificate by exact rational
arithmetic alone, and the volume is returned only after the check passes. A caller supplying its own
vertices and facets is verified by the same function, so the checker is independent of the search.

**The certificate**: planes `(a,b,c,d)` meaning `a·x ≤ d`, an interior point `v`, a vertex list, a
facet list (plane index + vertex indices in **cyclic order**), and the volume.

| | obligation |
| --- | --- |
| **V1** | every vertex satisfies every plane, exactly |
| **V2** | `v` is strictly inside every plane (`a·v < d`) — a valid apex |
| **V3** | each facet's vertices lie on its plane, are `≥ 3`, not collinear, and its **area vector — recomputed from the vertex order** — is a strictly positive multiple of the outward normal |
| **V4** | every vertex lies on at least three facets (no spurious vertices) |
| **V5** | the facet area vectors sum to **zero** |
| **V6** | facet completeness follows from V3+V5 (see §4) |
| **V7** | `V = (1/3) Σ_f x_f·A_f` is positive and equals the certificate's volume |

**V5 is the boundedness gate.** Writing `A_f = λ_f a_f` with `λ_f > 0` (V3), the closure identity
`Σ_f A_f = 0` exhibits `Σ_f λ_f a_f = 0` with **all** `λ_f > 0` — the normals positively span `R³`,
so the cone `{x : a_f·x ≤ 0}` is `{0}` and the polyhedron is bounded. The positive combination is
supplied by the facet areas; nothing has to search for it, and no LP is needed.

**Certified triple filtering.** A float pre-pass may *propose* the candidates, and that is now sound
by a two-part argument:

1. the certificate proves the polytope is exactly `⋂A` for the candidate planes `A`, with a complete
   vertex and facet structure — and because V5 certifies boundedness, the vertex set determines it;
2. every plane **not** in `A` is checked exactly against every vertex of `⋂A`. A half-space that
   properly cuts a polytope leaves a vertex outside it, so the check passing proves
   `⋂(all planes) = ⋂A`.

So the pre-pass only has to be fast, not right. `candidates=None` gives the slow, assumption-free
path; `cell_volume` falls back to it when the candidate path fails.

## 2. Two real bugs found while writing it

Both are in the tests, because both are the failure mode under review.

* **`solve3` divided two ints.** `det / D` with integer arguments is Python's *float* division, so
  vertices came back `2⁻⁵³`-rounded and then failed their own plane check by `1e-9`. The tilted-box
  case exposed it: only 4 of 8 vertices were found, and the area vectors failed to close by exactly
  one face's area. Now `F(det, D)`.
* **A sign convention changed the half-space.** Normalising by flipping `(−a,−b,−c,−d)` to make the
  normal positive turns `−x ≤ 1` into `x ≤ −1`. Only *positive* scaling is admissible, and the code
  now says so.

And one in the checker: it originally trusted the stored area vector, so **reversing a facet's vertex
order was accepted**. It now recomputes the area from the order (V3).

## 4. Why the checks are sufficient (checker-independent)

Let `P = ⋂{a_i·x ≤ d_i}` and `Q = conv(V)`.

* **V1** gives `Q ⊆ P`.
* **V3** gives every facet plane of `Q` (the facets listed are 2-faces of `Q`, since each is
  `Q ∩ {a_f·x = d_f}` and contains ≥3 non-collinear vertices) is one of the constraints.
  Hence `P ⊆ ⋂{facets of Q} = Q`.
* So `P = Q`, and by **V5** `P` is bounded, so `V` is the complete vertex set of `P` — a bounded
  polyhedron is the convex hull of its vertices.
* **V6**: a face of a polyhedron is its intersection with a *supporting* plane, so any constraint
  plane meeting `P` in a two-dimensional set **is** a facet; the remaining planes are redundant, and
  V1 already shows they do not cut `P`.
* **V7** is the divergence theorem on a closed oriented surface.

None of this depends on how `V` and the facet list were produced.

## 5. What the certificate cannot see, and what closes it

A certificate is about **the planes it is given**. For a Voronoi cell that leaves one gap: whether
the competitor list was complete. `cell_volume` now checks the sufficient condition

$$2\max_{x \in V} |x - v| \le r_{\max},$$

i.e. every competitor outside `r_max` is farther than twice the cell's circumradius and cannot cut
it. (This gate is conservative: it refused `r_max = 6` for the dense-shell fixture, whose shell cells
reach `5.47`, and the fixture now uses `r_max = 12`.) The global check — the cells summing to the
period volume — catches the same failure from the other side.
