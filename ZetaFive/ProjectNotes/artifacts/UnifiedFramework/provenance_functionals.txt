FUNCTIONAL PROVENANCE AUDIT — 14 November 2026

Compared directly: sources/Fauzan_2026_zeta5.txt, with the original PDF's
printed page markers, against zeta5_unified_framework.tex. This is a comparison
to this particular manuscript, not a global literature or novelty search.

Main finding
============
The functional foundation of our framework is overwhelmingly Fauzan's own
construction. In particular, neither the tau/mu correspondence nor the
continuous Hurwitz-zeta function we named Phi was newly found by our note:
both are explicit in the original. The added value is a change of starting
point and a collection of classical interpretations, with a few additional
elementary identities. The note should distinguish those categories clearly.

1. EXACTLY PRESENT IN FAUZAN

(a) Definitions of mu_X on monomials and square poles.
Original: Section 2.1, p.3, equations (2.2) and (2.3).
Our formulas are the same formulas with the same normalization, including
the seemingly ad hoc -1/4 + 1/(2j). They must be attributed to Fauzan.

(b) L(x^k)=B_k, tau(P)=L(P''')/24, and the harmonic-sum values of tau_X.
Original: Section 3 opening, p.5 (definitions immediately following the
Gauss-valuation paragraph). Same definitions in our note.

(c) mu_X(R)=tau_X(x^5 R(-x^2)).
Original: p.6, equation (3.1), with the exact same partial-fraction calculation
for x^5/(j^2-x^2). Thus the original already says these are the same
functional under a weighted pullback. Calling this an unidentified
correspondence newly discovered by us would be incorrect.

(d) Reflection and difference laws.
Original: p.6, equations (3.2) and (3.3); the regular-at-zero qualification
for the difference law is already present. Our axioms restate these laws.

(e) One pole value determines the others by difference and reflection.
Original: Section 3.2, pp.7–8, Lemma 3.2. Its introduction explicitly says
the new parameter is chosen at 1/x and translation/reflection then determine
every integer-pole value. The proof p.8 applies that argument. Our uniqueness
proposition packages this already-used argument with the elementary
surjectivity of forward difference on polynomials. It is a useful new ordering,
not a newly discovered determination principle.

(f) Positive density and moment representation.
Original: Section 2.4, p.5, Proposition 2.2 and equation (2.10).
mu_zeta(R)=integral R(y^2) w(y) dy, and the explicit exponential-series w,
are identical. The proof already computes the polynomial moments from Euler's
even-zeta formula.

(g) w(y)=y^5 f^(4)(y)/12 with f(y)=1/(exp(2*pi*y)-1), the four integrations
by parts, and Hermite's exact integral formula.
Original: the proof of Proposition 2.2, p.5. The O(y) endpoint justification
and exponential decay at infinity are also stated there. Our explanation of
the fourth derivative and kernel is substantially a reformulation of this
proof, not a replacement by another method.

(h) The exact Phi function, for every real a>0, not just integer samples.
Original: Proposition 2.2 proof, p.5, unnumbered displayed identity:
  integral_0^infinity w(y)/(y^2+a^2) dy
     = a^4 zeta(5,a) - 1/(2a) - 1/4.
The subsequent sentence specializes a=j to obtain the simple-pole value.
Our Phi gives a name to this very identity. Its positivity also follows
immediately from Fauzan's displayed integral, so neither Phi nor positivity
should be represented as an additional construction of ours.

(i) p-adic analytic continuation, far-pole Laurent series, Cp, Y, distribution.
Original: Section 3.1 pp.6–7, (3.5); Section 3.2 pp.7–8, (3.6)–(3.7),
Lemmas 3.1–3.2. The forced relation Y=p^5 X+Cp, the calculation at 1/x,
and Cp in p^5 Z_p are all explicit. Our note repeats these results and
adds an interpretation of their polynomial part via Volkenborn integration.

(j) Finite-product binomial identity and integer-valued q_i identity.
Original: Section 3.3, p.9, unnumbered displays preceding (3.11).
Our formulas D_m(-x^2)/(m!)^2=binom(m-x,m)binom(m+x,m) and
q_i(-x^2)=binom(x+i,2i)+binom(x+i-1,2i) are already present (the former
with m=N). Describing q_i as interpolation polynomials changes terminology,
not content.

2. ADDED PACKAGING OR CLASSICAL INTERPRETATIONS NOT STATED THIS WAY THERE

(a) Euler–Maclaurin operator symbol
  tau(P)=[partial^4/(24(exp(partial)-1)) P](0),
  tau(exp(u*x))=u^4/(24(exp(u)-1)).
These displayed forms do not appear in the manuscript. They follow directly
from its L/Bernoulli definition and the classical Bernoulli generating
function. They make the common source legible; they are not a new ingredient
of the irrationality argument. The original itself also uses the related
generating identity L((1+z)^x)=log(1+z)/z on p.8.

(b) Promoting (3.2)–(3.3) and tau_X(1/x)=-X to the initial definition.
This reorganizes the logic. Our Proposition 2.1 adds a concise complete
uniqueness statement, but its key pole propagation already occurs in
Fauzan's Lemma 3.2.

(c) Phi as a Stieltjes transform, and one function carrying both the integer
samples and all polynomial moments as asymptotic coefficients.
The term Stieltjes and this combined interpretation are not in the original.
The exact finite geometric-series remainder in our note is an added
elementary identity. This is a worthwhile explanatory statement built on
Fauzan's exact continuous-a integral, not an independently found kernel.

(d) Volkenborn terminology/realization of polynomial tau, and matching the
p-adic far-pole series with the real Euler–Maclaurin asymptotic expansion
of -zeta(5,a).
These comparisons are not stated in the manuscript. The underlying p-adic
series and distribution law are already there. The Volkenborn facts are
classical and do not independently establish the rational near-pole estimates.

(e) Finite products as gamma quotients and truncations of sin(pi*x)/(pi*x).
These interpretations are not stated in the manuscript. Its finite product
and binomial expression are already explicit. Gamma and sine connections are
classical contextual explanations.

3. ADDITIONAL IDENTITIES OR DISCUSSION NOT FOUND IN THE ORIGINAL

(a) The general Abel–Plana/Hermite formula for tau_zeta(g), with boundary
term (g'''(0)-g^(4)(0)/2)/24 and integral of
[g^(4)(iy)-g^(4)(-iy)]/(exp(2*pi*y)-1).
The original instead proves the mu representation directly on monomial and
pole bases. Our formula extends the presentation to the larger tau domain
and then derives the same weighted-pullback integral. It is an additional
classical representation, retaining the same Euler/Hermite mechanism.

(b) The prescribed regularized lattice-sum formula
  tau_zeta(g)=-(1/24) FP_{z=0} Z_g'(z).
This is not in the manuscript. It is an optional classical reinterpretation,
with its explicit regularization convention essential. It does not replace
any quantitative estimate.

(c) The cotangent lattice-row identity
  E_5(z)=sum_{n in Z}(z+n)^(-5),
  w(y)=(i/pi)y^5 E_5(iy).
No cotangent/Eisenstein lattice-row interpretation appears in the manuscript.
It follows from classical cotangent partial fractions and the already-present
Fourier series for w. Do not call it a new density or a new positivity proof.

(d) The separate E_6/Eichler-integral context.
No such modular discussion appears in the manuscript. It is classical
background, not a contribution to the proof as currently presented.

4. ATTRIBUTION AND PRESENTATION RECOMMENDATIONS

The note already identifies Fauzan as its source and explicitly calls itself
an exposition note, so reuse of his formulas is not inherently problematic.
But section-level attribution is less precise than the comparison now permits.
At the key claims say:

* 'Fauzan's pullback identity (3.1) ...'
* 'The function Phi already occurs, without that name, in Fauzan's proof
  of Proposition 2.2. We emphasize its Stieltjes-transform interpretation
  and the connection between its samples and asymptotic coefficients.'
* 'We take Fauzan's reflection and difference identities (3.2)–(3.3) as
  defining axioms; the propagation argument used in Lemma 3.2 gives uniqueness.'
* 'The Euler–Maclaurin symbol, Volkenborn, and cotangent descriptions below
  are classical interpretations of these same formulas.'

In particular, the current sentence 'Thus positivity is a consequence of
the same Euler summation kernel, rather than an unrelated representation
added later' risks implying Fauzan failed to connect the kernel and positivity.
He explicitly proves their connection on p.5. A fairer claim is that our
ordering puts this connection at the start of the functional discussion.

Verdict: there is meaningful expository synthesis in the functional part,
but most of its core identifications were already explicit. We should not
describe Phi, the tau/mu pullback, the density, or the pole-boundary propagation
as newly discovered mathematics. The additional classical views can improve
understanding without changing the argument's core or establishing novelty.
